detail: "Bayer will build a $2.2 billion pharmaceutical manufacturing plant in New Albany, the company and state officials announced, according to the Ohio Capital Journal. Gov. Mike DeWine called it the largest international healthcare project in Ohio and the largest German direct investment in state history. The project is projected to generate 1,500 construction jobs and 600 permanent, highly skilled positions focused on manufacturing the compounds used in Bayer's medicines.\n\nBayer CEO Bill Anderson declined to name the other cities the company considered, but credited the region's workforce, proximity to research and healthcare institutions, and coordination with JobsOhio and state and federal officials for the decision. Anderson said the company expects to hire primarily from Ohio's existing labor pool rather than relocate workers from elsewhere.\n\nTo help supply that workforce, the Ohio Life Science Training Center, a $30 million facility in New Albany expected to open next year, will train workers for careers in pharmaceutical biomanufacturing. Bayer's production facility is planned about five miles from the training center, placing both the jobs and the training pipeline in the same New Albany corridor that already hosts large data center and logistics development."
