summary: "The Public Utilities Commission of Ohio is deciding how to regulate Nationwide Energy Partners, a company that buys electricity wholesale and resells it to apartment residents through submeters, after the Ohio Supreme Court ruled in May that the company operates as a utility under state law. AEP Ohio argues NEP is providing retail electric service inside AEP's exclusive territory without PUCO approval, which NEP disputes. Gov."
detail: "Submetering companies like Nationwide Energy Partners buy electricity in bulk at wholesale rates and resell it to individual apartments through a master meter, a common billing arrangement in Central Ohio multifamily buildings. The PUCO originally found NEP was not a utility because landlords, not NEP, were technically supplying the power; the Ohio Supreme Court rejected that reasoning in May and returned the case to the commission with instructions to treat NEP as a utility. AEP Ohio contends that under the Certified Territories Act, only AEP can deliver retail electric service inside its territory, and that NEP needed PUCO approval it did not obtain. NEP argues the law's definitions of \"electric load center\" and \"distribution lines\" do not cover a company operating behind an apartment complex's master meter. Ohio lawmakers passed a legislative fix in June that Gov. DeWine vetoed, calling the submetering model flawed. The PUCO's eventual ruling will set the regulatory rules for every submetering company operating in Ohio apartment complexes, including how much oversight and what approval process applies going forward."
why_it_matters: "Many Columbus-area apartment owners bill tenants for electricity through a submetering vendor like NEP rather than AEP directly, and the PUCO's ruling will decide whether that arrangement needs new state approval or faces new restrictions, which could change how a landlord bills utilities to tenants."
