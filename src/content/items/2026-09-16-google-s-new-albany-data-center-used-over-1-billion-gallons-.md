detail: "Consumption records obtained by News 5 Cleveland through a public records request from Columbus Water & Power show two Google data centers, one in New Albany and one in Columbus, ranked among the utility's top 10 customers in 2025. The New Albany facility used more than 1 billion gallons of water that year, more than the entire suburb of Bexley, population about 13,000, consumed. Google's Columbus facility used roughly 448 million gallons, about 1.2 million gallons a day.\n\nColumbus water administrator John Newsome said data centers account for roughly 3 percent of total consumption across the department's service area, which supplied 156 million gallons a day last year against a system capacity of 245 million gallons a day. More than 150 data centers are operating or planned across Central Ohio, with 40 already sited inside the 12,000-acre New Albany International Business Park, whose tenants include Amazon, Google, Meta and Microsoft. In late 2025, the entire park consumed about 9 million gallons of water a day, 56 percent of the 16 million gallons it is entitled to under a 2015 service agreement approved before the current data center boom began.\n\nMost data center operators do not disclose water use publicly, and Ohio is among the states now considering mandatory reporting requirements for the industry."
