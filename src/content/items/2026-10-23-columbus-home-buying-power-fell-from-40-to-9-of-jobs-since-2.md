detail: "The National Housing Conference's Paycheck to Paycheck database, which estimates the income needed to buy or rent across 390 metro areas, found Columbus required a $116,538 income to buy a typical home with 10% down in 2025, NBC4 reported. The share of job sectors in the Columbus area earning enough to clear that bar fell from 40% in 2020 to 9% in 2025, a decline researchers said reflects a 103% rise in the income needed to buy over that span. Statewide, Mansfield posted an even steeper drop, from 82% of job sectors able to afford a home in 2020 to 30% in 2025, as home ownership costs there rose 121%. For Columbus renters, the income needed to afford a typical two-bedroom apartment rose from an estimated $39,700 in 2020 to $57,800 in 2025. NHC researchers said the mismatch between wages and housing costs is pushing affordability pressure beyond households earning below the area's median income and into the middle class, and that increasing the supply of affordable housing is the lever most likely to narrow the gap over time."
