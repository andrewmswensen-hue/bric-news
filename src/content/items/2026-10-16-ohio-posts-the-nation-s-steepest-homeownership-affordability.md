detail: "Ohio had the steepest decline in homeownership affordability of any state between 2020 and 2025, according to the National Housing Conference's second annual Priced Out: When a Good Job Isn't Enough report. The share of Ohio occupations able to afford a typically priced home with a 10% down payment fell from 67% in 2020 to 26% in 2025, a drop NHC President David Dworkin said reflects jobs once considered solidly middle class losing access to homeownership.\n\nMansfield recorded the state's largest affordability loss: a typically priced home there rose from $115,041 in 2020, requiring a $28,483 income, to $188,863 in 2025, requiring $62,931, a 121% jump in the income needed to qualify. Occupations that could afford a typical Mansfield home in 2020, including firefighters, EMTs and postal carriers, could no longer do so by 2025. Mansfield, Cleveland, Springfield, Toledo and Akron all ranked among the 25 largest affordability declines nationally.\n\nRental affordability also tightened. The share of Ohio occupations able to afford a two-bedroom apartment fell from 77% in 2020 to 69% in 2025. Marcus Roth of the Coalition on Homelessness and Housing in Ohio said the shrinking path to ownership is pushing more households into the rental market longer, adding to rental demand. A separate COHHIO analysis puts Ohio's shortage of affordable, available rental units at 266,000 units."
