title: "Records Show Columbus-Area Data Centers Use Billions of Gallons of Water"
summary: "Public records obtained from Columbus Water & Power show Google's New Albany data center used more than 1 billion gallons of water in 2025, more than nearby Bexley's roughly 13,000 residents, while its Columbus facility used about 448 million gallons. The city's water administrator said data centers account for roughly 3 percent of overall system consumption, well within the system's daily capacity, though Ohio and other states currently allow companies to withhold detailed water-use figures as trade secrets."
detail: "Water use records obtained by News 5 Cleveland from Columbus Water & Power show that Google's New Albany data center consumed more than 1 billion gallons of water in 2025, more water than the entire suburb of Bexley, population roughly 13,000. A separate Google data center in Columbus used about 448 million gallons, or roughly 1.2 million gallons a day. Columbus water administrator John Newsome said data centers make up about 3 percent of total consumption across the department's service area, which supplied 156 million gallons a day last year against a system capacity of 245 million gallons daily. The New Albany International Business Park, home to more than 40 data centers including facilities for Amazon, Google, Meta and Microsoft, consumed about 9 million gallons a day in late 2025, roughly 56 percent of the 16 million gallons it is entitled to under a 2015 service agreement signed before the area's data center boom. Companies can currently keep detailed water-use figures confidential citing trade secrets, and Ohio is among states considering mandatory reporting requirements; a related state law further restricting disclosure of economic-development records took effect Sept. 23, 2026. A fourth Columbus water treatment plant, expected to cost billions of dollars, is out for bid partly to meet demand from data centers and residential growth."
why_it_matters: "The city's own data shows data centers are a small share of total water use today, but the confidentiality around individual facility consumption is exactly the information gap Columbus City Council cited last week when discussing new water rates and fees for large users, so this is the baseline number to watch as that policy moves toward a vote."
