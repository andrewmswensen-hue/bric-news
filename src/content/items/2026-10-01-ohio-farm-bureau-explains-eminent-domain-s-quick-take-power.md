detail: "Eminent domain is the power under the Ohio Constitution to take private property for public use, with compensation set by a jury after the taking. In a Legal with Leah episode, Ohio Farm Bureau associate general counsel Leah Curtis walked through how that process normally works: most government entities can exercise eminent domain, and the state has delegated that authority to certain public utility companies building projects such as power lines, pipelines, or phone lines. She then explained quick take, a less common variation that allows some condemning authorities to take possession of property and begin work before the compensation amount is finally determined by a jury, rather than after. Curtis said Ohio Farm Bureau has a standing policy opposing expansion of quick take authority, arguing landowners need a fair process as development and infrastructure projects, including power lines and pipelines tied to the state's data center buildout, continue to spread across Ohio. The episode is aimed at farmers and other landowners who may be approached by a utility or government agency seeking an easement or right of way."
why_it_matters: "Central Ohio landowners along the path of a new transmission line or pipeline, increasingly common as Licking and Delaware county utilities scale up for data centers, should know that quick take can let a utility begin construction before a jury sets the final compensation."
