summary: "Governor-elect Amy Acton outlined four priorities for Ohio property tax relief as she prepares to take office January 11. She plans to raise the Homestead Exemption income threshold for qualifying seniors, disabled residents and veterans from $41,000 to $50,000, extending relief to roughly 55,000 more Ohioans. Acton also proposed a property tax spike rebate of up to $1,000 for households earning under $132,000 whose tax bills rise more than 4% year over year on homes valued at $350,000 or less."
detail: "Acton, who takes office January 11, said she plans to pursue four property tax changes once she is governor, continuing a bipartisan effort among state lawmakers to slow rising property tax bills. First, she wants to expand the Homestead Exemption, the tax relief program for qualifying seniors, disabled residents and veterans, by raising the income eligibility ceiling for seniors from $41,000 to $50,000 a year, which her office says would extend the exemption to about 55,000 more households. Second, she proposed a property tax spike rebate of up to $1,000 for households with incomes under $132,000 whose tax bills on a home valued at $350,000 or less rise more than 4% from the prior year. Third, she wants to fully fund Ohio's Fair School Funding Plan, which still uses 2022 salary and spending figures, to reduce school districts' reliance on local property tax revenue. Fourth, she wants to restrict tax lien sales to private collectors, a market that raised Franklin County more than $10 million in 2025, arguing the sales can lead to high interest charges and foreclosure for homeowners who fall behind. Acton would need cooperation from the Republican-controlled legislature; any funded changes would move through the next two-year state budget, due by July 1, 2027."
