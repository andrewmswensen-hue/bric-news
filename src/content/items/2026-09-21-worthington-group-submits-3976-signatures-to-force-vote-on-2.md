title: "Worthington group submits 3,976 signatures to force vote on 246-unit apartment rezoning"
summary: "A Worthington group, Democracy Before Developers, submitted nearly 4,000 signatures to put a referendum on the July rezoning of an East Dublin-Granville Road site for a 246-unit apartment complex on the 2027 ballot. About 3,000 valid signatures are needed. The group objects to the density and to a requested 10-year, 75 percent property tax abatement. The city says 30 percent of units would be reserved for workforce housing."
detail: "According to WOSU, Democracy Before Developers turned in nearly 4,000 signatures Friday for a zoning referendum on a property on East Dublin-Granville Road in Worthington. The group formed a few days after Worthington City Council rezoned the site at the end of July to allow a 246-unit apartment complex, and needed 3,000 signatures to qualify. It expects to learn by mid October whether enough are valid.\n\nOrganizer Marty Shumway said residents raised concerns about density and about the 10-year, 75 percent property tax abatement the developer requested, and that the group is not opposed to development on the site. The city responded that it does not consider the project high density. It said the developer applied for the abatement through Worthington's new Workforce Housing Program, committing to reserve 74 of the 246 apartments (30 percent) for households at 80 percent of area median income for 10 years. HUD puts that level at $62,450 for one person and $89,200 for four in the Columbus metro. The city said the project is not financially viable without the abatement, citing construction, labor, land and interest costs.\n\nThe referendum would appear on the 2027 ballot."
why_it_matters: "A referendum can hold up a rezoning that council already approved, so a 246-unit project in a tight inner-ring suburb now has a vote and a wait attached. Owners of nearby rentals and buyers eyeing similar sites in Worthington can watch how the abatement and the 80 percent AMI set-aside hold up under voter scrutiny."
