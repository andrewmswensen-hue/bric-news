summary: "Delaware County, now the 22nd fastest-growing county in the U.S. according to county officials, is asking voters this November to approve a 0.2% sales tax to expand Delaware County Transit service. The city of Delaware alone has more than 4,000 homes under construction. Commissioners have also committed $17 million to buy 141 acres along Sawmill Parkway for future development, and the Ohio Department of Transportation is studying a package of safety and capacity projects along the congested U.S. 23 corridor between I-270 and Waldo."
detail: "Delaware County officials say the county is now the 22nd fastest-growing in the United States, with more than 4,000 homes under construction in the city of Delaware alone. County Commissioner Jeff Benton said keeping infrastructure ahead of that growth is expensive, pointing to a June vote by commissioners to spend $17 million on 141 acres along Sawmill Parkway, a site ranked among the state's top development corridors by JobsOhio for its highway access and utility availability. The Ohio Department of Transportation is running a corridor action plan for the 23.5-mile stretch of U.S. 23 between I-270 and Waldo, ranking dozens of potential projects, including interchanges and added lanes, by priority tier, and is separately studying a U.S. 23/71 connector opposed by some rural landowners in Delaware, Marion and Morrow counties. Delaware County Transit is asking voters to approve a 0.2% sales tax levy this November, which officials say would fund seven service expansions over five years, including new routes in Powell and Orange Township. The agency said ridership has grown 174% in recent years, with roughly a quarter to a third of trips tied to commuting. If the levy fails, officials said the transit system would likely cut service."
