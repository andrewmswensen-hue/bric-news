title: "The Case for Leasing a Columbus Rental Through the Winter"
summary: "RL Property Management laid out the math on renting a Columbus property between November and February, the slowest leasing window of the year. National median rent falls about 1.6% from its summer peak to its November low, per Apartment List, before demand rebuilds in January. On a $1,500 a month unit, roughly Columbus's average per Zillow's August 2026 figure, holding a vacancy from mid-November to March 1 gives up about $5,100 in rent plus winter utilities. RLPM recommends pricing at or below comparable listings and using a one-time move-in incentive rather than a permanent rent cut."
detail: "RL Property Management, a Columbus brokerage and the site's disclosed editorial partner, published guidance on leasing rental units during the slow winter window rather than holding them vacant until spring.\n\nApartment List's seasonality research shows national median rent falling about 1.6% from its summer peak to its November low, with November and December the cheapest months before prices recover in January. A January 2026 Apartment List analysis found more than 30% of renter interest now happens in the first quarter, which RLPM says is compressing the slow period to roughly mid-November through New Year's.\n\nRLPM's math: a $1,500 a month unit, close to Columbus's citywide average of about $1,495 per Zillow's August 2026 data, earns about $49 a day. Holding it vacant from mid-November to March 1 gives up roughly $5,100 in rent plus winter utility costs.\n\nThe firm's recommendations for winter leasing: price at or slightly below comparable active listings rather than waiting for a spring premium that will not apply, offer a one-time move-in concession instead of a permanent rent reduction so renewal pricing starts from the higher base, and use daylight listing photos since fewer showings happen after dark.\n\nTwo local rules apply to lease terms written over the winter. Columbus Ordinance 3525-2024 requires leases entered into or renewed on or after January 31, 2025, to include at least 60 days' written notice before any rent increase above 10%. Ohio Revised Code 5321.17(B) lets either party end a month-to-month tenancy with 30 days' notice before the rent due date."
why_it_matters: "A 15 or 18 month lease signed this winter ends in spring or early summer instead of back in the slow season next December, which is the lever RLPM says owners most often skip when a unit goes vacant in November."
