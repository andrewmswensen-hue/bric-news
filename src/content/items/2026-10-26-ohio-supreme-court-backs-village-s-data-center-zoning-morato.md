title: "Ohio Supreme Court backs village's data center zoning moratorium"
summary: "The Ohio Supreme Court ruled unanimously this week that the village of Lordstown was not required to review a data center developer's site plan after the village enacted a moratorium banning new data centers. Bristolville 25 Developer had filed for site-plan review in October 2025, two weeks before Lordstown's council passed its ban, then asked the state's highest court to force the village to review the application under the zoning rules that existed when the review began."
detail: "Bristolville 25 Developer had purchased industrial-zoned land in Lordstown in February 2025 intending to build a data center and requested a site-plan review that October. The day after the request, the village's zoning administrator told the company a council vote on a data center ban was already underway and that the review could not proceed until council decided. Lordstown's council passed the ban in early November 2025, applying it to any new zoning, building or occupancy permit for a data center, though it exempted facilities already existing or under construction. The village later repealed that ban and replaced it with a temporary moratorium on new data center permits and applications, which it extended another 180 days in June. Bristolville asked the Ohio Supreme Court to order Lordstown to review its site plan under the zoning rules in place before the ban, arguing its request predated the change. The court disagreed, ruling the company had not met the village's legislative requirements for a permit and so had no vested right to enforcement of the earlier ordinance. Lordstown's moratorium is one of more than 100 data center moratoriums now in place across Ohio, according to the Ohio Capital Journal. The ruling confirms a municipality can cut off a pending zoning application by passing a moratorium before a permit actually issues, even if an applicant filed first."
why_it_matters: "The ruling means an Ohio municipality can cut off a pending zoning or permit application just by passing a moratorium before the permit issues, so a Columbus-area investor with a project awaiting approval has no vested right in the old rules simply because the application was filed first."
