title: "Court tosses Cuyahoga County's appeal of a property tax cut"
summary: "Ohio's Eighth District Court of Appeals dismissed Cuyahoga County's attempt to reverse a Board of Tax Appeals (BTA) decision cutting a University Heights property's assessed value from $191,200 to $155,000, ruling on November 19, 2026. The county's notice of appeal challenged only the BTA's March 17 denial of its reconsideration motion, not the underlying valuation decision itself, and the court found it lacked jurisdiction to review a denial that never changed the relief already granted."
detail: "The dispute traces to a complaint seeking to cut the University Heights property's assessed value from $191,200 to $150,000; a licensed appraiser filed it on behalf of the property's three co-trustee owners. At an August 2025 Board of Revision hearing, the appraiser testified he prepared the complaint for the taxpayer and confirmed his appraised value was actually $155,000. The Board of Revision amended the requested value to $155,000 but declined to reduce the assessment, prompting an appeal to the BTA, which held a hearing in February 2026. The county asked the BTA to dismiss the complaint on the theory that the appraiser lacked authority to file for the trustees. On March 9, 2026, the BTA granted the reduction without directly addressing that motion; the county's renewed motion was treated as a request for reconsideration and denied March 17 on the grounds the complaint had been filed by someone authorized under state law. The county's notice of appeal named only the March 17 denial, and the Eighth District ruled that denial was not independently appealable because it did not alter the relief already granted, distinguishing a prior case where a school board had been denied any notice of a BTA proceeding before the appeal window closed. Because the county had fully participated in the BTA proceedings and could have appealed the valuation decision directly, the dismissal left the $155,000 value in place."
why_it_matters: "Franklin County investors challenging a valuation at the Board of Tax Appeals should take the procedural lesson here: a county (or a property owner) that wants to overturn a BTA decision has to appeal the valuation order itself, not just a later denial of reconsideration, or an appellate court can dismiss the case on jurisdictional grounds without ever reaching the merits."
