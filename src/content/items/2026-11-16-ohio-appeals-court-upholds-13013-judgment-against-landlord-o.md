title: "Ohio Appeals Court Upholds $13,013 Judgment Against Landlord Over Curbed Belongings"
summary: "The Ohio Court of Appeals, Fifth District, affirmed a Stark County judgment against landlord Marvin Johnson for violating Ohio's residential landlord-tenant law after he moved a departing tenant's belongings to the curb, where members of the public went through them. The Nov. 13 ruling in Petties v. Johnson upheld $5,790 in compensatory damages plus $7,223 in attorney fees and costs, for a combined $13,013. The court rejected the landlord's challenges to adding a second plaintiff, to the damages calculation, and to the attorney-fee award."
detail: "The case began after tenants Jasmine and Charles Petties, who had rented a Stark County property since 2015, could not agree with landlord Marvin Johnson on a move-out date after he put the property up for sale in 2023. Johnson gave the couple a three-day notice to vacate on Sept. 15, 2023. The next day, while the couple was still loading a rental truck, Johnson told them he would move their remaining belongings to the curb, where passersby began going through them.\n\nJasmine Petties filed a lawsuit alleging unlawful landlord activity, trespass, negligence and civil conspiracy. At a bench trial, she testified to damaged or missing items, including a treadmill, an entertainment center, and a backpack containing an Apple Watch and jewelry, valued at $5,790. A magistrate found the landlords violated Ohio's residential landlord-tenant statute and awarded that amount, plus $7,223 in attorney fees and costs, while dismissing the trespass and punitive-damages claims.\n\nOn appeal, the landlords argued the damages were miscalculated, that adding Charles as a plaintiff was improper, and that the attorney-fee award exceeded the couple's fee agreement. The Fifth District Court of Appeals rejected all three arguments on Nov. 13, affirming the judgment in full, according to the Times of India."
why_it_matters: "Ohio law lets tenants recover attorney fees on top of damages for landlord-tenant violations, and a private fee agreement with a lawyer doesn't cap what a landlord can owe, worth remembering before handling any tenant's belongings after a contested move-out."
