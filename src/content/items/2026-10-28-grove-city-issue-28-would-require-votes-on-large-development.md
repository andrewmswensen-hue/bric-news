title: "Grove City Issue 28 would require votes on large developments"
summary: "Grove City voters decide Issue 28, the Community Consent Amendment, on Nov. 4. The measure would require a public vote before any development exceeding 50 acres, 20 megawatts of power draw, or 500,000 gallons of daily water use could proceed, with two public hearings, impact studies and quarterly reports paid for by the business, according to WSYX. Supporters, led by Protect Grove City, say it is a response to a 310-acre data center proposal the city halted with a moratorium."
detail: "Grove City's Nov. 4 ballot includes Issue 28, known as the Community Consent Amendment, which would give residents direct veto power over the city's largest development proposals. Under the measure, any project exceeding 50 acres, drawing 20 megawatts of power, or using 500,000 gallons of water a day would need voter approval, plus a minimum of two public hearings, impact studies, and quarterly reports, with the business bearing the cost. Existing operations that already meet those thresholds would be grandfathered in but would still have to meet community impact standards on noise, light, and vibration at the property line, facing fines from $25,000 to $500,000 a day for violations. Any expansion that grows acreage, water use, or energy use by 10% at an already-covered site would be treated as a new application and trigger the same vote requirement. Protect Grove City organizer Jen Belt said the amendment responds to a 310-acre data center project the city blocked with a moratorium and is meant to give residents a say over large industrial proposals generally. Save Grove City's Patrick Castro said the thresholds could also reach existing institutions, including Mount Carmel and South-Western City Schools, and argued the rule could restrict routine operations like school band practice or helicopter landings."
why_it_matters: "An investor planning any Grove City project near the 50-acre, 20-megawatt, or 500,000-gallon-a-day thresholds should price in the Issue 28 hearings, impact studies, and quarterly reporting costs, plus daily fines up to $500,000, before the Nov. 4 vote decides whether those rules take effect."
