detail: "Trenton City Council gave first reading to resolutions spelling out the financial consequences if voters defeat Issue 17 on the November 3 ballot, a citizen-initiated charter amendment banning data centers requiring more than 25 megawatts of electricity. The Ohio Supreme Court ordered the issue onto the ballot after ruling the city had improperly refused to certify it. The resolutions, tied to defeat of Issue 17 and a proposed Amazon data center project moving forward, would give residents a one-year holiday on the city's general fund property tax millage for tax year 2026, worth about seven mills, and bar the city from seeking new property tax levies for police, streets, and part-time fire and EMS services until 2035. A separate resolution would give residents a 30% discount on YMCA memberships once construction begins. Finance Director Matthew Mesisklis said that without the data center's roughly seven mills of tax value, a $300,000 homeowner could eventually face about $735 a year in added property tax. Mayor Ryan Perry said the legislation only lays out financial consequences, but some residents called the proposals \"dangling political carrots.\" Greg Lawson of the Buckeye Institute said explaining a ballot measure's tax impact is normal, but writing those consequences directly into legislation tied to the vote is unusual and could draw a post-election legal challenge. The project covers roughly 600 acres in Madison and St. Clair townships; an earlier annexation petition was withdrawn."
why_it_matters: "Central Ohio cities negotiating their own data center deals, including Hilliard and New Albany, are watching how Trenton ties tax relief to a single project, since a similar levy freeze or millage holiday shapes how much of local service funding shifts onto residential and rental property owners instead."
