detail: "The Ohio Environmental Council, a statewide advocacy group, held a public meeting at the Bexley Public Library to brief residents on plans to build hyperscale data centers across Central Ohio. OEC central Ohio regional director Annalisa Roca said data centers accounted for 63% of a recent increase in per-megawatt electricity costs on PJM Interconnection, the regional grid serving Ohio and several neighboring states, citing PJM's independent market monitor. A single hyperscale facility can use as much electricity as 75,000 to 100,000 homes and up to 5 million gallons of water a day, mostly through evaporative cooling, Roca said, adding that only about one in three data centers publicly track their water use. Roca cited American Electric Power Ohio bills that she said rose from $28.92 to $269.92 in summer 2025 and increased by another $329.17 in 2026, and said data centers are projected to consume 11% of Ohio's electricity by 2030. She pointed to a Hilliard case in which Amazon built data centers next to a neighborhood and later sought state approval for an adjacent natural gas plant without city council involvement. Ohio currently has no statewide requirement that data centers report water use or disclose planned projects, and the state's sales tax exemption for data centers, which Roca said cost $1.56 billion by late 2025, has been paused for new applicants by Gov. Mike DeWine. House Bill 646, which stalled before the legislature's summer recess, would have required usage reporting and cut the tax exemption in half."
