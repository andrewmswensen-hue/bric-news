summary: "Columbus Mayor Andrew Ginther proposed a capital budget topping $2.8 billion, including more than $2 billion in new spending, according to WOSU. The budget includes $24 million for traffic and pedestrian safety improvements on Livingston Avenue, $21.5 million for fire apparatus, and $20.9 million for bikeways and sidewalks. detail: "Columbus Mayor Andrew Ginther announced the proposed capital budget at the Driving Park Community Center, highlighting $24 million to end fatal and serious crashes on Livingston Avenue through pedestrian and traffic safety improvements, according to WOSU. Other proposed spending includes $21.5 million for fire apparatus, $20.9 million for bikeways and sidewalk construction and repair, more than $13 million for new parks on the southwest side to replace McCoy Park, and $13.5 million to reconstruct the Tuttle Park pool. The single largest project is a new fourth water treatment plant in southwest Delaware County, budgeted at more than $1 billion over several years, intended to meet water demand from new residential growth and data centers straining the existing system. The project's timeline, originally set for completion by 2032, is now uncertain because no contractors submitted bids when the city solicited them, which Columbus Water and Power Director Kristen Atha attributed partly to a spike in construction costs. The city is now meeting with multiple contractors and may adjust its bidding strategy. Columbus City Council still has to vote on the full capital budget."
why_it_matters: "The stalled fourth water plant is the infrastructure meant to support new residential growth in this corridor, so landlords planning new units in southwest Delaware County or nearby Columbus should expect water and sewer capacity to lag the area's growth for longer than the city's original 2032 target."
