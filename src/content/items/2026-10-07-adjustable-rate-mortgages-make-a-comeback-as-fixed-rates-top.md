detail: "Borrowers are turning back to adjustable-rate mortgages as fixed-rate financing gets more expensive, according to ICE Mortgage Monitor data covered by the New York Post. Nearly 11% of first-lien rate locks in the week ending Sept. 18 were ARMs, the highest share in nearly four years and more than 3 percentage points above the level three months earlier. Among homebuyers specifically, close to 9% chose an ARM, the second-highest weekly share since 2022.\n\nThe move follows seven straight months of rising rates: ICE's 30-year fixed index climbed 136 basis points from a February low of 5.95% to 7.31% by the end of September, its highest level since November 2023. An ARM carries a fixed rate for an introductory period before adjusting with the market, so borrowers who choose one are betting rates will fall before the reset.\n\nThat bet carries risk. ICE counts 3.1 million active first-lien ARMs nationally, the most in roughly five and a half years, and projects 186,000 of them will reset for the first time in 2027, up from 148,000 this year. The median borrower resetting in 2027 is projected to see a rate increase of about 2.2 percentage points, adding roughly $645, or 24%, to the monthly payment. Borrowers who took out seven-year ARMs in 2020, when rates were near historic lows, face the steepest resets: ICE estimates some of those rates will jump from 2.75% to 5.79% at first adjustment."
