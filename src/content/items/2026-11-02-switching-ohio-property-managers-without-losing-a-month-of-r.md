detail: "RL Property Management, BRIC's disclosed editorial partner, laid out a transition process for owners switching property managers across a multi-property portfolio. Ohio law sets no termination notice period for management agreements; Ohio Revised Code 4735.18 only requires the written agreement to carry a definite expiration date, leaving notice periods, exit fees, and leasing clawback terms to the contract itself. The guide recommends timing the switch to land right after a rent cycle closes, so the outgoing manager finishes one full collection and disbursement cycle before the new manager takes over, and warns that a mid-cycle switch can cause an owner to lose track of a month's rent. Because Ohio brokers must keep rents, deposits, and reserves in a separate trust account and account to owners at least quarterly, those records should already exist, and owners can request signed leases, 12 months of tenant ledgers, a per-tenant security deposit schedule, inspection reports, and vendor lists in writing; brokers must keep complete transaction records for three years. Security deposits should move with a schedule both managers sign, crediting each tenant, since Ohio law requires deposits returned within 30 days of lease termination with 5% annual interest owed on amounts above a floor set by ORC 5321.16. In Columbus, the rental registry must be updated within 30 days when the local operator changes, and the statewide county filing within 60 days under ORC 5323.02. The guide also flags portfolio-specific issues: leases renewing within 60 days of the switch, which can trigger a 60-day rent-increase notice requirement under Columbus City Code 4551.071, vacant units mid-turn, and pending evictions that need a clear owner of record in Franklin County Municipal Court."
