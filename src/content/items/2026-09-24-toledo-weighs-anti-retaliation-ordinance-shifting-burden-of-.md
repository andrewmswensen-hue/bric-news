title: "Toledo weighs anti-retaliation ordinance shifting burden of proof to landlords"
summary: "Toledo City Council gave a first reading to an ordinance adding a 'Retaliation Prohibited' chapter to city code, protecting tenants who raise health and safety concerns from eviction or rent increases in response. Landlords would bear the burden of proving good cause for an eviction or rent increase, reversing the standard under Ohio's existing anti-retaliation law, which requires tenants to prove retaliation occurred. Violations would become a second-degree misdemeanor."
detail: "The ordinance responds to conditions at Saxon House apartments, where tenant union steward Matty Watkins has organized around mold and water damage complaints. Watkins and other tenants told 13abc that raising maintenance issues can feel risky because Ohio's current anti-retaliation law puts the burden on tenants to prove a landlord's actions were retaliatory, which The Fair Housing Center's CEO and general counsel, George Thomas, said is a vague standard in practice.\n\nToledo's proposed ordinance would flip that burden: if a landlord raises rent, refuses to renew a lease, or reduces services after a tenant raises a health or safety concern, the landlord would have to show good cause unrelated to the complaint. A violation would become a second-degree misdemeanor under the city code. Fair Housing Center staff attorney Noah Woods said the change would make the retaliation defense easier to raise in an eviction case for a tenant without legal representation, though he cautioned it would not resolve every case. The ordinance had its first reading before Toledo City Council; the timeline for a final vote was not reported."
