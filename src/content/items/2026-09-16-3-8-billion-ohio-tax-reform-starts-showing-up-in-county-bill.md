title: "$3.8 Billion Ohio Tax Reform Starts Showing Up in County Bills"
summary: "Butler County collected slightly less in new property taxes this year than last, though officials say it's hard to isolate how much came from a $3.8 billion state property tax reform package passed last year versus other factors. The law phases in tax credits for homeowners in school districts already at the state's 20-mill floor, and Butler County commissioners layered on roughly $20 million in local relief by rolling back inside millage. County Auditor Nancy Nix expects the county's next sexennial reappraisal to show about a 20 percent average value increase."
detail: "Butler County Treasurer Mike McNamara collected $659.7 million in new property taxes this year, down slightly from $664.9 million last year, though he said it is difficult to separate how much of the change came from recent state and local tax relief versus other factors like new construction and levies. The county's delinquency rate also improved slightly, to 3.14 percent from 3.2 percent.\n\nThe state legislature passed a $3.8 billion property tax reform package last year that phases in over several years and includes credits for homeowners in districts where school levies have already hit Ohio's 20-mill floor, the point past which levy millage can no longer be reduced as values rise. County Auditor Nancy Nix said those credits total about $17.5 million in Butler County, where 8 of 10 school districts are at the floor. County commissioners separately rolled back inside millage for about $20 million in additional relief, saving roughly $32 per $100,000 of home value, plus targeted Homestead exemption relief for nearly 19,000 qualifying homeowners.\n\nNix is now finalizing the county's sexennial reappraisal and expects an average 20 percent value increase, similar to the 37 percent seen in 2023. State Rep. David Thomas, who authored much of the relief package, said the changes should limit unvoted portions of tax bills to inflationary growth instead of tracking the full value increase."
why_it_matters: "Landlords in any Ohio county at the 20-mill floor should expect the same relief mechanics now showing up in Butler County: credits and inside-millage rollbacks that blunt, but do not eliminate, the tax hit from this reappraisal cycle's roughly 20 percent value increases."
