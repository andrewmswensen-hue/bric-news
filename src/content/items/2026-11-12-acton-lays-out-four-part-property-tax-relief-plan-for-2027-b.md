title: "Acton Lays Out Four-Part Property Tax Relief Plan for 2027 Budget"
summary: "Ohio Governor-elect Amy Acton outlined four property tax priorities for her first budget: raising the Homestead Exemption income limit for qualifying seniors from $41,000 to $50,000 a year, adding a rebate of up to $1,000 for households whose tax bill rises more than 4 percent in a year, fully funding the Fair School Funding formula to reduce reliance on local levies, and curbing predatory tax lien sales to private collectors. Acton will submit her first budget draft by mid-March 2027, and lawmakers must finalize it by July 1, according to WCMH."
detail: "Acton said her first priority is expanding Ohio's Homestead Exemption, the tax relief program for qualifying seniors, disabled residents, and veterans, by raising the household income limit from $41,000 to $50,000 a year, which she said would extend relief to another 55,000 Ohioans. She also proposed a property tax spike rebate of up to $1,000 for households earning under $132,000 whose home is valued at $350,000 or less and whose tax bill rises more than 4 percent year over year.\n\nActon's other two priorities target the underlying causes of rising bills rather than direct rebates. She wants to fully fund Ohio's Fair School Funding plan, which still uses 2022 salary and spending figures, arguing that updating it would ease pressure on local districts to rely on property tax levies. She also wants to limit tax lien sales to private investors, who can collect interest as high as 18 percent and eventually foreclose if an owner does not pay; Franklin County alone made more than $10 million selling lien certificates in 2025.\n\nEnacting the plan requires cooperation from the Republican-controlled legislature. Acton will issue her first budget draft by mid-March, with a final version due by July 1, 2027, according to WCMH."
why_it_matters: "Franklin County investors who buy tax lien certificates should watch this closely: Acton specifically named lien sales to private collectors as a target, and any new restriction on interest rates or foreclosure timelines would change the economics of that strategy."
