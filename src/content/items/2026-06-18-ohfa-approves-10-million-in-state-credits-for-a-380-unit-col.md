title: "OHFA approves $10 million in state credits for a 380-unit Columbus development"
summary: "The Ohio Housing Finance Agency board approved financing on June 18 for seven affordable housing developments around the state. The largest by unit count is Central 525 Sawyer Blvd in Columbus, which received an allocation of $10 million in Ohio low-income housing tax credits and will create 380 new units for families in Franklin County. It also holds a 2025 four percent federal LIHTC reservation. Co-developers are Paths Development, S. Cain Development and Construction, and Simco Corporate. The other six approvals cover Elyria, Sandusky, Cleveland, Toledo, Albany and a southwest Ohio project."
detail: "OHFA's board approved awards from several programs to create or preserve affordable units across Ohio. The Columbus project, Central 525 Sawyer Blvd, received a $10 million Ohio low-income housing tax credit allocation on top of a 2025 four percent federal LIHTC reservation, and is planned for 380 new family units in Franklin County. Its co-developers are Paths Development LLC, S. Cain Development and Construction, and Simco Corporate. Elsewhere in the round, McGregor Landing in Elyria received authorization for up to $17.63 million in multifamily revenue bonds for 115 units for older Ohioans; Sandusky Senior Lofts received a $2.5 million housing development loan, $2 million from the Ohio Housing Trust Fund, a $12.5 million Ohio LIHTC allocation and authorization for up to $24 million in bonds for 117 senior units; Woodhill Center North in Cleveland received a $2 million housing development loan for 82 family units; Mission Point in Toledo received a $2.5 million loan for 65 units serving people facing homelessness and people with mental illness; and Albany Arms Apartments in Athens County received a $1.75 million loan to preserve 46 family units. Awards combine state credits, federal credits, bonds, trust fund dollars and loans."
why_it_matters: "380 subsidized family units is a large single addition for one Columbus submarket, and the four percent credit and bond structure means the units carry long affordability restrictions rather than converting to market rate. For anyone underwriting rents nearby, that supply is committed well before it delivers, and it is priced by program rules rather than by the market."
