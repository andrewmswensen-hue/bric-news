title: "The 1986 Tax Rule Behind the Short-Term Rental Tax Break"
summary: "A federal tax provision written in 1986 lets some short-term rental owners deduct rental losses against wage income, according to BiggerPockets. The rule hinges on two tests under IRC Section 469: the property must qualify as a short-term rental activity rather than ordinary rental property, and the owner must materially participate in operating it. A high-earning investor who buys a short-term rental, meets both tests, and pairs a cost segregation study with bonus depreciation can generate a loss large enough to reduce that year's tax bill by tens of thousands of dollars."
detail: "Under Internal Revenue Code Section 469, rental income is passive by default, and passive losses can normally only offset passive income, not wages. A high-earning W-2 investor whose rental property throws off a paper loss typically cannot use that loss to reduce salary income; the loss is suspended until the owner generates passive income elsewhere or sells. Short-term rentals sit in a different bucket. Because a property that turns over every few days functions more like a hotel than a long-term lease, the passive activity rules Congress wrote in 1986 carve out a hotel-style exception for that kind of rental activity, according to BiggerPockets. Clearing that exception is only the first step. The owner must also materially participate in running the property, a separate test with its own hour and involvement requirements. Meeting both tests lets a short-term rental owner combine a cost segregation study and bonus depreciation to generate a loss large enough to offset wage income directly, rather than being limited to the narrow $25,000 allowance under a different section of the code, one that phases out entirely for higher earners. The strategy depends entirely on the specific facts of each property and each owner's documented involvement, and does not apply to ordinary long-term rentals."
