title: "Ohio lawmakers pitch ballot issue capping property value hikes at 3%"
summary: "Ohio Reps. Jeff LaRe (R-Violet Township) and Kevin Miller (R-Newark) are pitching a constitutional amendment that would tie annual increases in a home's appraised value to inflation and cap the taxable increase at 3% a year. Value gains above that cap would be exempt from property tax as long as the owner or their spouse continues living in the home. The proposal targets the reappraisal-driven tax hikes that follow rising home values, a recurring complaint from Franklin County homeowners after recent county auditor reappraisals."
detail: "The amendment would change how Ohio calculates the taxable value of an owner-occupied home when the county auditor's periodic reappraisal shows the market value has risen. Under the proposal, the taxable increase in any year would be limited to the rate of inflation or 3%, whichever is used as the cap, with any appreciation above that threshold excluded from the tax bill for as long as the current owner or their spouse occupies the property. The cap would reset once the home changes hands to a new owner who does not qualify for the continuity provision. LaRe and Miller are pitching the idea as a response to sharp property tax increases that followed the state's triennial and sexennial reappraisal cycles, which have pushed some Franklin County bills up significantly as home values climbed. As a constitutional amendment, the proposal would need to clear the legislature and then go before Ohio voters, and it is at the earliest pitch stage with no bill language, cosponsors, or hearing scheduled yet."
why_it_matters: "The cap as described applies to owner-occupied homes, not rental property, so a Columbus landlord's own investment properties would keep facing full reappraisal-driven tax increases even if this passes, while a landlord's personal residence would not."
