detail: "An industry survey PetScreening conducted for its 2026 State of Pets in Rental Housing report, covering 673 property managers and leasing professionals, found undisclosed or unauthorized pets is their single biggest pet-related problem. The survey also found only 43 percent of renters report having a pet, compared with 71 percent of U.S. households generally, a gap of 28 points the report attributes largely to pets that went undisclosed to the landlord.\n\nThe financial exposure runs in two directions. Property damage from an undisclosed pet, chewed baseboards, damaged flooring, odor, is typically excluded from standard renter's insurance policies and from many landlord policies as well, which leaves the security deposit as the main recovery mechanism, and a deposit is frequently not enough to cover it. Bite liability is larger: the Insurance Information Institute and State Farm reported insurers paid $1.86 billion on 28,450 dog-related injury claims in 2025, an average of $65,450 per claim. Separately, HUD withdrew its assistance-animal guidance in September 2025 and issued a May 22, 2026 memo narrowing federal enforcement to animals individually trained for disability-related tasks, while state laws on emotional support animals have not changed, which landlord-side attorneys say makes ESA denials a higher-risk area to navigate without documentation."
