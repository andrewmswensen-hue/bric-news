title: "FHA weighs easing 203(k) consultant rule limiting rehab loans to 4,000 units a year"
summary: "HUD signaled it may ease requirements for the FHA's 203(k) renovation mortgage, which lets buyers finance rehab costs into one FHA-insured loan. Matt Jones, the FHA's deputy assistant secretary for single-family housing, told the Mortgage Bankers Association's annual conference Tuesday that a shortage of HUD-approved consultants limits the standard 203(k) program to 3,000 to 4,000 units nationally a year. detail: "HUD signaled possible changes to the FHA's 203(k) renovation mortgage program, which lets a borrower buy or refinance a home and roll rehabilitation costs into one FHA-insured mortgage. Speaking at the Mortgage Bankers Association's annual conference in Chicago, Matt Jones, the FHA's deputy assistant secretary for single-family housing and the agency's nominee for FHA commissioner, said a shortage of HUD-approved 203(k) consultants is the main obstacle limiting the standard program's reach. The standard 203(k), used for major rehabilitation, currently finances only 3,000 to 4,000 units a year nationally, a small share of FHA's overall single-family lending, according to Jones. He said HUD wants to look at easing the consultant requirement while keeping existing safeguards, though he gave no timeline for a change.\n\nThe program's smaller sibling, the Limited 203(k), caps financed repairs at $75,000. It already changed this year: FHA announced in June that it would allow up to four contractor draw requests instead of fewer, smoothing payment schedules for smaller rehab jobs. Ginnie Mae President Joe Gormley, expected to hand the FHA commissioner role to Jones once the Senate confirms him, said renovation lending remains underused relative to its potential, particularly in older urban housing stock."
why_it_matters: "If HUD follows through on loosening the consultant requirement, a Columbus investor using a 203(k) loan to rehab an older rental could get FHA-insured financing without waiting on one of the limited HUD-approved consultants that currently cap the program at 3,000 to 4,000 loans nationally a year."
