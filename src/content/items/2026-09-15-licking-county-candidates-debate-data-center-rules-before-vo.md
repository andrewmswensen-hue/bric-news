title: "Licking County Candidates Debate Data Center Rules Before Vote"
summary: "Democratic candidates for Ohio House, U.S. House and Licking County commissioner fielded resident questions about data centers at a Sept. 10 community forum in Johnstown. Candidates discussed the roughly 49 data centers already in the county, the NDAs officials sign with data center companies before projects become public, and proposals to require centers to pay for the roads, power grid upgrades and environmental cleanup tied to their construction."
detail: "A panel of Democratic candidates for Ohio House, Licking County commissioner and U.S. House took resident questions about data centers at a Sept. 10 community forum in Johnstown, organized by Ohio House candidate Mason Blankenship. Candidates said Licking County now has roughly 49 data centers, and residents asked how the facilities affect water supply, property taxes, electricity bills and traffic. Panelists criticized the use of non-disclosure agreements that let officials negotiate with data center companies before plans become public, and several said they would work to limit or ban such agreements if elected. Licking County commissioner candidate Kathy McLeish said data center operators should pay for road damage and an independent assessment of long-term environmental impact rather than passing those costs to residents. Candidates pointed to a lack of statewide regulation, meaning zoning and cost-sharing decisions currently fall to local governments and county auditors, who track land purchases tied to future data center sites. Several candidates said current data center construction supports an estimated 20 to 50 long-term local jobs. The forum reflects a broader debate ahead of a November election in which data centers appear as a ballot issue in numerous Ohio communities, including some in Licking County."
