title: "New Albany data center boom pushes some land prices past $600K an acre"
summary: "Homeowners along New Albany's data center corridor are negotiating to sell as Meta, Amazon, Microsoft and Intel build around them, with two parcels behind one Licking County home selling this month for $1.18 million and $1.35 million, roughly $600,000 to $700,000 an acre, according to The American Prospect. The same homeowners bought their two-acre lot in 2003 for about $100,000 an acre; the county auditor valued it this year at $442,300."
detail: "The American Prospect traced how the New Albany Company, owned by Leslie Wexner, and tech firms including Meta have assembled land around New Albany's data center corridor over two decades, often through subsidiary LLCs that did not disclose their tech-industry buyers to sellers. Starting in 2000, the company's MBJ Holdings LLC bought undeveloped farmland off Beech Road for $8,250 to $18,350 an acre; by the end of 2011, after nearby owners petitioned to annex the area into New Albany, the company bought 16 more properties at prices from $25,000 to $345,000 an acre. In 2017, that assembled 323-acre site sold to Meta for more than $36 million, versus the roughly $8 million the New Albany Company had spent to acquire it. Homeowners still in the area, including residents on Bermuda Drive in Licking County, said they are now negotiating sales under nondisclosure agreements that bar them from discussing price, with two neighboring parcels selling this month for $1.18 million and $1.35 million, or $600,000 to $700,000 an acre, compared with the roughly $100,000 an acre those owners paid in 2003. The article noted the New Albany Company used a similar approach to assemble more than 700 acres from 52 owners for the nearby Intel chip plant site in 2021 and 2022. Residents said the construction, noise and dust have made some unwilling to stay even where they could profit from selling."
why_it_matters: "Land near New Albany's data center corridor has moved from roughly $100,000 an acre in 2003 to $600,000 to $700,000 an acre in recent sales, so a Licking County parcel's current appraisal is a poor guide to what it could trade for if a hyperscale buyer's LLC comes calling."
