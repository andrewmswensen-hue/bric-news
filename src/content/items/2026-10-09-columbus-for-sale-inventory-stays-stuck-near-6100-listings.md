title: "Columbus For-Sale Inventory Stays Stuck Near 6,100 Listings"
summary: "Central Ohio MLS data cited by Keller Williams agent Barbara Gale shows only about 6,098 active listings across the Columbus metro, short of the 10,000 to 15,000 Gale says would be a normal supply level for a region of 2.23 million people. Total listings, active and pending, sit around 8,600. Gale attributes the shortage largely to owners who bought or refinanced at 2020 and 2021 rates and have little incentive to sell into today's higher rates. New construction in Dublin continues to move, while landlocked Upper Arlington sees older homes torn down and rebuilt at far higher prices."
detail: "Barbara Gale of the Gale Group at Keller Williams Consultants Realty, who has worked the Columbus and Dublin markets since the 1970s, says the metro's housing supply has not loosened even as buyer demand has cooled from pandemic-era bidding wars, according to Central Ohio MLS data.\n\nActive listings across the Columbus metro total roughly 6,098, with about 8,600 listings overall including pending sales, far below the 10,000 to 15,000 active listings Gale considers a normal supply level for a metro of 2.23 million residents. She attributes much of the shortage to homeowners who bought or refinanced at 2020 and 2021 rates in the 2.5% to 3% range and have little financial incentive to sell into current rates in the high sixes and low sevens.\n\nNew construction continues to move in Dublin, where land remains available for outward development. In landlocked Upper Arlington, where no undeveloped land remains, Gale describes builders buying older homes, tearing them down, and building new ones, citing a recent sale where a dated home on a 60 by 120 foot lot sold for $600,000 with a $2.2 million new build already under contract on the site, compared to $700,000 to $800,000 for surrounding existing homes.\n\nGale points investors toward older housing stock in Upper Arlington and Clintonville Beechwold, noting properties needing significant work increasingly go to auction. She expects the market to stay slow while rates stay elevated, with the inventory shortage keeping a floor under prices even as transaction volume slows."
