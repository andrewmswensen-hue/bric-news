title: "Ohio governor candidates detail competing property tax rollback plans"
summary: "Republican Vivek Ramaswamy and Democrat Amy Acton, Ohio's major-party candidates for governor, have released competing property tax plans ahead of November. Ramaswamy has pledged to roll back the statewide property tax base to pre-pandemic levels and cap future growth, calling it the largest property tax cut in state history, though he has not put a dollar figure on it or said how it affects levies voters approved since 2021."
detail: "Property taxes and data centers have become the two dominant issues in Ohio's 2026 governor's race, with both Ramaswamy and Acton releasing plans this week. Ramaswamy said his rollback would bring the property tax base back to its pre-pandemic level in aggregate, paired with a statewide cap on future growth, and said he does not believe it would require cuts to local police, fire or schools. He also said he wants to move Ohio schools toward more statewide funding over time and away from heavy reliance on local property taxes, though he described that as a long-run goal rather than an immediate change. He did not specify how the rollback would apply to levies Ohio voters have approved since 2021.\n\nActon's plan is narrower, aimed at middle-income and older or disabled homeowners through an expanded homestead tax exemption and a rebate that triggers when a homeowner's property tax bill rises more than 4% in a year; her campaign says she would also fully fund Ohio's Fair School Funding Plan. Both plans are campaign proposals, not enacted law, and any changes would need to go through the state legislature."
why_it_matters: "Whichever property tax plan advances next year could change how a Columbus rental property owner's tax bill is calculated: Ramaswamy's rollback would reset the statewide tax base, while Acton's plan works through exemptions and a rebate trigger rather than a broad rollback."
