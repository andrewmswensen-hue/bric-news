title: "Toledo Weighs Local Anti-Retaliation Law to Shield Tenants"
summary: "Toledo City Council held a first reading of an ordinance that would add a new \"Retaliation Prohibited\" chapter to the city's municipal code, protecting tenants from eviction, rent increases or service cutoffs after they raise health and safety complaints. Under the proposal, landlords, not tenants, would carry the burden of proving they had good cause for an eviction or rent increase, and violations would become a second-degree misdemeanor."
detail: "The ordinance responds to complaints from tenants at buildings such as Saxon House, where a tenant union has organized around mold and water issues. Matty Watkins, the union steward there, said fear of retaliation keeps many renters from raising problems with a landlord or property manager at all. Ohio already bars landlord retaliation under state law, but George Thomas, CEO and general counsel of Toledo's Fair Housing Center, said the state standard is vague and puts the burden of proof on the tenant. Toledo's proposed ordinance would flip that burden onto the property owner, who would have to show a legitimate, non-retaliatory reason for an eviction or rent increase. It would also make retaliation a second-degree misdemeanor under city code and, attorneys say, give tenants without legal representation a clearer defense to raise in eviction court. The measure has had only a first reading before Toledo City Council; it still needs further readings and a vote before taking effect. Fair Housing Center attorney Noah Woods said the ordinance would not resolve every retaliation dispute but would make the defense easier to assert for tenants who cannot afford a lawyer."
