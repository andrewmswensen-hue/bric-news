title: "Some Ohio school districts pitch income tax instead of property tax hikes"
summary: "Several school districts in the Toledo area, including Springfield and Sylvania, are asking voters this November to approve an earned income tax instead of raising property taxes. The Northwest Ohio Realtors Board is backing Springfield's proposal because it would not add to property tax bills while still funding schools tied to home values. Sylvania's version pairs the income tax with a small property tax rollback. Similar income-tax-for-schools measures have failed at the ballot before, and most Ohio districts that use one are concentrated in the western part of the state."
detail: "Facing years of what districts describe as underfunding from the state, several Toledo-area school districts are turning to earned income taxes rather than property tax levies to raise money this November. Springfield Schools has drawn a letter of support from the Northwest Ohio Realtors Board, which favors the approach because it does not raise property tax bills while still funding the schools that the board says support home values. Sylvania is pursuing its own income tax measure alongside a small rollback of its existing property tax. An earned income tax does not touch Social Security income, so retirees and residents without earned income would not see a direct increase. According to Policy Matters Ohio, most Ohio school districts that already use an income tax are in the western part of the state, and similar measures have failed at the ballot in these districts before. Voters decide in November whether Springfield's and Sylvania's proposals pass."
