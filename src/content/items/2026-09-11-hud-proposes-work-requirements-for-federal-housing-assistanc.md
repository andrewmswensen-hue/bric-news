title: "HUD proposes work requirements for federal housing assistance"
summary: "The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development unveiled a proposed rule that would let local public housing authorities require able-bodied adults on federal housing assistance to work up to 40 hours a week and cap benefits after two years or more. HUD Secretary Scott Turner said seniors, children and people with disabilities would be exempt. The rule needs a public comment period before taking effect."
detail: "HUD announced the proposed rule alongside officials from the Housing Authority of Champaign County and the Vermilion Housing Authority, the first two agencies to join HUD's new \"Work and Dignity Coalition.\" Under the proposal, local public housing agencies would gain the authority to require up to 40 hours per week of work from able-bodied adults receiving federal housing assistance and to set time limits of two years or more on that assistance. Turner said the goal is to reduce long-term dependency on government support and open spots for families on waiting lists, and stressed that seniors, children and people with disabilities are not affected. Champaign County Executive Director Lily Walton said her agency already runs self-sufficiency programs under existing federal flexibility provisions and supports the broader rule. Vermilion Housing Authority Executive Director Jaclyn Vinson said her agency hopes to build similar programs. The proposal is not final: it must clear a public comment period, and HUD has not set a timeline for when, or whether, individual public housing authorities would choose to adopt work requirements once the rule takes effect."
why_it_matters: "Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority has not joined HUD's coalition, but landlords who accept Housing Choice Vouchers should watch whether it does, since a local work requirement would change how, and how long, some voucher-holding tenants stay eligible for the assistance that covers part of their rent."
