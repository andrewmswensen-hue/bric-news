title: "New federal housing law speeds environmental review for small builds"
summary: "The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, now federal law, streamlines environmental review for housing development, housing policy analysts told Shelterforce and Next City. The law exempts new construction of up to four units and rehabilitation of one- to four-unit buildings from certain environmental impact statement requirements under the National Environmental Policy Act. Analysts said the law's effect on housing supply depends on future congressional funding and HUD's capacity to implement it, since the law does not mandate new housing production."
detail: "The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, which supporters call the largest federal housing supply bill since 2008, makes a series of technical changes meant to remove friction from housing development rather than fund new construction directly. Its biggest changes streamline environmental review under the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA), the 1970 law requiring public notice and environmental and economic impact evaluation before many federally linked development projects can proceed. According to the Bipartisan Policy Center, a quarter of NEPA reviews requiring a full environmental impact statement take more than five years to complete. Section 206 of the new law, Unlocking Housing Supply Through Streamlined and Modernized Reviews, exempts several categories of housing activity from that review, including new construction of up to four units, rehabilitation of one- to four-unit buildings, and acquisition of public land for housing. Housing policy analysts who spoke with Shelterforce and Next City, including Tram Hoang of PolicyLink, said the law does not mandate that any new housing actually gets built and that its real impact depends on future congressional appropriations and on HUD's capacity to carry it out. Mark Kudlowitz of the Local Initiatives Support Corporation, which helped shape parts of the bill, called it significant but noted most of what it promises will not happen automatically."
why_it_matters: "An investor rehabbing or building a small multifamily property, four units or fewer, may now face a shorter, cheaper federal environmental review on projects that previously could have been delayed by it."
