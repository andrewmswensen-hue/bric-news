title: "Federal judge blocks HUD's halt of fair housing enforcement grants"
summary: "A federal judge in Massachusetts granted a temporary restraining order Aug. 27 blocking HUD from halting Private Enforcement Initiative funding that supports more than 100 fair housing organizations nationwide, according to Jurist.org. U.S. District Judge Myong J. Joun found the National Fair Housing Alliance, which sued over the cut, was likely to succeed on claims HUD acted arbitrarily and capriciously, failing to adequately explain the change when it acted and ignoring organizations' long-standing reliance on the funding for their enforcement work."
detail: "HUD moved earlier this year to end Private Enforcement Initiative (PEI) grants under the Fair Housing Initiatives Program, which fund private organizations that investigate housing discrimination complaints and bring enforcement actions. The National Fair Housing Alliance sued, arguing the cutoff violated federal rulemaking requirements.\n\nJudge Joun found HUD likely acted arbitrarily and capriciously. The agency relied on what the court called an impermissible affidavit to justify the change and did not adequately explain the funding restructuring when it first announced the decision, only later in litigation. The court also found HUD ignored the reliance interest of the more than 100 organizations that had built their operations around PEI funding, and that ending it would not serve HUD's stated goal of broadening participation in fair housing work.\n\nSeparately, the judge rejected new conditions HUD had attached to the funding tied to immigration status, religion and gender ideology, ruling those requirements fell outside the agency's discretion and were unexplained. The TRO halts HUD's funding cutoff while the underlying case proceeds. HUD's main defense, that the funding decision was a matter of unreviewable agency discretion, did not persuade the court, which found the change caused irreparable harm to both the organizations and people seeking fair housing help."
