detail: "A frozen pipe is first an insurance problem, not just a maintenance one, according to BiggerPockets. Most property insurance policies contain a freezing exclusion: wording that denies coverage for damage from frozen household systems unless the owner used reasonable care, typically defined as either keeping the heat running or draining the plumbing and shutting off the water supply. Many versions of that clause apply specifically when a property sits vacant or unoccupied, such as during a gap between tenants, but some carriers write the exclusion without limiting it to vacancy at all, meaning an occupied rental with a tenant away for a weekend during a hard freeze could still face a denied claim.\n\nThe financial stakes are real. State Farm alone reported paying more than $628 million on over 20,000 freeze-related water damage claims between 2024 and June 2025, with the average payout exceeding $30,000. A single hard freeze overnight, especially during a vacancy or lease turnover, can soak a property before anyone notices the leak.\n\nThe practical takeaway for landlords is to read the freezing exclusion in their own policy before winter, not after a claim, and to document whatever winterization steps the policy requires, whether that is confirming a minimum thermostat setting during vacancies or draining exposed lines, so that a claim does not get denied over a condition of coverage the owner did not realize applied."
why_it_matters: "A Columbus-area landlord with a unit sitting vacant between tenants this winter should check whether their policy's freezing exclusion applies only when the property is vacant, since that is exactly when a denied claim is most likely and when a documented heat-on or water-shutoff step can be the difference between a covered loss and a $30,000 bill out of pocket."
