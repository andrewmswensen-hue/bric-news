title: "Bipartisan Ohio mayors pitch workforce housing fund for next governor"
summary: "The Ohio Mayors Alliance, representing 31 of the state's larger cities, released a four-part policy agenda for the incoming governor and legislature that includes a workforce housing program with financing for water, sewer, and transportation infrastructure. The plan would create an Ohio Core City Transformation Fund, financed by reserving 20% of the state's annual growth in sales and use tax collections, to offer grants and zero-interest loans for site preparation and redevelopment."
detail: "Beyond the workforce housing plank, the mayors' proposal includes a fund to help cities hire, train, and retain first responders, totaling 10% of what local governments spend on police and fire payrolls, and a broader \"reset\" of the state-local relationship, including a bipartisan task force on state revenue-sharing programs, a Home Rule Commission, and a standing forum for state-local collaboration. The Alliance said the four planks were developed jointly by Republican and Democratic mayors and are intended to be adopted regardless of who wins the governor's race. The workforce housing piece would direct state dollars toward the kind of water, sewer, and transportation infrastructure that often determines whether a housing project pencils out, rather than funding housing units directly. None of the four planks has been introduced as legislation; the release is a policy recommendation timed to reach the next governor and incoming General Assembly."
why_it_matters: "If adopted, dedicated state funding for water, sewer, and transportation infrastructure tied to workforce housing could lower the public-infrastructure cost of new residential projects in growing Central Ohio suburbs, though the plan remains a proposal with no legislation introduced yet."
