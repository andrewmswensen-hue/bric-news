title: "Ohio governor candidates detail dueling property tax rollback plans"
summary: "Republican Vivek Ramaswamy and Democrat Amy Acton have both released proposals to cut Ohio property taxes and restrict new data centers ahead of the 2026 governor's race, according to the Statehouse News Bureau. Ramaswamy said he would sign an order requiring new data centers to generate their own power and would roll property taxes back to pre-pandemic levels, calling it the largest property tax rollback in Ohio history, though he would not put a dollar figure on it or say how it would affect local levies passed since 2021."
detail: "In an interview for 'The State of Ohio,' Ramaswamy said excess power that data centers generate beyond their own needs would go into a power purchase agreement benefiting nearby residents, though when pressed he clarified this would only offset the power portion of a bill, not distribution and transmission charges, so it would not eliminate electric bills entirely. He also said he is considering requiring landscaping around data centers to reduce noise and visual impact, and that new projects should prioritize abandoned industrial sites over farmland.\n\nOn property taxes, Ramaswamy pledged to roll rates back to pre-pandemic levels without naming a dollar figure or specifying how the rollback would treat local levies approved by voters since 2021, saying only that the net effect would be worked out 'in aggregate' through legislation next year. He said the cut would not come at the expense of police, fire or school funding.\n\nActon, the Democratic candidate, has proposed a moratorium on new data centers unless projects cover 100% of their own utility costs, are built on brownfields or other previously developed industrial sites rather than farmland, and are constructed with union labor, among other conditions. Both campaigns have made data centers and property taxes into the two leading issues of the race, per the Statehouse News Bureau."
why_it_matters: "Either plan, if enacted, would be the biggest change to Ohio property tax bills in years for anyone holding rental property, but Ramaswamy's aggregate rollback promise leaves open whether levies Franklin County voters approved since 2021, the ones funding schools and libraries, would be cut along with the rest or preserved and made up elsewhere."
