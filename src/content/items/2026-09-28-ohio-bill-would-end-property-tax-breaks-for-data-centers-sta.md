title: "Ohio bill would end property tax breaks for data centers statewide"
summary: "Ohio House Bill 999, introduced by Rep. Daniel Troy (D-Willowick), would bar state and local agencies from granting property tax exemptions to data centers and examine ending existing abatements. Troy said removing the breaks would shift tax burden off residential property owners. Columbus gave a Google-linked data center 15 years of property tax exemption in 2021, an estimated $54 million break, and Amazon's Hilliard data centers saved $5.4 million in property taxes in 2024."
detail: "House Bill 999 would prohibit any Ohio state or local agency from granting new property tax exemptions to data centers and direct a review of whether existing abatements can be terminated. Rep. Daniel Troy, D-Willowick, the bill's sponsor, said the measure would make large data center operators pay their full share of property taxes, which he said would lessen the burden on residential property owners. Troy has worked with Rep. David Thomas, R-Jefferson, on property tax relief generally, though Thomas is not a cosponsor. Property tax abatements for data centers are granted locally and vary by municipality: Columbus approved 15 years of property tax exemption for a Google-linked data center in 2021, an estimated $54 million break, while Amazon saved $5.4 million on its Hilliard data centers in 2024. New Albany, a hub for the industry, typically grants 10 to 15 year abatements, with larger projects reaching 30 years, while requiring a minimum annual payment. HB 999 goes further than an earlier bill, HB 646, which would have capped property tax abatements at 50% rather than banning them outright and stalled over a separate dispute about the state's sales tax exemption for data centers, which Troy said has cost Ohio $1.6 billion since 2025. Gov. Mike DeWine has already paused new sales tax exemption agreements. HB 999 awaits a committee assignment and is unlikely to see action before the November election."
why_it_matters: "Central Ohio communities including Hilliard and New Albany have used these abatements to land data center projects, so a statewide ban would change what local governments can offer and could shift more of the tax base onto existing residential and rental property owners in those districts."
