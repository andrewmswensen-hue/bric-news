title: "HUD to bar mixed-status immigrant families from housing aid"
summary: "The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development plans to bar households with mixed immigration status from federally funded housing starting next month, ending the prorated aid that let such families keep a reduced subsidy. HUD Secretary Scott Turner said the change frees subsidies for veterans and elderly or disabled citizens."
detail: "Federal housing aid has always been limited to U.S. citizens and people with an eligible immigration status such as a green card, but mixed-status households, where some family members qualify and others do not, have long received a prorated subsidy covering only the eligible members. HUD's new rule ends that practice, requiring agencies to exclude the entire household once it starts next month. According to CalMatters, housing authorities are scrambling to shift affected families onto city, county, state, or philanthropic funding before the change takes effect, with success varying by county based on available resources. The agency warned the change could also reduce total households served: in some counties, a voucher vacated by a departing mixed-status family is retired instead of reissued, and mixed-status families generally pay more in rent and receive smaller subsidies than citizen households, so replacing them could mean less funding available overall. California alone could see about 7,000 households affected, according to the report."
why_it_matters: "Any Columbus-area landlord who rents to a voucher holder should expect the local public housing authority to re-verify every household member's immigration status starting next month, since a mixed-status household that previously kept a reduced subsidy can now lose the voucher entirely under HUD's new rule."
