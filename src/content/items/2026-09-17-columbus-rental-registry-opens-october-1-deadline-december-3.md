title: "Columbus Rental Registry Opens October 1, Deadline December 31"
summary: "Columbus landlords have a 92-day window, October 1 through December 31, 2026, to register every residential rental property with the city under Chapter 4515 of the Columbus City Code. The rule applies to single-family homes, duplexes and condos with no small-owner exemption, only four narrow exemptions tied to other existing city registries or permits. Registration costs $15 per dwelling unit, capped at $1,500 per complex, and owners of 10 or more units must also name a local operator living within 100 miles of Columbus. As of September 1, the city had not yet published a registration portal."
detail: "Columbus City Council passed ordinance 0923-2026 on April 20, 2026 by an 8-1 vote, creating a Registry of Residential Rental Properties under Chapter 4515 of the Columbus City Codes. The registry collects ownership information, a responsible party, unit counts, parcel IDs and an emergency contact for every residential rental property in the city.\n\nThe exemptions are narrow: properties already covered by the Vacant Building Registry, hotel, motel or short-term rental permits, dormitories, or facilities licensed under a separate state chapter. Single-family homes, duplexes and condos are not exempt, and there is no carve-out by portfolio size. A single rented condo carries the same registration duty as a 200-unit complex, and there is no phase-in by unit count, zip code or date.\n\nThis is a second, separate filing from the existing Ohio requirement to register owner and parcel information with the county auditor under state law, which carries its own $150 non-compliance fine.\n\nRegistration costs $15 per dwelling unit annually, capped at $1,500 per complex, confirmed through follow-up legislation in July 2026 that froze the fee schedule for at least three years. Owners face a civil penalty of $2 per dwelling unit per day for failing to comply with a formal notice of violation. Registration must be renewed annually, and owners have 30 days to update the city after any change, including a new property manager, ownership change or a change in emergency contact.\n\nAs of early September, the city had not yet published a registration portal, so the near-term task for owners is gathering parcel IDs, unit counts and contact information ahead of the October 1 opening."
