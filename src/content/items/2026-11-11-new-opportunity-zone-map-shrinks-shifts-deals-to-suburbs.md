detail: "Bisnow reported that the Opportunity Zone 2.0 map, released last week, designates 6,576 census tracts nationwide, down from 8,764 tracts under the original 2018 Opportunity Zones program. An analysis from FBT Gibbons Managing Director Frances Kern Mennone found that about two-thirds of the tracts on the new map were not part of OZ 1.0, meaning many communities with no prior designation now qualify, while 481 counties that had at least one designated tract under OZ 1.0 have none under the new map. The first round of the program drew more than $110 billion in investment, concentrated heavily in large urban cores, according to Bisnow. Fund managers are raising new capital for OZ 2.0 even though the program has about 25% fewer designated tracts overall, which Kern Mennone's analysis suggests will push more investment toward suburbs and secondary markets that received little OZ capital the first time. Federal officials prioritized rural investment in drawing the new map, and an Economic Innovation Group analysis cited by Bisnow found rural tracts make up about 39% of the designated zones, up from 37% under OZ 1.0."
why_it_matters: "Opportunity Zone status determines whether an investor can defer or reduce capital gains tax on a sale by reinvesting in that tract, so a Central Ohio investor should check the new map against any Columbus-area property under consideration, since a tract that qualified under OZ 1.0 may have lost that status under OZ 2.0, or a previously ineligible tract may now qualify."
