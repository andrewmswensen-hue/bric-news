title: "Athens County reappraisal lifts home values, with state tax changes ahead"
summary: "Athens County homeowners are receiving 2026 reappraisal notices showing higher values than the last reappraisal three years ago, in many cases by tens of thousands of dollars. WOUB found homes sold for an average of 19 percent more than the new appraised value, based on 170 single-family sales through August, with a median price of $271,250. Property tax is calculated on 35 percent of appraised value, and higher bills begin next year. The report also describes new state laws that may soften future increases."
detail: "WOUB reports that Athens County property owners have received letters showing the auditor's new value for their homes, and for most the figure is higher than at the last reappraisal three years ago. County Auditor Jill Davidson told the outlet her office is working from sales that are two or three years old because of state deadlines, and that appraisals are done from the outside without interior visits.\n\nWOUB analyzed 170 open-market single-family sales from January through August. The median price was $271,250, and homes sold on average for 19 percent more than the 2026 appraised value. Nearly a third sold for more than $100,000 over their 2023 appraisal. Because appraisals trail the market, the report says another sizable increase is possible at the next reappraisal unless prices fall.\n\nThe article walks through the math. Ohio taxes 35 percent of appraised value, so a $300,000 home has $105,000 in taxable value. That figure is multiplied by the millage. Older levies are reduced as values rise, but a 20-mill floor limits how far school operating levies can fall. Higher bills start in 2027, and state lawmakers have passed measures intended to moderate the increases, according to WOUB."
why_it_matters: "The 35 percent assessment rule and the 20-mill floor are state law, so the same arithmetic sets tax bills on rentals in Franklin, Delaware, and Licking counties; underwriting a purchase on the old bill understates carrying costs after a reappraisal."
