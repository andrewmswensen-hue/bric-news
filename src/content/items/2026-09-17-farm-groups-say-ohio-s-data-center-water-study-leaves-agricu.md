title: "Farm groups say Ohio's data center water study leaves agriculture out"
summary: "An Ohio Chamber of Commerce Research Foundation study found central Ohio's water resources are broadly sufficient for data center growth through 2030, but farm advocates say the study understates longer-term water competition. A 2025 Ohio EPA and Department of Natural Resources study projected industrial water demand in a 15-county region, driven mainly by data centers, could rise 120% by 2050, while agricultural irrigation demand could reach 110 million gallons a day by 2040."
detail: "The Ohio Chamber of Commerce Research Foundation study, released in June, examined water and electricity availability for data center growth in central and southwestern Ohio and did not extend its forecast past 2030, citing uncertainty from evolving cooling technology. It found data centers currently use about 2% of central Ohio's total water consumption, rising to a projected 8% by 2030, a pace the foundation's executive director, Demetrius Thomas, said does not amount to a rapid increase. Licking County, home to 49 of Ohio's roughly 240 data centers, more than any other county, was excluded from the study for lack of comparable data.\n\nA separate, longer-range 2025 study by the Ohio EPA and Ohio Department of Natural Resources found industrial water demand across the 15-county region could climb 120% between 2021 and 2050, while Ohio farmers, most of whom do not currently irrigate, may need to begin regularly irrigating fields during the July-through-September growing season as demand tightens. Ohio Farmers Union vice president Bryn Bird said agriculture is routinely left out of these planning conversations. Both the study and Bird noted that on-site power generation built to supply electricity to data centers could ultimately consume more water than the data centers themselves. The American Farm Bureau Federation and Ohio Farm Bureau have separately raised concerns about data centers' effect on farmland loss and local water competition."
why_it_matters: "A Chamber-commissioned water study stopping its forecast at 2030 does not answer the question that matters for a 20- or 30-year rental property hold in Licking County, which has more data centers than any other Ohio county: whether municipal water capacity keeps pace with both data center and residential growth past that date."
