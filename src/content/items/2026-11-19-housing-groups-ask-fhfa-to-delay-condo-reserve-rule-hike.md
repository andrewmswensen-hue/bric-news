title: "Housing groups ask FHFA to delay condo reserve rule hike"
summary: "A coalition of housing and lending groups asked the Federal Housing Finance Agency to delay a planned increase in condo association reserve funding requirements by one year, warning associations need more time to prepare. The requirement affects eligibility for conventional loans sold to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac."
detail: "The coalition, which includes mortgage and condo industry groups, told FHFA that condominium associations typically operate on annual budget cycles and cannot easily adjust funding mid-year, and some will need updated reserve studies, board approvals or homeowner votes before changing contribution levels. Lenders, servicers and condo management companies would also need time to update underwriting systems and project reviews.\n\nThe letter raised a second concern: associations that cannot meet the higher reserve requirement, or qualify under an alternative reserve study provision, could lose eligibility for financing through Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. The coalition said that could shrink the pool of buyers able to get a loan, make it harder for current owners to sell or refinance, and put downward pressure on resale values, with the effect likely sharper in older buildings, smaller associations and communities with more modest-income homeowners.\n\nA third concern is cost: Fannie Mae has estimated the higher reserve requirement could add an average of $13 to $14 a month to a condo owner's assessment, though the coalition said the actual increase would vary by association depending on how much of the gap gets funded through regular dues. The coalition is asking FHFA for a one-year extension before the new reserve standard takes effect, according to HousingWire."
why_it_matters: "A Columbus-area investor who owns a condo unit, or is weighing one as a rental, should watch whether FHFA grants the delay: if a condo association can't meet the higher reserve bar, the unit could lose eligibility for conventional Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac financing, making it harder to finance a purchase or a future buyer's loan."
