detail: "The average mortgage holder with a single-family home paid $209 a month for property insurance in the second quarter, up 8.7 percent from a year earlier and nearly 80 percent since the start of 2020, according to the September ICE Mortgage Monitor. Insurance now makes up 9.6 percent of the average monthly mortgage payment, making an accurate estimate increasingly important when lenders calculate a borrower's debt to income ratio. The report found homeowners who switched insurance carriers over the past year reduced premiums by an average of 6.6 percent, saving about $440 a year, or nearly $37 a month, compared with those who kept their existing carrier and saw premiums rise 10.4 percent. Switchers did not appear to trade down on coverage: their limits rose an average of 7.3 percent while deductibles fell 1.4 percent. Premium growth varies widely by market. Some of the largest annual increases hit Greenville, South Carolina (15.8 percent), Honolulu (14.7 percent) and Minneapolis (13.1 percent), while Miami and New Orleans, the two most expensive insurance markets, saw smaller increases despite already high costs. Nationally, annual premium growth has slowed from a peak of 15.1 percent at the end of 2024, but higher coverage limits still account for roughly two thirds of the increase."
