title: "ROAD Act housing law faces funding and HUD staffing delays"
summary: "The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act created about a dozen new federal housing programs, but implementation is stalling on funding and staffing, a Bipartisan Policy Center and National Association of Affordable Housing Lenders panel found, HousingWire reported. The House's fiscal 2027 HUD funding bill, passed before ROAD became law, does not fund its provisions, and the Senate bill is still pending; some programs may not be funded until fiscal year 2028."
detail: "The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, described as the first comprehensive federal housing law in decades, authorized roughly a dozen new programs meant to expand affordable and market-rate housing supply, but most require congressional funding that has not been appropriated. The House's fiscal 2027 Transportation-HUD bill, passed in June before ROAD's enactment, includes no money for the law's provisions, and the Senate has not released its own bill; Bipartisan Policy Center associate director Kristen Klurfield said some programs may go unfunded until fiscal 2028. HUD must also write nine new rules to implement the law, complicated by staffing that has fallen from 8,800 employees in fiscal 2024 to about 5,800 now, a 34% drop. HUD rules typically take 18 months to two years to finalize, pushing some ROAD provisions into 2028. One rule with real reach: HUD plans to drop the permanent chassis requirement from its manufactured-housing definition, letting manufacturers build stackable, more site-built-looking manufactured homes, though state and local zoning still has to catch up before that changes what gets built. The law also lets USDA decouple Section 515 rental assistance from maturing loans, so some rural affordable projects keep funding after their mortgage ends, and it raises HOME program income limits and purchase price caps, changes advocates say will take time to reach the ground."
why_it_matters: "For an investor watching for new supply-side federal programs, HUD's rulemaking backlog means most of ROAD's incentives, including the manufactured-housing chassis rule and HOME program changes, are unlikely to take effect before 2027 or 2028."
