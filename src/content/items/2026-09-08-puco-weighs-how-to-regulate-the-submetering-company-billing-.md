title: "PUCO weighs how to regulate the submetering company billing Ohio apartments"
summary: "The Public Utilities Commission of Ohio is deciding how to regulate Nationwide Energy Partners, a company that buys electricity wholesale and resells it at retail rates to residents of Ohio apartment complexes, after the Ohio Supreme Court ruled in May that NEP is operating as a utility under state law and sent the case back to PUCO. AEP Ohio and Nationwide Energy Partners are disputing how state law should treat the practice, while the Ohio Consumers' Counsel argues that apartment residents should not bear the cost of the unresolved regulatory fight between the two companies."
detail: "Submetering companies like Nationwide Energy Partners buy electricity wholesale and sell it on to residents of multi-unit complexes at retail rates, a billing arrangement common in Ohio apartment buildings that do not have individual utility accounts for each unit. PUCO initially found NEP was not operating as a utility because landlords, not NEP, were technically supplying the power. In a unanimous May ruling, the Ohio Supreme Court rejected that reasoning: Justice Patrick DeWine wrote that regardless of the landlords' role, there was no question NEP itself is in the business of supplying electricity.\n\nThe case is not PUCO's first pass at the question. In 2020 the commission decided submetering companies fell outside its jurisdiction, and the Ohio Supreme Court sent that decision back as well. With the case now returned again, AEP Ohio and NEP are sparring over how state law should classify and regulate the practice. The Ohio Consumers' Counsel has filed with the commission arguing that apartment residents, who the agency says neither created nor control the underlying dispute, should not bear its costs or consequences while PUCO works out an answer."
why_it_matters: "Any Columbus landlord whose apartment complex bills electricity through a submetering company like Nationwide Energy Partners is watching a live question about how that billing gets regulated, and possibly priced, once PUCO rules."
