title: "Ohio utilities' data center demand forecasts are pushing up power bills"
summary: "AEP Ohio's Ohio Power Co., the state's largest regulated utility, expects electricity demand to nearly triple over the next decade, largely because of planned data center projects, and is using that forecast to justify new grid investment. Critics, including the Ohio Manufacturers' Association Energy Group and the Ohio Consumers' Counsel, say the projections are unreliable because many announced data centers may never get built, leaving ratepayers to cover infrastructure built on inflated demand estimates."
detail: "AEP Ohio's transmission subsidiary anticipates demand nearly tripling over the next decade, driven mostly by data center growth; as of February the utility's forecast reflected signed contracts for 17.8 gigawatts of data center demand through 2035, down from an earlier 2025 estimate. Researcher Jonathan Koomey of Koomey Analytics said utility forecasts generally assume recent trends continue, but computing efficiency gains and uncertain AI demand make that assumption shaky, and developers often shop the same project to multiple states looking for the best deal.\n\nThe forecasts matter because they drive two kinds of costs: capacity prices, which pay power plants to be available for demand spikes and have risen more than tenfold in the PJM Interconnection region since May 2025, and new transmission infrastructure that utilities build today based on tomorrow's projected need. A March 2026 filing from Public Utilities Commission of Ohio staff noted some double-counting in AEP Ohio's data center demand figures. The Ohio Consumers' Counsel has asked federal regulators to scrutinize the cost-effectiveness of these transmission projects, warning that ratepayers will be stuck paying for grid buildout even if the underlying data center demand never materializes."
why_it_matters: "AEP Ohio serves most of Columbus, so a rate base built on data center demand that never shows up is a cost that lands on every AEP customer's bill, landlords included, whether or not their properties are anywhere near a data center."
