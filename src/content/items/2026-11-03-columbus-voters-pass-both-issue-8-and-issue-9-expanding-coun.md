detail: "Columbus voters passed both competing City Council ballot measures on election night, unofficial results show, with 81.76% approving Issue 8 and 57.23% approving Issue 9 with all precincts reporting, WCMH reported. Because the two charter amendments do not directly conflict, both are expected to take effect, which means the system proposed under Issue 9 governs going forward.\n\nIssue 8, from the grassroots coalition Our City, Our Say, limits election and recall of each of the nine district representatives to residents of that district rather than the whole city. Issue 9, put forward by sitting council members, keeps that district-only voting rule but also adds four new council members elected citywide, two seated in 2028 and two in 2030, bringing the council to 13 members with staggered four-year terms. It lowers district council candidate signature requirements from 250 to 150 (citywide candidates still need 250), raises the allowable population deviation between council districts from 1% to 7.5%, and creates an independent, citizen-led commission to redraw district boundaries before the 2029 election, to be reappointed every 10 years starting in 2031. A prior BRIC item estimated the pay-raise cost of a 13-member council at roughly $793,000 a year if council later adopts a recommended compensation increase; that increase has not yet been adopted."
