title: "Columbus rental registry opens Oct. 1; all owners must register by Dec. 31"
summary: "Columbus City Code Chapter 4515 requires owners of residential rental property to register with the city between October 1 and December 31, 2026. Council passed the ordinance 8-1 on April 20. Registration costs $15 per dwelling unit each year, capped at $1,500 per complex. Single-family homes, duplexes and condos are included, and only four narrow exemptions apply. Owners of 10 or more units must name a Local Operator living within 100 miles of Columbus. As of September 1, the city had not published a registration portal."
detail: "Columbus City Council passed ordinance 0923-2026 on April 20, 2026, by an 8-1 vote, creating a Registry of Residential Rental Properties under Chapter 4515 of the city code. The registry collects who owns each rental, who manages it day to day, the unit count, the parcel identification and an emergency contact.\n\nThe first registration window runs October 1 through December 31, 2026, with annual renewal after that and 30 days to report any change. According to RL Property Management, the ordinance draws no line at portfolio size: one rented condo carries the same obligation as a 200-unit building. Four exemptions apply: properties on the Vacant Building Registry, hotel, motel or short-term rental permit properties, dormitories, and facilities licensed under Chapter 4561.\n\nThe annual fee is $15 per dwelling unit, capped at $1,500 per complex. Owners of 10 or more units must name a Local Operator whose primary residence is within 100 miles of Columbus. The routine three-year inspection covers exteriors, common areas and shared mechanical systems, and unit interiors are excluded unless the tenant or owner asks.\n\nThe enacted civil penalty is $2.00 per dwelling unit per day for failing to comply with a Notice of Violation, according to the source. A $10 per day minimum and $2,500 cap that circulated online came from a January 2026 draft. The registry is separate from the county auditor filing Ohio law already requires under ORC 5323.02. As of September 1, the city had not published a registration portal, so the near-term work is gathering parcel IDs, unit counts and contacts. The source notes it is informational and not legal advice."
