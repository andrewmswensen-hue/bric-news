summary: "National median monthly mortgage payments for new purchase loans rose to $2,215 in September, up $53 from August and $148 over the past year, according to Mortgage Bankers Association data reported by Scotsman Guide. The 2.5% monthly increase reversed three months of improving affordability. detail: "The Mortgage Bankers Association's Purchase Applications Payment Index, which tracks how new monthly mortgage payments move relative to income, rose about 3.1% year over year in September, according to Scotsman Guide. A rising index signals weaker affordability. The national median payment applied for on a new purchase mortgage was $2,215 in September, up 2.5% from August and more than 7% above year-ago levels, outpacing wage growth. New-construction loan payments rose more than 4% to a median of $2,307. MBA vice president Edward Seiler attributed the shift to higher mortgage rates, which moved from about 6.8% through August to 7.5% by the end of September. The increase tracked a run-up in 10-year Treasury yields, the benchmark for 30-year mortgage pricing, which rose from 4.75% to 5.3% over the month before pulling back to just under 5% in recent weeks. Affordability worsened in 39 states in September, the MBA reported. Conventional loan payments backed by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac rose 6.6% year over year to $2,244, while FHA-insured payments rose 6.3% to $1,906. President Trump signed an executive order the prior week aimed at easing mortgage costs; industry groups welcomed the move ahead of what they expect to be an active legislative year for housing finance in 2027."
