title: "Columbus rezoning plan covers 40% of city in new phase"
summary: "The city of Columbus is updating its zoning code for the first time in more than 70 years, and the latest phase of the 'Zone In' initiative covers more than 40% of the city, according to NBC4 WCMH-TV. Project manager Luis Teba said the changes aim to open up housing and business opportunities as the city grows. Forest Park resident Joseph Higginbotham said he learned a wooded area behind his 35-year home could become a four-story apartment building under the proposal. Public comment closed Oct. 24; City Council hearings are set for Nov. 5 and Nov. 18."
detail: "Columbus is in the latest phase of 'Zone In,' a multi-year rewrite of the city's zoning code that project manager Luis Teba says is the first comprehensive update in more than 70 years. More than 40% of the city is included in this phase's proposed map and code changes, which the city says are meant to open up more housing and business opportunities to accommodate growth. The change has drawn concern from longtime residents whose neighborhoods fall inside the affected area. Forest Park resident Joseph Higginbotham, who has lived in his house for 35 years, told NBC4 that a wooded area and creek behind his backyard could be rezoned for residential or commercial use, potentially allowing buildings as tall as four stories roughly ten feet from his property line. The city collected public comment on the proposed map and code through Oct. 24 and says every comment is being reviewed, with map changes already being made in response. Two public hearings follow: Nov. 5 from 5 to 7 p.m. to share what the city heard during the comment period, and Nov. 18 from 5 to 8 p.m. to review the code and map version that goes to City Council. Residents can review the proposal and submit feedback at columbus.gov/zoningupdate."
why_it_matters: "Landlords and investors holding property inside the roughly 40% of Columbus covered by this phase should check the Zone In map before the Nov. 5 and Nov. 18 hearings, since a parcel's allowed density or use, and a neighbor's, could change under the new code before it reaches City Council."
