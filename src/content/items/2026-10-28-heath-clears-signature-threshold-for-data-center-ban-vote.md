title: "Heath clears signature threshold for data center ban vote"
summary: "Organizers in Heath collected 481 valid signatures, more than double the threshold needed to put a ban on data centers using 23 megawatts or more of power per month before voters in a special election, the Licking County Board of Elections confirmed. Organizer Margo Mayberry expects the special election in early 2027; Ohio law requires cities to hold the vote 60 to 120 days after council approves an enabling ordinance, which Heath's council may do in November. Heath joins Johnstown, Pataskala and Granville among Licking County communities moving to restrict data center development."
detail: "Organizers in Heath submitted a petition on Oct. 19 with nearly 570 signatures seeking a special election on banning large data centers that use 23 megawatts or more of power per month; the Licking County Board of Elections validated 481 of them, more than double the 220 needed. The threshold requires signatures equal to 10% of voters in Heath's November 2025 general election. Organizer Margo Mayberry expects the vote in early 2027. Ohio law requires a special election 60 to 120 days after a city council approves the enabling ordinance, something Heath's council could do at a November meeting; the city will have to pay for the election itself because it missed the Sept. 4 deadline to place the question on this November's ballot. Johnstown faced the same missed deadline and expense after the city initially rejected a combined petition, citing a state law barring changes to a petition once filed; Heath's organizers collected signatures again rather than risk the same rejection, also shifting the threshold from 25 megawatts to 23 to distinguish their petition. Pataskala and Granville voters decide similar data center bans by charter amendment on Nov. 3. Heath is working with Conserve Ohio, a group also pursuing a statewide constitutional amendment restricting 25-megawatt data centers."
why_it_matters: "A fifth Licking County community is on track to ban or restrict data centers locally, and each city has had to fund its own special election after missing the September ballot deadline, a cost and timeline that anyone marketing land for data center use in the county should factor in before signing."
