summary: "The Ohio Supreme Court has ruled that Mayfield Heights voters, not city council, will decide the fate of Sommers Development Group's $38 million Park Place Townhome Community. detail: "Mayfield Heights City Council spent 45 minutes hearing public comment at its Oct. 26 meeting on a zoning dispute the Ohio Supreme Court has already weighed in on. The fight centers on a 7.7-acre parcel on Parkland Boulevard that council rezoned from executive office use to a Planned Unit Development District on May 27, clearing the way for Sommers Development Group's proposed $38 million Park Place Townhome Community. Surrounding business owners, concerned the rezoning would undercut their office-park investments, gathered signatures for an initiative petition, now Issue 107, asking voters to revert the parcel to office use. The Ohio Supreme Court ruled that the referendum goes to voters rather than being decided administratively, and the city's own zoning code gave council 90 days from the petition's filing to act, a deadline that fell the day after the election. Council voted 6-1 on Oct. 26 to reject the petitioners' request outright rather than adopt it, with Michael Buffa the lone dissent, meaning Issue 107 proceeds to the Nov. 4 ballot as previously scheduled. Residents raised separate safety and process concerns, saying notice of the zoning change came too late for meaningful input. According to the News-Herald, the city has posted information on Issue 107 to its website."
