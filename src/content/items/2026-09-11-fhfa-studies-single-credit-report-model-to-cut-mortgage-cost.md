detail: "FHFA Director Bill Pulte told reporters in September that the agency is studying the use of a single credit report in mortgage underwriting, according to Reuters, but did not clarify whether he meant a report from one credit bureau or a portable report borrowers could reuse across lenders. Loans sold to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac currently rely on merged, or tri-merge, reports drawing on Equifax, Experian and TransUnion, since the three bureaus do not always report identical information on a borrower. Two competing proposals could match Pulte's description. The Mortgage Bankers Association favors a single bureau model, where lenders pull one bureau's report first and only order a full tri-merge if that score falls short. The Broker Action Coalition instead backs a portable report: one verified credit report with a reference number a borrower could share among multiple lenders while shopping for a mortgage. The coalition estimates borrowers currently pay about $375 for an average of 2.5 credit pulls, versus roughly $150 for a single portable report, though those figures are the group's own and have not been independently verified. Pulte's push follows separate public criticism of FICO and the three credit bureaus over pricing. FHFA has not released a formal proposal or said which model it is pursuing, and no credit report requirement has changed for lenders so far."
