title: "Hilliard Drops Appeal of Amazon Data Center Fuel Cell Permit"
summary: "The City of Hilliard has withdrawn its administrative appeal of an Ohio EPA permit for a 72.9-megawatt natural gas fuel cell facility at an Amazon Web Services data center campus near Scioto Darby Road and I-270. Hilliard City Council President Emily Cole said the withdrawal is not an endorsement of the project, noting that state law gives local communities limited authority over such facilities. The city had asked for studies on air quality impacts and plans to conduct its own air quality testing once the fuel cells become operational."
detail: "The City of Hilliard withdrew its 2025 administrative appeal, filed with the state's Environmental Review Appeals Commission, challenging an Ohio EPA permit for a 72.9-megawatt solid oxide fuel cell facility planned at an Amazon Web Services data center campus near Scioto Darby Road and Interstate 270. Council President Emily Cole said the decision does not amount to an endorsement of the project, and that the city pursued the appeal despite expecting a difficult case because state law gives local governments limited authority over facility siting decisions like this one.\n\nResidents had raised health concerns about emissions from the fuel cells. The city had requested studies during the appeal process; those studies indicated projected air quality impacts would remain below established health and safety thresholds. Hilliard says it now plans to conduct its own air quality testing once the fuel cell facility becomes operational, rather than continuing the legal challenge.\n\nThe Amazon data center campus off Scioto Darby Road is one of several large facilities that have expanded along the Interstate 270 corridor in western Franklin County in recent years, and the outcome illustrates how little legal leverage a suburb retains once a data center project clears state-level permitting."
