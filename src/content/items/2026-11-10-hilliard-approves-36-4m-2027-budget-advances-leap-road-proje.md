summary: "Hilliard City Council voted 7-0 to approve a $36.4 million capital improvement budget for 2027, including $900,000 to continue design and begin right-of-way acquisition for the Leap Road Corridor Improvements Project south of Cemetery Road. The road reconstruction, the city's most expensive nonroutine capital project through 2029, will add a shared-use path, replace aging water lines and a culvert, rebuild drainage and add two mini-roundabouts, with most of the roughly $20 million in spending coming in 2029, according to ThisWeek."
detail: "Hilliard City Council approved the city's $36.4 million capital improvement budget for 2027 on a 7-0 vote Nov. 9, continuing a years-long effort to reconstruct Leap Road south of Cemetery Road. The 2027 budget allocates $900,000 to keep designing the project and begin acquiring right-of-way, City Engineer Letty Schamp told council at an Oct. 26 meeting. The project is the city's most expensive nonroutine capital item in its current five-year plan, with roughly $18.6 million, about 43% of that year's capital budget, planned for 2029.\n\nThe work includes a shared-use path, a rebuilt street, new drainage, a replaced culvert and sanitary sewer work, new curbs and two mini-roundabouts; the water line work alone is budgeted at about $2.5 million, Schamp said. Hilliard expects $6.7 million in reimbursement through the Central Ohio Transit Authority's LinkUS Transit Supportive Infrastructure program, plus further funding from the Ohio Public Works Commission. The project began in 2022 as part of the city's Pedestrian/Bicycle Mobility and Safety Program, addressing a stretch of road with chronic flooding, sidewalk gaps and drainage problems, according to ThisWeek."
why_it_matters: "Right-of-way acquisition is starting now along Leap Road south of Cemetery Road, so an owner with rental property fronting that stretch should expect contact from the city about easements or partial takings before the roughly $18.6 million main construction phase in 2029, and the LinkUS transit funding tied to the project signals the corridor is positioned for more density over time."
