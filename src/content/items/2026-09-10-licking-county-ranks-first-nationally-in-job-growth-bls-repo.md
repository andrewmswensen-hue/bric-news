title: "Licking County ranks first nationally in job growth, BLS report finds"
summary: "A Bureau of Labor Statistics report found Licking County had the largest year-over-year employment increase, 3.6%, of the nation's 376 largest counties between March 2025 and March 2026, adding about 2,700 jobs to reach roughly 77,000 total. Average weekly wages rose 5.7% to $1,287. The gains concentrated in trade, transportation and utilities. National employment grew just 0.1% over the same period, and Ohio's fell 0.2%. The county ranked second in the state for wage growth, behind Delaware County's 10.6% increase."
detail: "The BLS report measured employment change from March 2025 to March 2026 across the nation's 376 largest counties and found Licking County's 3.6% growth rate led all of them, well above the 0.1% national increase and in contrast to Ohio's overall 0.2% decline over the same period. County officials attributed the growth to new and expanding employers, including Kansai Helios in Johnstown, Westerman Inc. in Hebron, Prudential's new facility in Heath, Axium Packaging's expansion in Jersey Township, and Covestro's expansion in Hebron, alongside continued construction hiring at Intel's delayed New Albany chip plant and growth at Illuminate USA in Pataskala. Average weekly wages in the county rose 5.7% to $1,287, and the county ranked second among Ohio's 15 largest counties for wage growth, trailing only Delaware County's 10.6% increase. Grow Licking County Executive Director Cameron Garczyk and County Commissioner Tim Bubb both credited sustained public and private investment for the ranking. The county continues to field interest from data center developers pitching projects across the region."
why_it_matters: "Job growth at 3.6%, more than 30 times the national rate, combined with a 5.7% wage increase, is the kind of employment and income growth that tends to firm up rent demand in Licking County, which is worth weighing against how much of that growth is construction-phase hiring at Intel that will not necessarily repeat next year."
