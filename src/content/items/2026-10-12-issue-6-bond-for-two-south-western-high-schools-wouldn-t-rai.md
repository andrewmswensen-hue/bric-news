detail: "South-Western City Schools is asking Franklin County voters to approve a 2.26-mill bond issue on the Nov. 3 ballot to replace Grove City High School and Westland High School, both more than 50 years old, and to fund targeted improvements at Central Crossing and Franklin Heights high schools, according to WSYX. The district calls it a no-new-millage bond: because an existing bond is scheduled to be paid off at the end of 2026, the district can reissue bonds at the same collection rate residents have paid since 2009 rather than raise it. That local share would unlock $113 million from the state through the Ohio Facilities Construction Commission, which found that repairing Grove City High School would cost 82% of full replacement and repairing Westland High School would cost 83%, the agency's threshold for recommending replacement over renovation. Outdated plumbing, HVAC, electrical and sewer systems are cited as factors. The bond would not increase property tax rates, but if voters reject it, the district says taxes would instead decrease by about $79 a year per $100,000 of county-auditor appraised value, since the expiring bond would not be replaced. If approved, construction could begin in 2028 with the new buildings opening for the 2031-2032 school year."
