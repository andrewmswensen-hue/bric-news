title: "ROAD Act housing law faces a multi-year funding and staffing gauntlet"
summary: "A session hosted by the Bipartisan Policy Center and the National Association of Affordable Housing Lenders this week outlined why the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, the first comprehensive federal housing law in decades, will take years to implement. The law created roughly a dozen programs that still need congressional funding, and HUD, which owns about 70% of the law's 125 required federal actions, must complete nine new rulemakings while operating with 34% fewer staff than in 2024."
detail: "Speakers at the Bipartisan Policy Center and NAAHL session said many ROAD Act provisions could sit unfunded well into fiscal year 2028. The House's fiscal 2027 transportation and housing appropriations bill, passed before ROAD became law, does not fund the act's roughly dozen new programs, and the Senate has not yet released its own version. \"Until those programs get funded, they really only exist on paper,\" said Aaron Shroyer, NAAHL's director of policy and advocacy. Separately, HUD must write rules and guidance for the law's regulatory changes, most notably nine new rulemakings, a process that typically takes 18 months to two years per rule. HUD's own staffing has dropped from about 8,800 employees in fiscal 2024 to about 5,800 now, a 34% decline that could push some rulemakings past 2028. One change already advancing is the removal of the permanent chassis requirement from HUD's manufactured housing definition, which would let manufacturers build taller, higher-density manufactured housing suited to infill and urban lots rather than only single-story homes on a steel frame."
why_it_matters: "Most of the ROAD Act's voucher and housing-supply provisions will not reach Columbus landlords for at least a year or two given the funding and staffing gap, but investors interested in manufactured or infill housing should track the chassis-requirement rule, since it is one of the few pieces of the law not waiting on a congressional appropriation."
