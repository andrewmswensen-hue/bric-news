title: "FTC settlement would fine a tenant screening company $2.25 million over report accuracy"
summary: "The Federal Trade Commission announced a proposed settlement on July 9 that would resolve a complaint against a tenant screening company that sells background reports to landlords and property managers. The complaint, filed in federal court in Washington, alleges Fair Credit Reporting Act and FTC Act violations, including letting a single criminal conviction or eviction case appear as several separate entries, which made applicant histories look longer than they were. The proposed order carries a $2.25 million civil penalty and ten years of compliance monitoring."
detail: "The FTC alleges the company is a reseller under the Fair Credit Reporting Act because it builds tenant screening reports from data supplied by other consumer reporting agencies, and that it did not follow reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy. The central allegation is duplicate reporting: the same conviction or eviction proceeding surfacing as multiple entries on one report, so an applicant's record appeared more extensive than the underlying data supported. The complaint also alleges the company did not disclose all sources in a consumer's file on request, including a third-party database used to match public records to applicants by name and address history, and did not forward certain disputes back to the agencies that supplied the disputed data. Separately, the FTC alleges that when a consumer successfully disputed information but the overall screening result did not change, the company told the consumer that landlords had been notified even though the notice sent to landlords mentioned no change. Under the proposed stipulated order the company would pay a $2.25 million civil penalty within seven days of entry, be permanently barred from those practices, file a compliance report within one year, and keep compliance records for ten years subject to FTC and Justice Department monitoring."
why_it_matters: "Duplicate entries are the specific defect the FTC went after, and a Columbus landlord reading a screening report is the person who acts on them: one eviction case shown three times reads as a pattern and can drive a denial the underlying record does not support. The order also puts the vendor on a ten-year compliance clock, which is a reasonable thing to ask about when a screening provider renews."
