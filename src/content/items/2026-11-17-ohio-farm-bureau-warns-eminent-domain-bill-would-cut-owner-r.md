summary: "The Ohio Farm Bureau says a proposed change to the state's eminent domain law would let utility companies and data center developers begin construction before a landowner's challenge is heard in court. detail: "The Ohio Farm Bureau is raising concerns about proposed legislation that would expand eminent domain authority for utility and data center projects. Leah Curtis, the Farm Bureau's associate general counsel, told Brownfield Ag News that under current Ohio law, a property owner facing eminent domain can challenge in court whether the taking is actually necessary before the case moves to a compensation hearing, giving owners a chance at judicial review before losing their land. The proposed change would let the government file for a taking, make a deposit, and take possession of the property almost immediately, removing that pre-possession review. Curtis said eliminating judicial review removes a backstop that currently gives landowners leverage to negotiate, since developers can already complete many projects without using eminent domain when there is an incentive to negotiate in good faith. The Farm Bureau has launched a grassroots campaign asking farmers and other landowners to contact their state representatives and senators to oppose the bill. The story centers on farmland, but the eminent domain procedure it describes applies to any Ohio property owner, including owners of rental property, facing a utility or data center taking."
why_it_matters: "If this change passes, a Columbus-area investor whose rental property sits in the path of a utility line or data center project could lose the chance to contest whether the taking is even necessary before the state takes possession, leaving only a later fight over compensation."
