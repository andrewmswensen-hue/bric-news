title: "What state-by-state late rent data suggests about fixing your own collections"
summary: "BiggerPockets interviewed RentRedi co-founder Ed Barone, whose platform serves close to 200,000 landlords and renters, on rent-payment patterns. Late-payment rates on the platform vary up to 4x by state, from around 5% in Utah and Hawaii to as high as 20% in states like Mississippi. Barone's recommended fix for landlords in higher-rate markets is systemic rather than tenant-by-tenant: default autopay at lease signing, reminders sent before the due date rather than after, and a late fee that is written into the lease and charged consistently every time it applies, not selectively."
detail: "The interview draws on payment data from RentRedi, a platform Barone says close to 200,000 landlords and renters use, to argue that a landlord's late-payment rate partly reflects the state a rental sits in rather than tenant quality alone; rates run as low as roughly 5% in Utah and Hawaii and as high as 20% in states like Mississippi, a 4x spread. Barone's practical fix does not depend on identifying which state a landlord operates in: set autopay as the default at lease signing rather than an opt-in, send payment reminders before the due date rather than after, and put a specific late fee in the written lease that is charged the same way every month it applies. The article argues small landlords most often lose money on that last point, charging a late fee inconsistently rather than every time it is owed, which weakens both the deterrent and the landlord's position if a dispute arises. The piece also flags that late-fee rules vary by state statute, using Texas's requirement that a fee be specified in the lease as an example, and recommends any landlord re-read their own state's late-fee statute rather than assume an old lease clause still complies."
why_it_matters: "Ohio law does not cap a residential late fee by statute the way some states do, but a Columbus landlord whose lease charges the fee only when convenient rather than every time it is owed is undercutting the fee's own enforceability if a tenant later disputes it in small claims or a set-off against the deposit."
