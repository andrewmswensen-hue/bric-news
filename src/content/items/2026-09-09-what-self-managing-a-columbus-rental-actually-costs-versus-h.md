title: "What self-managing a Columbus rental actually costs versus hiring a manager"
summary: "RL Property Management breaks down the real cost gap between self-managing a Columbus rental and hiring a manager. Flat-rate professional management on a $1,400 rental runs $1,529 to $2,333 a year depending on service tier. Self-managing looks cheaper on paper but carries hidden costs: each vacant day on that unit runs about $46, so a saved week of vacancy is worth roughly $322. Columbus's new rental registry, opening Oct. 1 at $15 per unit with no exemptions, adds another compliance task, and a Franklin County eviction filing now costs $149 as of Aug. 1, 2026."
detail: "The analysis compares three RL Property Management flat-fee tiers against the cost of self-managing a $1,400-a-month Columbus rental. Passive management runs $117 a month ($1,529 a year including a $125 accounting fee), Standard is $137 a month ($1,769 a year), and Premium is $184 a month ($2,333 a year); leasing is free on all three. Self-managing avoids those fees but shifts the cost into time, vacancy days, and retail-priced repairs rather than contractor rates. A well-priced Columbus single-family rental typically leases in 14 to 21 days once rent-ready, and each additional vacant day on a $1,400 unit costs about $46 in lost rent. The piece also flags two new compliance costs that apply to every Columbus rental regardless of management choice: the city's rental registration program running Oct. 1 through Dec. 31, 2026 at $15 per unit annually with no exemption for single-family homes or phase-in by portfolio size, and a Franklin County eviction filing fee of $149 effective Aug. 1, 2026, which runs about $448 all-in through a management company. Six weeks of lost rent from a bad turnover stretch outweighs any single fee line on either side of the comparison, which is the point the piece keeps returning to: flat-fee pricing tends to win in a year a unit actually turns over, while percentage-of-rent pricing tends to win during a long, quiet tenancy with no turnover at all, so the better structure depends on a landlord's own historical turnover rate rather than a fixed rule of thumb. Portfolios of five to nine units qualify for a 10% per-unit discount under RL Property Management's pricing, and a one-time $300 onboarding fee applies when a unit comes on vacant, separate from ongoing management fees. The article frames the choice as a math problem tied to a landlord's actual turnover rate rather than a universal answer, with the new registry fee and the higher eviction filing cost applying to either side of that comparison equally."
why_it_matters: "Every Columbus landlord, self-managed or not, now owes $15 per unit for the new rental registry starting Oct. 1 with no size exemption, and Franklin County's eviction filing fee has already risen to $149 as of Aug. 1, both new fixed costs worth building into a 2026 budget regardless of who manages the property."
