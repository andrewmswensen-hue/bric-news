title: "OhioHealth Buys $22.5M Office Building Near Former Big Lots HQ"
summary: "OhioHealth purchased an 11-acre, roughly 141,000-square-foot Class A office property in the Hamilton Quarter development near Westerville, next to the former Big Lots corporate campus, for $22.5 million, according to Franklin County property records; the sale closed Aug. 16. A proposed Columbus ordinance would transfer the building's existing enterprise-zone tax agreement from seller HQ Office I LLC, a Daimler Group affiliate, to OhioHealth. The 2020 agreement grants a 75 percent property tax abatement on real property improvements for 10 years, potentially running through 2032."
detail: "OhioHealth's purchase of the roughly 141,000-square-foot Class A office building closed Aug. 16, according to Franklin County property records, adding to the health system's growing footprint in the Route 161 and Hamilton Road corridor in northeast Columbus. The 11-acre property sits at the Hamilton Quarter development beside the former Big Lots corporate campus at 4900 E. Dublin Granville Road. A Columbus city ordinance, read for the first time by City Council on Sept. 14 with a second reading expected soon, would authorize the city's Department of Development to transfer the building's existing enterprise-zone agreement from seller HQ Office I LLC, an affiliate of developer Daimler Group, to OhioHealth. That agreement, approved in 2020, grants a 75 percent property tax abatement on real property improvements for 10 years, potentially running through 2032, in exchange for a $19.2 million investment commitment and a pledge to create 15 full-time jobs with a combined annual payroll of about $750,000. An OhioHealth spokesperson said the health system has not yet determined how it will use the space and did not say whether the building's existing tenants would remain or whether OhioHealth would lease space to outside businesses."
why_it_matters: "The transfer keeps a 75 percent property tax abatement running through 2032 attached to the building rather than resetting it, which is the mechanism a Columbus investor evaluating a similar enterprise-zone deal should note: the tax break follows an ownership change as long as the new owner takes on the original job and investment commitments, not just the property."
