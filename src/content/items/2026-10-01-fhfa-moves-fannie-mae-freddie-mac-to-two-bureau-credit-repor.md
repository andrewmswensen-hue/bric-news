title: "FHFA moves Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac to two-bureau credit reports"
summary: "The Federal Housing Finance Agency is requiring Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to accept bi-merge credit reports, pulling data from two of the three major credit bureaus instead of all three, FHFA Director Bill Pulte said this week. The change follows Pulte's earlier move allowing the government-sponsored enterprises to treat VantageScore 4.0 scores as equivalent to Classic FICO scores on a single pricing grid, reversing a 20-point downward adjustment previously applied to VantageScore."
detail: "The Federal Housing Finance Agency plans to drop the long-standing requirement that mortgage lenders pull a tri-merge credit report, one combining data from Equifax, Experian and TransUnion, for loans sold to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. Under the new bi-merge policy, lenders could rely on only two of the three bureaus. FHFA Director Bill Pulte announced the direction on social media, saying the credit bureaus had been \"overcharging Americans for far too long.\" A bi-merge option was considered during the Biden administration under then-FHFA Director Sandra Thompson but was delayed over implementation concerns; the Mortgage Bankers Association has more recently pushed for a single-file option. The move follows a separate FHFA decision earlier in September letting Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac treat VantageScore 4.0 as equivalent to Classic FICO on one pricing grid, reversing a previous 20-point downward adjustment applied to VantageScore scores. Classic FICO remains available and is still required for manually underwritten loans. The FHA, a separate agency, plans to begin using the newer FICO 10T model for its own underwriting starting in January 2027. The bi-merge requirement is expected to take effect one to three months after FHFA formally announces it, according to Bloomberg reporting cited by HousingWire."
why_it_matters: "Fewer required credit pulls on a conventional purchase or refinance loan means a lower credit-report fee at closing, a small but real line item on every financed rental purchase."
