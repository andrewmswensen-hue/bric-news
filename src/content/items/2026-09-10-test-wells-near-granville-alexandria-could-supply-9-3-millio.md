title: "Test wells near Granville, Alexandria could supply 9.3 million gallons a day"
summary: "Test wells drilled near Alexandria and Granville as part of a state-funded search for water sources for Intel's New Albany chip plant found the sites could produce up to 9.3 million gallons a day. Neither city has an immediate need to tap the supply: Intel has pushed its production start to 2031, and Columbus has said it will separately provide the roughly 5 million gallons a day Intel will eventually need. Granville officials say any future withdrawal would need controls to protect the aquifer, the village's well field, and nearby residential wells."
detail: "The state of Ohio funded the well testing after promising to help secure water sources for Intel's delayed chip manufacturing campus in New Albany. Test and observation wells were drilled at two sites near Alexandria and one near Granville in the Raccoon Creek valley, and the results indicate the sites together could produce up to 9.3 million gallons of water a day. Granville Village Manager Herb Koehler said the relatively low-volume finding at the Granville Township site should be cautiously encouraging for local water customers, but said any future development of the source must include controls to protect the aquifer, the village's own well field, and nearby residential wells, and should not proceed without consulting Granville. New Albany, which funded the testing alongside Granville after an earlier dispute over New Albany's purchase of farmland and a 2024 test-well permit request, said it is reviewing the findings and has no immediate next steps to share. Intel has repeatedly delayed its production start, now targeted for 2031, and the City of Columbus has said it will provide Intel's roughly 5 million gallons a day in water needs once production begins, reducing pressure to tap the Raccoon Creek valley sources soon."
