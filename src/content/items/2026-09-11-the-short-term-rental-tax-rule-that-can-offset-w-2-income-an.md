title: "The short-term rental tax rule that can offset W-2 income, and its two tests"
summary: "A BiggerPockets explainer breaks down the federal tax mechanism known as the short-term rental loophole: a short-term rental can be treated as nonpassive under Internal Revenue Code section 469 if it clears an exception to the rental-activity definition and the owner materially participates in running it. Passing only the first test is not enough."
detail: "Under section 469, rental income is passive by default, so rental losses normally can't offset wages or other active income. The short-term rental exception, in Treasury Regulation section 1.469-1T(e)(3)(ii)(A), takes a property out of the passive-rental bucket if the average guest stay is seven days or less, calculated as total nights rented divided by total separate stays, not by calendar days. Clearing that test only exits one bucket; the owner still has to show material participation under a second, separate test, most commonly by working more hours on the property than anyone else involved, including cleaners and contractors. The article stresses that a cleaner's hours count against the owner's total, and that hours spent researching or arranging financing do not count as participation under the IRS's own audit guide. It cites Lucero v. Commissioner, a U.S. Tax Court case where claimed travel hours were rejected for an unreliable participation record, as a caution against relying on travel time. Once both tests are cleared, the deduction itself typically comes from cost segregation, which reclassifies components of a building into shorter depreciation schedules, combined with bonus depreciation, rather than from the exception itself. The article is explicit that the strategy is fact-dependent and that getting either test wrong can mean the loss simply doesn't count against outside income."
why_it_matters: "This is a federal mechanism that runs through IRC section 469 rather than Ohio law, so it applies the same way to a Columbus-area investor's short-term rental as anywhere else, but Columbus owners weighing a cost segregation study should understand both the seven-day stay test and the material-participation test are required before assuming the study alone creates a deductible loss."
