title: "Delaware County's growth strains roads, pushes a transit levy to the ballot"
summary: "Delaware County is now the 22nd fastest-growing county in the country, according to county leaders, with more than 4,000 homes under construction in the city of Delaware alone. Delaware County Transit is asking voters in November to approve a levy to expand service and routes as traffic grows. Commissioners have already voted to spend $17 million to buy 141 acres along Sawmill Parkway for future development, and ODOT is planning a multi-year corridor project along U.S. 23 to handle traffic the growth is expected to add."
detail: "County leaders say keeping up with Delaware County's growth is expensive: Commissioner Jeff Benton said building and widening roads and building sewer plants and lines is costly but necessary as new growth corridors take shape across the county. One example is Sawmill Parkway, where commissioners voted in June to spend $17 million to purchase 141 acres that Benton described as a top-five site per JobsOhio, citing its location near the airport and existing utilities. Benton said the county's road network cannot yet absorb the added traffic; he specifically flagged U.S. 23 as incapable of handling an additional five to ten thousand cars a day. The Ohio Department of Transportation is continuing work on a U.S. 23 Corridor Action Plan covering a 23.5-mile stretch between I-270 and Waldo, with projects tiered by priority and including plans to replace some traffic signals with overpasses, underpasses or connector-road interchanges. A separate, more contested proposal would build a new connector between U.S. 23 and U.S. 71. Delaware County Transit is asking voters this November to approve a levy to expand transit service and routes, arguing that road expansion alone will not keep pace with the county's growth."
why_it_matters: "Investors buying in Delaware County should note commissioners are already committing real money, $17 million just to acquire 141 acres along Sawmill Parkway, to capture growth, and a transit levy on the November ballot would add a funding source to keep pace with the 4,000-plus homes already under construction in the city of Delaware."
