title: "Hilliard Drops Its Appeal of Permit for Data Center Fuel Cell Plant"
summary: "The city of Hilliard has withdrawn its appeal of an Ohio EPA permit for a 72.9-megawatt natural gas fuel cell facility at the Amazon data center campus off Scioto Darby Road near I-270. detail: "Hilliard filed an administrative appeal with the state's Environmental Review Appeals Commission in 2025 challenging an Ohio EPA permit for a 72.9-megawatt solid oxide fuel cell facility, powered by natural gas, at the Amazon Web Services data center campus near Scioto Darby Road and Interstate 270. The city has now withdrawn that appeal. City Council President Emily Cole said the decision does not amount to an endorsement of the facility, explaining that state law gives local communities very limited authority over projects like this one, but that the city felt it owed residents an attempt to pursue every available option. Residents near the site had raised health concerns about emissions from the fuel cells. As part of its case, the city had requested two studies indicating that expected air quality impacts from fuel cell operations would remain below established health and safety thresholds. Hilliard says it plans to conduct its own air quality testing once the fuel cells become operational, rather than relying solely on the developer's projections."
why_it_matters: "The withdrawal confirms that Ohio law leaves Hilliard and similar suburbs with little formal leverage over data center power infrastructure once a state environmental permit is issued, a limit that will keep shaping how nearby residential and rental property is affected by future data center buildout in the corridor."
