title: "Columbus weighs tighter retention pond rules after two child deaths"
summary: "Columbus City Council held a hearing on emergency legislation to strengthen the city's retention pond safety ordinance after two toddlers drowned in apartment complex retention ponds this year, most recently at Polaris Place in September. The measure would build on the city's 2024 ordinance, which already requires multiunit complexes with ponds to post warning signs, install life preservers every 300 feet, and notify residents annually. The legislation is on council's agenda for November 2 and would take effect immediately if passed."
detail: "Columbus City Council examined proposed emergency legislation to expand the city's retention pond safety requirements after two recent child drownings, including 2-year-old Benny Mbombo, who went missing from the Polaris Place apartment complex in late September and was found in the complex's retention pond hours later, and 2-year-old Georgina Villar, who drowned in a retention pond at Bayside Commons Apartments in July. Council President Pro Tempore Rob Dorans said the hearing was prompted by those deaths. The new measure would build on a 2024 ordinance that already requires multiunit residential complexes with retention ponds to post at least four warning signs, install life preservers every 300 feet, and notify residents annually of the dangers. Ponds built after the 2024 ordinance must include a shallow safety shelf near the shoreline. That ordinance also lets the city's Department of Building and Zoning Services declare a retention pond a public nuisance if it has been the site of a drowning or lacks required safety features, and require the property owner to make safety modifications. The new legislation is scheduled for council's November 2 agenda and would take effect immediately if passed."
why_it_matters: "An owner of a multiunit complex with a retention pond should expect inspection and compliance costs for signage, life preservers, and resident notices, and should know the city can already declare a pond a public nuisance and order safety modifications after a drowning, a power this bill would strengthen."
