title: "Maryland AG settles with landlord over voucher-based screening rules"
summary: "Maryland Attorney General Anthony Brown announced a settlement with Disney Properties, a Salisbury landlord with about 100 Eastern Shore rental units, after the state's Civil Rights Division found it refused to rent to Housing Choice Voucher holders and required pay stubs that screened out applicants relying on vouchers, disability benefits or other non-employment income."
detail: "Maryland's Civil Rights Division investigated Disney Properties, LLC, which owns, operates or manages about 100 rental properties on the Eastern Shore, after complaints about its tenant screening. Investigators found the company refused applicants using Housing Choice Vouchers, the federal program commonly called Section 8, and required three to four current pay stubs on every application, a standard that excluded people relying on vouchers, disability benefits, retirement income or other non-employment income, according to the Attorney General's office.\n\nUnder the settlement, Disney Properties must accept vouchers and other lawful income at all of its properties and judge income requirements against only the portion of rent a tenant pays, not the full rent. It must drop pay-stub and physical Social Security card requirements where other documentation works, revise its advertising and lease paperwork, retrain staff on fair housing law, and waive application fees for up to 100 voucher-using applicants. The company will pay $100,000 in civil penalties and file compliance reports for two years. The case was resolved under Maryland's source-of-income protection law, the Housing Opportunities Made Equal Act, which the state strengthened again on October 1, 2026."
why_it_matters: "Ohio has no statewide ban on source-of-income screening, but the practices penalized here, requiring pay stubs and judging applicants against full rent rather than the voucher-covered share, are common defaults in many lease applications and are the exact mechanics regulators elsewhere are now targeting."
