title: "Ohio Supreme Court blocks Wilmington data center zoning measure from ballot"
summary: "The Ohio Supreme Court ruled 6-1 that the Clinton County Board of Elections was right to keep a Wilmington data center zoning initiative off the November ballot, because a clause allowing residents to sue data centers for zoning violations exceeded the city's legal authority. The initiative, drafted by residents opposing a proposed $4 billion Amazon data center, would have added noise, chemical documentation, and decommissioning rules to Wilmington's zoning code."
detail: "The ruling settles a dispute over a citizen-drafted petition that sought to add data center regulations, including noise limits, wastewater chemical documentation, generator use logs, and decommissioning rules, to Wilmington's zoning code. A section of the proposed ordinance would have let residents sue data centers directly for zoning violations, and six of seven justices found the city lacked legal authority to create that private right of action, so the Clinton County Board of Elections was within its rights to exclude the measure from the ballot.\n\nOrganizer Quintin Koger Kidd, who helped draft the initiative with an AICP-certified planner, said the disputed section had not been flagged during legal review and that the initiative included language to sever any part found unlawful, a point the majority opinion did not address. In dissent, Justice Patrick Fischer wrote that boards of elections lack authority to make legal determinations about ballot language and that the question should have gone to a court instead. The ruling means Wilmington voters will not see the zoning measure this November; organizers said they will revise it and restart signature gathering. Four separate referendums to reverse a related rezoning from residential to light industrial remain on the November ballot."
why_it_matters: "The ruling sets a real limit on what Ohio citizen zoning initiatives can contain: a local ballot measure that tries to create a private right to sue over zoning, rather than just changing the zoning rule itself, can be kept off the ballot by the board of elections without a court ruling first, which matters to any Central Ohio group organizing a zoning petition against a data center or any other project."
