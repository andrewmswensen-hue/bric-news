title: "HUD Narrowed Assistance Animal Rules; Ohio's Own Rule Did Not"
summary: "A HUD enforcement memo issued May 22, 2026 narrowed federal fair housing enforcement to assistance animals individually trained for a task tied to a disability, dropping emotional support animals from that standard, according to RL Property Management. The memo changed how HUD enforces the Fair Housing Act, not the underlying statute, and did not touch Ohio's own civil rights rule. Ohio Administrative Code 4112-5-07 still protects any animal that aids a person with a disability, without requiring training, and still bars an extra fee for one."
detail: "HUD's shift arrived in three steps. On September 17, 2025, the agency withdrew eight fair housing guidance documents, including the 2020 framework providers had used to evaluate assistance animal requests. A Federal Register notice formalized that withdrawal on April 6, 2026. Then on May 22, 2026, HUD's Assistant Secretary for Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity issued an enforcement memo stating that HUD will find reasonable cause for denying an accommodation only where the animal has been individually trained to perform a task tied to the resident's disability, explicitly excluding animals that provide only emotional support, comfort or companionship. That memo changes how HUD's own enforcement staff evaluate complaints. It does not amend the Fair Housing Act itself, and a resident can still sue directly in court under the statute regardless of HUD's enforcement posture, according to RL Property Management. Ohio has a separate rule that was not affected. Ohio Administrative Code 4112-5-07 defines an assistance animal as any animal that aids a person with a disability, without using the word trained, and bars landlords from charging an extra fee for one. The Ohio Civil Rights Commission, a HUD-funded agency that investigates many of these complaints locally, had published no position as of September 2026 on how the federal change affects its own broader rule. That leaves a real gap for property owners: HUD dropped its ban on charging pet fees for emotional support animals, while Ohio's rule on its face still prohibits any extra charge for an assistance animal, trained or not, and no agency has resolved which standard controls a complaint filed in Ohio."
why_it_matters: "A Columbus landlord who denies every emotional support animal request based only on the new HUD memo could still lose a complaint filed with the Ohio Civil Rights Commission, since Ohio's own rule has not adopted HUD's training requirement."
