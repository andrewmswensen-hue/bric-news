title: "What Ohio Law Actually Requires for Smoke and CO Alarms"
summary: "Ohio rental smoke alarm rules come from the Ohio Fire Code, not the commonly cited ORC 3781.104, which only applies to buildings over 75 feet tall. Existing buildings must have smoke alarms, and carbon monoxide alarms are required when a unit meets any of four fire code triggers, including a shared fuel-burning furnace or an attached garage, with a compliance deadline that passed January 1, 2019. Columbus adds its own rules: hard-wired detectors in one- to three-family buildings built after April 26, 1978, with tenants responsible for day-to-day upkeep under city code."
detail: "Most landlord guides cite Ohio Revised Code 3781.104 for smoke alarm requirements, but that statute applies only to apartment buildings and condominiums over 75 feet tall. The rules that actually reach a typical Columbus-area duplex or small apartment building sit in the Ohio Fire Code (OAC 1301:7-7-11). Existing rental buildings must have working smoke alarms, and carbon monoxide alarms are required when a unit meets any of four separate triggers: a fuel-burning appliance or fireplace in the unit, a fuel-burning forced-air furnace serving the unit, fuel-burning equipment elsewhere in the building with a communicating opening, or an attached private garage. Existing buildings had until January 1, 2019 to add CO alarms where required, a deadline now seven years past. Ohio Revised Code 5321.04 turns these code requirements into a landlord duty to keep the property in a fit and habitable condition, and the Ohio Supreme Court's 2000 ruling in Sikora v. Wenzel held that violating that duty counts as negligence per se, meaning a code violation alone can establish liability unless the owner neither knew nor should have known about it. Fire code civil penalties run up to $1,000 per violation and up to $1,000 for each day an uncorrected violation continues. Columbus adds its own layer: city code requires hard-wired detectors in one- to three-family buildings built after April 26, 1978, with battery-only units allowed in older buildings. The city splits responsibility in writing: the owner installs detectors, and in one- to three-family dwellings, the occupant is responsible for day-to-day operation and maintenance, though the owner's underlying code compliance duty does not transfer to the tenant. Columbus is also opening initial rental registration under a new ordinance from October 1 through December 31, 2026, at $15 per unit, which adds a periodic exterior and common-area inspection that includes fire alarm panels."
