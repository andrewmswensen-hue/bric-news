summary: "The U.S. Treasury Department released rules Thursday implementing the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, which bars investors owning more than 350 houses from buying additional single-family homes, effective Jan. 7. Treasury said the compressed timeline between the guidance's release and the law's start date leaves little time for large investors to adjust, so it plans to phase in enforcement."
detail: "The Treasury Department released a 96-page rulemaking document on Thursday implementing the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, a law Congress passed this summer. The rule bars any investor that owns more than 350 single-family houses nationwide from buying additional ones, with the ban taking effect Jan. 7, according to Bisnow. Treasury said it intends to phase in the rule because the law's implementation date leaves little time for large investors to adjust their acquisition plans. President Trump had pushed since the start of the year to restrict institutional buyers in the single-family market, writing on social media in January that he wanted to stop corporations from buying up homes; he had earlier withheld his signature from the bill over an unrelated voter ID dispute before it became law this summer. The rule applies only to the largest investors and does not restrict smaller landlords or occasional investors buying individual properties. National Housing Conference President David Dworkin told Bloomberg that whether the rule helps or hurts affordability depends on whether the broader market keeps investing in new rental construction once the largest buyers are sidelined, or whether reduced institutional investment slows the building of needed rental housing. The rule arrived alongside a separate Trump executive order addressing mortgage and refinancing costs, issued the same day."
why_it_matters: "The 350-house threshold is far above what a Columbus investor with a handful of rentals owns, so this rule changes who you compete against at auction and on MLS listings, not whether you can keep buying."
