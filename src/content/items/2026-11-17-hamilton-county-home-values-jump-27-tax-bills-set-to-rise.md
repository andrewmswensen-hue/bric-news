detail: "Hamilton County Auditor Jessica Miranda said the county's latest triennial mass appraisal found home values rose an average of 27%, a state-mandated process that compares recent home sales to set new property values. The appraisal covers roughly 350,000 parcels countywide, spanning residential, commercial, industrial and agricultural property. The increase is smaller than the 34% average jump recorded in the prior full reappraisal.\n\nMiranda said the rise reflects continued strength in the real estate market, with properties still selling for high amounts. The higher valuations will likely add to 2026 property tax bills, payable in 2027, though actual tax rates will not be finalized until mid-December, when the auditor's office receives rates from the Ohio Department of Taxation. Once bills are calculated, the auditor's office certifies them and the county treasurer sends invoices to property owners.\n\nMiranda pointed to House Bill 186, passed in 2025, which created inflation cap credits limiting the tax rate for homeowners in school districts at the 20-mill floor to the rate of inflation rather than real estate market inflation; she said the measure does not fully offset the increase. Property owners can challenge their new valuation by filing a complaint with the county Board of Revision between January and March."
