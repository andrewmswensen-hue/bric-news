title: "Worthington Voters Will Decide Fate of 248-Unit Housing Project"
summary: "A referendum petition against a Worthington zoning change gathered enough valid signatures, 3,476 of 3,017 required, to put the rezoning on the November 2027 ballot, the city announced October 7. Worthington City Council approved the rezoning on July 20 for 445 East Dublin Granville Road, the site of a planned $52.4 million, 248-unit development from builder Elford Development. The land is owned by disability nonprofit I Am Boundless, which agreed to sell to Elford and expand its own workforce by 65 to 80 jobs as part of the deal. The project stays on hold until voters weigh in next November."
detail: "A citizen group called Democracy Before Developers collected 3,976 signatures, of which 3,476 were valid, clearing the 3,017 needed under Ohio law to force a referendum on a Worthington zoning change. Ohio law allows citizens to put most city council votes to a referendum if they gather signatures equal to at least 35% of the city's turnout in the last gubernatorial election.\n\nThe referendum targets Worthington City Council's July 20 vote to rezone 445 East Dublin Granville Road for a planned 248-unit, $52.4 million housing development from builder Elford Development, expected to break ground next fall. The city confirmed on October 7 that the petition had enough valid signatures, and said the project remains in question until voters decide the zoning question in the November 2027 general election.\n\nThe land is currently owned by I Am Boundless, a disability services nonprofit that agreed to sell the site to Elford Development. As part of the deal, I Am Boundless agreed to retain its existing jobs and add 65 to 80 more. Neither Democracy Before Developers nor I Am Boundless responded to requests for comment cited in the original report.\n\nThe city noted that close to 40% of Worthington voters who turned out in the last gubernatorial election signed the petition, a signal of how contested the rezoning is locally."
why_it_matters: "An investor eyeing land near this corridor should expect the zoning question, and anything that depends on it, to stay unsettled for over a year, since the 248 units cannot proceed until the November 2027 vote."
