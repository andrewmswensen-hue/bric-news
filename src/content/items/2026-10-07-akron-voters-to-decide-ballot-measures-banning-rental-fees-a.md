detail: "Akron residents will vote in November on two charter amendments that would reshape how landlords screen and charge prospective tenants, according to Cleveland 19 News. Issue 11 would ban landlords from charging application fees, background-check fees, and other upfront fees, and would require landlords to let tenants pay security deposits in installments over three to six months. Landlords who refuse to offer that installment option could face criminal charges under the measure.\n\nIssue 12 would prohibit landlords from factoring an applicant's criminal record into a rental decision, with exceptions for arson convictions and registered sex offenders. The Freedom Bloc, a tenant advocacy group led by executive director Rev. Raymond Greene, collected the signatures that put both charter changes on the ballot. Greene said the current fee structure and screening practices function as barriers and that criminal-history screening operates like a second form of discrimination against formerly incarcerated applicants.\n\nAkron Mayor Shammas Malik has come out against both measures, calling them too extreme and warning they could hurt tenants and landlords alike by discouraging investment in rental housing in the city. Because both issues would amend Akron's city charter directly, a yes vote in November would take effect without further council action."
why_it_matters: "If Akron voters approve Issue 11 or 12, it becomes the template Ohio tenant groups point to when pushing similar fee and screening restrictions in other cities, so Columbus-area landlords who rely on application fees or criminal-background screening should watch how the vote and any legal challenge play out."
