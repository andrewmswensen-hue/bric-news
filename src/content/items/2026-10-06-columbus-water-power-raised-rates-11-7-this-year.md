detail: "Columbus Water & Power, the roughly 1,450-person city department that delivers water and sewer service to 1.4 million customers and electricity to 19,600 customers across central Ohio, raised its combined water, stormwater and sewer rates 11.7% this year, according to a Columbus Monthly profile of director Kristen Atha. As a public utility, the agency says it sets rates based on cost of service rather than profit, and Atha noted Columbus bills remain lower than in nearby Delaware, Pickerington, Westerville, Pataskala and Marysville.\n\nThe agency is midway through replacing 48,000 lead or galvanized steel water lines to meet a federal deadline of 2037, a project it estimates will cost $100 million a year, or $1 billion total, with average per-line replacement costs up $1,000 to $7,500 in recent years. It is also building a fourth water treatment plant in Delaware County, the first new plant since 1980, at an estimated cost of $2.5 billion and a 2034 completion date, driven primarily by residential growth rather than data center demand, which the agency says accounts for about 3% of water consumption. Separately, the city is studying whether to convert the department into a regional authority, a move the union representing its workers opposes."
