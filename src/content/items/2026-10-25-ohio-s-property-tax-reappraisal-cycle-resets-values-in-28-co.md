title: "Ohio's property tax reappraisal cycle resets values in 28 counties"
summary: "Richland County's auditor released updated 2026 property values as part of Ohio's state-mandated reappraisal cycle, one of 28 counties undergoing the statistical midpoint update this year while 13 others, including Knox and Ashland, undergo a full inspection-based reappraisal, according to Richland Source. The new values apply to tax bills payable in 2027. Because the state adjusts neighborhood values based on recent sale prices rather than inspecting each parcel, owners who haven't sold or remodeled can still see higher valuations if comparable nearby properties sold for more."
detail: "Ohio law requires all 88 counties to update property values on a six-year cycle: a full reappraisal with exterior inspections every six years, and a statistical triennial update at the midpoint based on sales data rather than inspections, Richland County Auditor Pat Dropsey explained. For the triennial update, the Ohio Department of Taxation's tax commissioner calculates broad percentage adjustments by neighborhood and property type using recent sale prices, so a parcel whose owner hasn't sold or renovated can still be revalued upward if comparable homes on the same street sold for more. Richland is one of 28 Ohio counties undergoing the triennial update this year; 13 others, including Knox and Ashland counties, are undergoing the full sexennial reappraisal instead. The new values take effect on tax bills mailed in 2027. State officials noted a higher valuation does not automatically produce an equal tax increase, because Ohio's House Bill 920 requires voter-approved levy rates to adjust downward as overall property values rise. Owners who believe their new value is inaccurate can file a Board of Revision Complaint Form between Jan. 1 and March 31, 2027. Separately, the article noted Ohio's new House Bill 479 will deliver a one-time property tax credit, averaging $450 to $475, to roughly 710,000 homeowners already enrolled in the Homestead Exemption, a program limited to qualifying seniors and people with disabilities rather than rental property owners."
why_it_matters: "If your rental sits in one of this year's 28 triennial-update or 13 sexennial-reappraisal counties, the appeal window for a value you think is wrong runs Jan. 1 through March 31, 2027, not whenever you happen to open the tax bill."
