summary: "HUD's Oct. 7 action plan ends the requirement that manufactured homes sit on a permanent steel chassis, a rule the agency says has added up to $10,000 to the cost of each unit since the 1970s. HUD also plans a new federal construction code for factory-built residential buildings with five or more units, aimed at a national path for repeatable multifamily construction. detail: "The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development released a Next Generation Manufactured Housing Action Plan on Oct. 7, carrying out provisions of the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act that became law in July. The law eliminated a chassis mandate dating to the 1970s that required manufactured homes to be built on a permanent steel undercarriage, a requirement HUD says could add as much as $10,000 to the cost of a new unit. HUD's plan revises efficiency standards, rewrites technical rules for homes built without a chassis, and sets new installation requirements for them. The agency also plans to commission research on how off-chassis housing performs once installed. Separately, HUD said it wants to develop a federal safety and construction code built around performance standards for factory-built residential buildings with five or more units, which could create a national path for repeatable multifamily construction using modular or factory-built components. FHA acting assistant secretary Joe Gormley said the change opens the door to rethinking how manufactured homes are designed, shipped, assembled and regulated, with the aim of expanding supply for first-time buyers. The code changes are expected to roll out over time as HUD finalizes the new technical standards."
