title: "Grove City, Sunbury Add New Limits on Data Center Development"
summary: "Grove City voters in Franklin County approved a requirement that any data center project larger than 50 acres, drawing 20 megawatts or more of power, or using 500,000 gallons of water a day or more go to a public vote before it can proceed, according to Data Center Dynamics. Sunbury voters in Delaware County banned data centers that draw more than 25 megawatts outright. The two votes were among at least 14 Ohio data center restrictions passed in the Nov. 3 election, adding to earlier charter bans in the Licking County communities of Pataskala and Granville."
detail: "Grove City and Sunbury became the latest Central Ohio communities to restrict data center development in the Nov. 3 election, Data Center Dynamics reported, as Ohio recorded at least 14 passed bans or other restrictions nationwide, more than any other state. In Grove City, in Franklin County, voters approved a measure requiring any data center project larger than 50 acres, drawing 20 megawatts or more of power, or consuming 500,000 gallons of water or more a day to go before voters directly rather than being approved through the normal zoning process. In Sunbury, in Delaware County, voters banned outright any data center drawing more than 25 megawatts of power. Both votes follow earlier charter amendments in the Licking County communities of Pataskala and Granville banning data centers within their limits, part of a broader pattern of Ohio towns using ballot measures rather than council votes to restrict large power- and water-intensive projects. The outlet noted its tally could still change as more local races are certified."
why_it_matters: "Grove City and Sunbury sit inside BRIC's core coverage area, and a data center buyer willing to pay well above normal land prices has been a real option for owners of large parcels in both; Grove City's new public-vote trigger at 50 acres, 20 megawatts, or 500,000 gallons a day, and Sunbury's outright 25-megawatt ban, both narrow that exit for sellers and change what a developer can promise a landowner without first winning a townwide vote."
