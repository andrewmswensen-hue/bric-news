title: "FHFA removes VantageScore penalty from unified mortgage pricing grid"
summary: "The Federal Housing Finance Agency released a unified loan-level price adjustment grid for Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac that drops a 20-point downward adjustment previously applied to VantageScore 4.0 scores relative to Classic FICO. Lenders can still choose either model but must use the same one for every borrower on a loan. Rocket Mortgage said Monday it will make VantageScore 4.0 its default model for eligible loans, and United Wholesale Mortgage said about 25 percent of its borrowers currently score better under VantageScore."
detail: "Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac lenders have been able to choose either Classic FICO or VantageScore 4.0 for eligible conventional loans since early September, but VantageScore scores were subject to a 20-point downward adjustment meant to account for differences between the two models. The pricing grid FHFA Director Bill Pulte released removes that adjustment, so a VantageScore now carries the same weight as a FICO score of the same number under the shared grid. Classic FICO remains available and is still required for manually underwritten loans. FICO 10T, a newer model, has not yet been approved for delivery to the GSEs, though the FHA plans to begin using it for underwriting in January 2027.\n\nHistorical data shows VantageScore 4.0 can produce higher scores than Classic FICO for the same borrower, which is why the adjustment mattered. Rocket Mortgage said it tested both models on 1.4 million credit reports this year and will now use VantageScore 4.0 as its default for eligible loans, saving some borrowers an average of $1,600 at closing. United Wholesale Mortgage said about 25 percent of its borrowers currently see a better result under VantageScore, a share it expects to reach 40 percent by the end of September. FICO's stock fell more than 26 percent Tuesday afternoon, which HousingWire and Yahoo Finance attributed to the pricing change."
why_it_matters: "If you finance rental purchases or refinances through a conventional Fannie or Freddie loan, the credit-score model your lender defaults to can now change your rate quote on its own, since VantageScore no longer carries a built-in penalty: it is worth asking which model your lender pulls before you lock a rate."
