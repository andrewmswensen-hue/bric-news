title: "German Village Commission approves 52-unit Cedar Square after year of revisions"
summary: "The German Village Commission voted 3-2 on Sept. 2 to approve Cedar Square, a mixed-use development at 245-277 E. Livingston Ave., after more than a year of revisions and resident opposition. The plan calls for 52 units in a three-story building, down from an original 96-unit, four-story proposal from developer Mershad Development. A petition opposing the design collected more than 800 signatures. The approved plan includes small retail along Livingston Avenue and demolishes a two-story brick building on the site."
detail: "Mershad Development first brought Cedar Square before the German Village Commission in February 2025 with a 96-unit, four-story concept. After repeated commissioner and resident feedback, the developer cut the project to 52 units in a three-story building, then made further changes ahead of the September vote, including more brick on the north and south facades, less siding, added landscaping along Blenkner Street, and a redesigned rooftop garden made less visible from the street. Public comment ran roughly 45 minutes at the Sept. 2 meeting; residents argued the project's scale and more than 300 feet of continuous building along Blenkner Street remain incompatible with the historic district's codified guidelines. A petition opposing the design gathered more than 800 signatures, with organizers saying they supported redevelopment but wanted the third story removed and the Blenkner Street building reduced further. One resident, Michael Gallagher, warned the approval could set a scale precedent for future German Village projects seeking similar height variances. The commission's 3-2 vote completes Cedar Square's design and demolition review, clearing the project to move forward."
why_it_matters: "The commission's approval of 52 units at three stories, after rejecting an earlier 96-unit, four-story version, sets a working ceiling on the height and unit count German Village will approve for a comparable-sized lot, a useful reference point for any investor eyeing infill development in the district."
