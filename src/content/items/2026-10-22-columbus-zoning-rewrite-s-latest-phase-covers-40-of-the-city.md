title: "Columbus zoning rewrite's latest phase covers 40% of the city"
summary: "Columbus is in the latest phase of Zone In, its first full zoning code rewrite in more than 70 years, with more than 40% of the city included in proposed map changes. The city says the update is meant to open up more housing and business development as Columbus grows, while some residents worry nearby parcels could be rezoned for taller apartment buildings. Public comment on the proposed map runs through October 24, with city council hearings scheduled for November 5 and November 18."
detail: "The City of Columbus is rewriting its zoning code for the first time in more than 70 years through its multi-year Zone In initiative, and the latest phase covers more than 40% of the city's land area. Luis Teba, the project manager for Zone In, said the rewrite is meant to support more housing and business opportunities as the city continues to grow. Some residents have raised concerns about specific parcels: Forest Park resident Joseph Higginbotham said a wooded area behind his 35-year home could become a residential or commercial development under the proposal, potentially allowing apartment buildings up to four stories near his property line. The city is taking public comment on the proposed zoning code and an online map through the end of the day on October 24 at columbus.gov/zoningupdate, and says staff are revising the map based on comments received so far. Two public hearings follow: one on November 5 from 5 to 7 p.m. and another on November 18 from 5 to 8 p.m., both intended to gather feedback before a final proposal goes to city council."
why_it_matters: "An investor who owns or is evaluating a property inside the 40% of Columbus covered by this phase should check the proposed zoning map before the October 24 comment deadline, since a parcel's allowed density or use could change under the rewrite and affect what can be built or added there."
