title: "Franklin County study: tax abatements take decades to pay back"
summary: "Franklin County's 2026 Tax Incentive Review Council report found that Community Reinvestment Area property tax abatements can take decades for local governments and schools to recoup in foregone revenue, even as they boost property values. Economists Mark Partridge and Nick Messinger studied 2,140 residential properties whose CRA abatements expired between 2014 and 2025 and found the median property generated just $162 more in tax revenue after expiration."
detail: "The report, compiled for Franklin County's Tax Incentive Review Council, examined 2,140 residential properties whose Community Reinvestment Area (CRA) tax abatements expired between 2014 and 2025. Researchers found that while property tax revenue generally rose after an abatement ended, the increase was often small and slow: the average property generated about $1,515 more a year, but the median property gained just $162, and the researchers said it could take years or even decades for a taxing authority to recoup what it gave up during the abatement period.\n\nThe study also found that neighborhoods with higher levels of CRA investment saw larger increases in household income and educational attainment over time, along with declines in the share of non-white residents, patterns the economists did not say the abatements caused but flagged as a tradeoff worth tracking. On the sale side, homes with abatements that had recently expired sold for about 12.3% more than comparable, non-abated homes in the year the incentive expired, but researchers found that price premium disappeared within three years."
why_it_matters: "An investor weighing a purchase inside a Franklin County Community Reinvestment Area should know the resale premium on a recently expired abatement fades within about three years, and county officials now have data suggesting these incentives may face closer scrutiny or tighter terms going forward."
