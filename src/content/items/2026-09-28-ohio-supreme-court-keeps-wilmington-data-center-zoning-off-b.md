title: "Ohio Supreme Court keeps Wilmington data center zoning off ballot"
summary: "The Ohio Supreme Court ruled Sept. 18 that the Clinton County Board of Elections correctly kept a Wilmington citizen initiative on data center zoning off the November ballot. Six of seven justices found the proposed ordinance, which would have let residents sue data center operators directly over zoning violations, exceeded the city's legal authority to enact by initiative. The measure grew out of opposition to a proposed $4 billion Amazon data center in Wilmington. The board of elections had rejected the petition before the case reached the court."
detail: "The case began when a group of Wilmington residents, reacting to a proposed $4 billion Amazon data center, drafted a citizen initiative to add stricter data center regulations to the city's zoning code. The draft ordinance included a provision letting residents sue data center operators directly over zoning violations. The Clinton County Board of Elections rejected the petition, finding the city lacked legal authority to create that private right of action, and the organizers appealed to the Ohio Supreme Court. In a Sept. 18 majority opinion, six of the seven justices agreed the board acted properly in keeping the measure off the November ballot, writing that the city did not have the legal grounds to enact that particular rule through a citizen initiative. Board of Elections Director Andrea Tacoronte said the ruling let the board focus on preparing for the November election. Wilmington resident Quintin Koger Kidd, who helped draft the initiative, said the fight was about whether the city's existing zoning code sufficiently protects the property rights of residents who already live near proposed data center sites."
why_it_matters: "The ruling marks a limit on how far an Ohio city's citizen-led zoning initiative can go to restrict a neighboring project like a data center, a question several Columbus-area suburbs are weighing as they draft their own data center zoning rules."
