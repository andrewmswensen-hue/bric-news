title: "Blacklick and Pataskala Are One Corridor, Two Different Markets"
summary: "Properties sharing a \"Blacklick, OH\" mailing address can sit in Columbus, Reynoldsburg, Gahanna or unincorporated Jefferson Township, spread across five different school districts, while Pataskala is an incorporated Licking County city with its own two districts. The distinction matters starting this October: Blacklick addresses inside Columbus city limits must register under the city's new rental registry, while township and Pataskala addresses do not."
detail: "Blacklick is not a city. It is a ZIP code, 43004, covering an unincorporated part of Jefferson Township, Franklin County, along with parcels inside Columbus, Reynoldsburg and Gahanna, spread across five school districts: Columbus City, Gahanna-Jefferson City, Licking Heights Local, New Albany-Plain Local and Reynoldsburg City. Pataskala, by contrast, is an incorporated Licking County city, enlarged by a 1996 merger with Lima Township, with two school districts inside its limits: Licking Heights Local and Southwest Licking.\n\nThat jurisdictional split determines more than school assignment. Blacklick addresses that fall inside Columbus city limits must register under the city's new rental property registry, Chapter 4515, starting October 1, 2026, at $15 per unit. Township and Pataskala addresses do not. Investors evaluating a parcel need to pull the actual parcel record, through the Franklin County Auditor for Blacklick, Reynoldsburg, Gahanna or Columbus addresses, or the Licking County Auditor's OnTrac system for Pataskala, Etna or Jersey Township, rather than relying on the mailing address or a listing site.\n\nThe two markets also differ in housing stock and returns. About 72 percent of housing in the 43004 ZIP was built in 2000 or later, compared with a median construction year of 1998 in Pataskala. Three-bedroom rents in the corridor ran roughly $1,945 to $2,445 as of late August 2026, per Zillow and Zumper. RL Property Management's 2026 market data places Blacklick's older, lower-basis pockets in a 7 to 10 percent cap rate band alongside Whitehall and Eastmoor, distinct from the newer subdivisions nearby.\n\nBoth markets sit near the New Albany employment corridor, including Intel's New Albany semiconductor site, though that project's targeted 2031 completion has slowed in pace. Buyers underwriting either submarket on the strength of that corridor story alone, rather than on current rents and vacancy, risk overpaying for a timeline that keeps moving."
