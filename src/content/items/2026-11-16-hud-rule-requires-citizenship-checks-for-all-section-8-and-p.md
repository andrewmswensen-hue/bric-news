detail: "The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development published a final rule in the Federal Register on Nov. 12 tightening citizenship and immigration verification for HUD-assisted housing, covering Public Housing, Section 8 rental assistance, Housing Development Grant programs, and Section 236 rental assistance. The rule takes effect Dec. 14.\n\nUnder current practice, HUD can provide prorated assistance indefinitely to mixed-status households, where some members qualify and others do not. The new rule ends that indefinite arrangement: assistance to a household with an ineligible member becomes temporary, continuing only until every resident's status is verified. Anyone claiming U.S. citizenship must now provide documentation.\n\nHUD said an audit with the Department of Homeland Security found nearly 200,000 HUD-assisted tenants with incomplete or unknown eligibility verification, and HUD Secretary Scott Turner said the rule closes a loophole that let ineligible noncitizens access housing assistance. About 2.3 million households receive Section 8 assistance nationally, through roughly 2,000 local housing authorities, with multi-year waitlists already common.\n\nThe National Housing Law Project and the Center on Budget and Policy Priorities oppose the rule, estimating it could affect about 80,000 people, including 37,000 U.S. citizen children in mixed-status households, who could lose assistance or be separated from family members, according to the Santa Clarita Valley Signal."
why_it_matters: "Columbus-area landlords who accept Section 8 vouchers will see local housing authorities request updated eligibility documentation from every household member before Dec. 14, which can delay a tenant's assistance payment if paperwork isn't complete."
