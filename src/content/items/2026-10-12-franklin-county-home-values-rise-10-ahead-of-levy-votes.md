detail: "Franklin County Auditor Michael Stinziano said the county's triennial property value review is wrapping up with home values up about 10% countywide this year, down from a 41% jump in 2023, a sign the market has stabilized compared to recent years. Homeowners pay real estate tax on 35% of a property's appraised value, with that figure multiplied by the tax rate of whatever levies voters have approved. Stinziano said nine bonds or levies are on the Nov. 3 countywide ballot, and the outcome of those votes, not the valuation change itself, will determine how much tax bills actually move. In Upper Arlington, residents will vote on a renewal and increase for the police and fire pension fund along with a school district bond and levy for operations and facilities. Upper Arlington Schools Superintendent Robert Hunt said that combination could raise property taxes by about $1,250 a year on a home appraised at $500,000, and that the district is returning to voters because rising home values alone do not increase the money schools receive."
why_it_matters: "Franklin County landlords should check which of the nine county levies and bonds on the Nov. 3 ballot touch their specific municipality or school district, since that vote, not this year's milder 10% valuation bump, will decide how much their tax bill actually rises."
