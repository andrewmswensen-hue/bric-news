title: "HUD proposes work requirements, time limits for federal housing aid"
summary: "HUD unveiled a proposed rule Friday that would let local public housing authorities impose work requirements and time limits on able-bodied adults receiving federal housing assistance, including housing choice vouchers. The rule would allow up to 40 hours of required work a week and time limits of two years or more, with exemptions for seniors, children and people with disabilities. HUD Secretary Scott Turner announced the proposal with the Housing Authority of Champaign County, Illinois, the first agency to join HUD's new work-requirements coalition."
detail: "HUD's proposed rule would give the nation's roughly 3,300 public housing authorities the option to require able-bodied adults on housing assistance to work up to 40 hours a week and to cap their time in subsidized housing at two years or more. Seniors, children and people with disabilities would be exempt. HUD Secretary Scott Turner said the goal is to reduce long-term dependency rather than remove people from housing, citing federal data that nearly half of non-elderly, able-bodied assisted households did not have a working adult in 2024, and that average stays in major rental assistance programs have grown to eight or nine years. HUD estimates the changes could raise participants' income by as much as $51 million nationally.\n\nThe Housing Authority of Champaign County and the Vermilion Housing Authority, both in Illinois, are the first agencies to join HUD's new coalition promoting work-focused housing policy. The rule is a proposal, not a final regulation. It must go through a federal public comment period, and any local public housing authority that wanted to adopt work requirements or time limits would need that final rule in place first."
why_it_matters: "Any Columbus landlord who rents to Section 8 voucher holders should watch this rule: if it is finalized, some local public housing authorities could add work requirements and time limits for voucher tenants, which would affect how long a tenant stays eligible and how stable that rent payment is."
