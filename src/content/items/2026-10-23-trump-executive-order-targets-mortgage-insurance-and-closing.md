title: "Trump executive order targets mortgage insurance and closing costs"
summary: "President Trump signed an executive order Thursday directing federal agencies to lower homeownership costs ahead of the midterm elections. The order tells the Federal Housing Finance Agency to weigh a pilot letting borrowers who have built enough equity get mortgage insurance premiums canceled automatically, and to consider expanding a program letting borrowers use cheaper alternatives to lender's title insurance when refinancing."
detail: "The executive order, signed Thursday, directs several federal agencies to look at reducing the cost of buying, financing and refinancing a home, the White House said. It instructs the Federal Housing Finance Agency director to consider a pilot program that would proactively cancel mortgage insurance premiums for Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac borrowers once they have built enough equity relative to their home's current value, according to WACH. The FHFA is also directed to consider expanding a pilot that lets lenders sell certain low-risk refinancing loans without requiring a lender's title insurance policy, which would lower refinancing closing costs. The Department of Veterans Affairs and the Department of Agriculture are told to look at lowering interest rates on the single-family loan programs they run. The order also creates a multiagency task force, involving the FHFA and HUD Secretary Scott Turner, to write guidelines meant to speed approval of manufactured housing and raise its quality. The order sets no timeline, and parts of it, including the mortgage insurance pilot, are conditioned on FHFA Director Bill Pulte determining the move serves the public interest, so some provisions may not take effect. The order was signed the same day Treasury released a separate rule limiting large investors in the single-family market."
why_it_matters: "The mortgage insurance and title insurance pilots this order creates apply to Fannie and Freddie loans, which generally cover owner-occupied purchases rather than rental financing, so a Columbus investor should watch whether FHFA extends either pilot to investment loans before assuming it changes refinancing costs."
