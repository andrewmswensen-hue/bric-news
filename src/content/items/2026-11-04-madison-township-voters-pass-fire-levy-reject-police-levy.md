summary: "Madison Township voters approved an additional levy to fund the fire department while rejecting a separate additional levy for the police department, according to final unofficial Franklin County results. The fire levy, for 4.89 mills, passed with about 56% support and will cost property owners $171 per $100,000 of county auditor market value. The police levy, for 1.99 mills and projected to raise more than $2.78 million, failed with about 52% opposed. detail: "Madison Township voters split their verdict on two public safety levies in the Nov. 3 election, according to final unofficial results from the Franklin County Board of Elections. The fire department's additional, continuing levy of 4.89 mills passed with about 56% support, adding $171 per year for every $100,000 of a property's county auditor market value. Fire Chief Derek Robinson said before the election that the levy was needed to sustain operations as call volume has grown 4% to 5% annually since the department's last levy in 2015. The police department's additional levy, 1.99 mills, projected to raise more than $2.78 million and cost property owners $70 per $100,000 of market value, failed with about 52% opposed. Police Chief Victor Boyd said the department is four officers short of its target staffing of 18 full-time officers and is struggling to compete on pay with the Columbus Division of Police, the Franklin County Sheriff's Office and other suburban departments. The median home value in the township is about $271,000. The Franklin County Board of Elections will certify the results within 21 days."
