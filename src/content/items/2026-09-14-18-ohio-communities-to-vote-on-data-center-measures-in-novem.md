summary: "Voters in 18 Ohio municipalities and townships will decide data center-related ballot measures on Nov. 3, after petitioners met a Sept. 4 filing deadline. Nine of the petitions, including ones in Granville, Pataskala and Sunbury, mirror a statewide push by the group Conserve Ohio to ban data centers that draw more than 25 megawatts of power a month. Organizers say the measures follow local fights over large power-hungry projects; Ohio has the fourth-most operational data centers of any state, according to Pew Research Center."
detail: "Petitioners in 18 Ohio municipalities and townships filed charter amendment petitions in time for the November ballot, ahead of the Sept. 4 deadline. Nine of those petitions, in communities including Granville, Pataskala and Sunbury, closely track a statewide effort organized by Conserve Ohio to ban data centers that draw more than 25 megawatts of energy a month; the statewide version missed its own signature deadline for 2026 and is aiming for the 2027 ballot instead. In Piqua, organizer Alisha Lange said her opposition began in 2023 after a nearby battery plant caused health complaints, and escalated after Meta announced a data center project there that would require around 1,400 megawatts of power; she has since filed petitions covering recall elections and the city's power agreement with the project. Ohio has the fourth-most operational data centers of any state, according to an April analysis by the Pew Research Center, as developers continue pursuing sites across the state, including several in central Ohio's Licking and Delaware counties."
