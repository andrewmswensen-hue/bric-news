summary: "Toledo City Council held a first reading of an ordinance creating a new \"Retaliation Prohibited\" chapter in the city code to protect tenants who raise health and safety concerns. detail: "Toledo City Council gave a first reading to an ordinance that would add a \"Retaliation Prohibited\" chapter to the city's municipal code, aimed at tenants who fear losing their housing for reporting problems like mold or water damage. The proposal would shift the burden of proof: rather than a tenant proving a landlord retaliated, the property owner would have to show good cause for an eviction or a rent increase. A violation would become a second-degree misdemeanor under the ordinance. Ohio law already prohibits landlord retaliation, but George Thomas, CEO and general counsel of the Toledo-based Fair Housing Center, said the state standard is vague and puts the burden on tenants to prove it. The center surveyed nearly 200 clients on their level of concern about retaliation, from one to five; more than half rated their concern a five, the highest level. Advocates say the local ordinance would not resolve every case but would make it easier for tenants without legal representation to raise a retaliation defense in an eviction proceeding. The proposal follows organizing around conditions at the Saxon House apartments, where a tenant union has pushed for repairs. The ordinance would apply only within Toledo; it has not yet had a second reading or a council vote."
why_it_matters: "If Toledo adopts the ordinance, landlords there would need to document a non-retaliatory reason before raising rent or pursuing eviction after a tenant reports a habitability issue, a burden-shifting approach other Ohio cities, including Columbus, could look to if they pursue similar protections."
