title: "Analysis puts Ramaswamy property tax rollback at $4.6 billion statewide"
summary: "The Dayton Daily News reviewed Ohio Department of Taxation collection data to estimate what Republican gubernatorial candidate Vivek Ramaswamy's property tax rollback would cost local governments and school districts. Returning net collections to tax year 2021 levels would remove roughly $4.6 billion statewide in the first year and about $609.8 million across six southwest Ohio counties. Statewide property tax collections rose from $20.6 billion in 2021 to $25.2 billion in 2025, with residential and agricultural property accounting for $14 billion and $17.4 billion of those totals."
detail: "Ramaswamy first said he wanted to eliminate Ohio property taxes and now says he would return them to at least where they stood before the end of the pandemic and cap growth from there. According to the Dayton Daily News, his campaign has not answered repeated requests for the plan's parameters, so the outlet modeled a rollback to tax year 2021 using state net collection figures. Statewide that removes about $4.6 billion of first-year revenue from local governments and school districts. Across six southwest Ohio counties the combined figure is about $609.8 million, ranging from $46.1 million in Clark County to $163.9 million in Montgomery County. Warren County Auditor Matt Nolan, a past president of the County Auditors Association of Ohio, told the paper the number could be smaller if the proposal targets only post-pandemic valuation increases rather than new growth and voter-approved levies, and he questioned using a past year as the baseline. Butler County Chief Appraiser Mike Gildea said the calculation would create administrative challenges because auditors value property under current law. Ramaswamy has said growth from his tax cuts would offset the loss. A governor cannot cut the tax alone. Lawmakers passed roughly $3.8 billion in property tax changes late last year, including House Bill 186, which limits increases at the 20-mill floor to inflation."
why_it_matters: "A rollback to a 2021 baseline would run through county auditor valuations and the 20-mill floor rather than the tax line on a bill, so the open question for Franklin County owners is whether the hundreds of levies passed since 2021 are carved out. Warren County's auditor says that is unresolved, and it separates a modest adjustment from a material change to the tax assumption in an Ohio rental pro forma."
