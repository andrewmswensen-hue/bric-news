title: "Columbus council weighs data center rules on water, sewer and decommissioning"
summary: "Columbus City Council held a public hearing in July on proposed data center regulations covering water usage, sewer discharge and decommissioning requirements, and on whether Columbus Water and Power could set a special rate class for data centers. Councilmember Christopher Wyche said the city is not pursuing a moratorium because no hyperscale projects are currently in the pipeline, which gives the city time to build a regulatory framework first. More than a dozen residents spoke, many calling for a ban and stronger penalties. No industry representative spoke at the hearing."
detail: "Councilmember Christopher Wyche told reporters before the hearing that the city is not proposing a temporary or permanent ban on new data centers. Wyche and Councilmember Emmanuel Remy heard from more than a dozen residents, many of whom said the city should go further. According to WOSU, the regulations drafted by Wyche's office target water usage, sewer discharge and the rules for decommissioning a facility that shuts down. The city is also weighing how revenue collected from data centers could fund community projects, and whether Columbus Water and Power can set special rates for a data center customer class. Wyche said he is not pushing a moratorium because no hyperscale project is currently seeking to locate in the city, which lets the city act on the front end, and that a moratorium would not resolve the water and wastewater transparency questions residents raised. Residents asked for rules addressing electricity usage and grid impact, air quality monitoring, protection of the Big Darby Creek watershed, and renewable energy. Several said proposed fees and fines were too lax. Spencer Dirrig of the Ohio Environmental Council proposed fines that scale with company size with a floor and no ceiling, and permit loss for sustained noncompliance. Central Ohio is the state's largest hyperscale data center hub."
why_it_matters: "A special water rate class and decommissioning rules would apply inside Columbus city limits, which is where a lot of infill and industrial land sits, and Wyche's stated reason for acting now is that no hyperscale project is pending. For owners holding land near industrial corridors, the framework the city writes in this window sets what a future data center buyer can and cannot do on that ground."
