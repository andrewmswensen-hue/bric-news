title: "Delaware clears land for a roughly $50 million downtown hotel project"
summary: "The city of Delaware is planning to transfer nearly an acre of downtown land, now used mostly for surface parking, to developer Westwood Collective for a roughly 100-room hotel near Ohio Wesleyan University. It would be the first hotel in the core of walkable downtown Delaware. City council placed the transfer ordinance on first reading September 14; the deal includes deadlines requiring a building permit within two years and substantial completion within five, or the city can reacquire the property."
detail: "The proposed Hotel Wesleyan, planned for Spring Street between South Franklin and South Sandusky streets, would include about 100 rooms and 280 parking spaces and could be branded either Marriott Tribute or Hilton Tapestry Collection, according to documents submitted to the city. The city issued a request for proposals for the site and selected Westwood Collective as developer; because of how state law structures municipal property transfers, the land will first convey to the Community Improvement Corporation of Delaware County, which will handle selling it to the developer, likely below market rate, according to the city's economic development director, Nic Langford. The agreement sets deadlines: the developer must obtain a building permit within two years and substantially complete the project within five, or the city can reacquire the property. Westwood Collective founder Andy Warnock, a Delaware native and Ohio Wesleyan graduate, said in bid documents that the project is meant to serve as a template for further infill redevelopment in the city's core, with total project cost potentially approaching $50 million. Two retail tenants, Playa Bowls and a student-run coffee shop called Bishop's Brew, have already expressed interest in ground-floor space."
why_it_matters: "The city's structure, transferring land through the Community Improvement Corporation of Delaware County at a below-market price with built-in completion deadlines, is a template investors interested in downtown Delaware infill sites can expect the city to reuse for future parcels, not just this one project."
