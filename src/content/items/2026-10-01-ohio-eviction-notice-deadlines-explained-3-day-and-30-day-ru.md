title: "Ohio eviction notice deadlines explained: 3-day and 30-day rules"
summary: "An Ohio eviction notice does not set a removal date: it starts the waiting period a landlord must observe before filing in court, according to a legal explainer citing Ohio Revised Code sections 1923.04, 5321.11 and 5321.17. A three-day notice generally precedes a filing for nonpayment of rent, while a 30-day notice applies to lease violations or month-to-month terminations. More than 100,000 eviction cases were filed in Ohio in 2023, the highest total since 2015, according to WOUB. A landlord cannot remove a tenant without a court judgment and a sheriff-executed writ."
detail: "Ohio law separates the notice a landlord delivers from the court process that actually recovers possession of a rental unit. Under Ohio Revised Code Section 1923.04, a landlord pursuing a forcible entry and detainer action, commonly for unpaid rent under ORC 1923.02, must first deliver a notice to leave that displays the statute's required warning, then wait at least three qualifying days, excluding weekends and legal holidays, before filing in court. A separate 30-day notice applies under ORC 5321.11 to certain lease violations, where a tenant who corrects a remediable violation within a reasonable time generally keeps the tenancy, and under ORC 5321.17 to either party ending a month-to-month tenancy without alleging any breach. Acceptable delivery methods under ORC 1923.04 include certified mail with return receipt, personal delivery, or leaving a copy at the premises. Once the notice period expires, the landlord files a complaint under ORC 1923.05, and a judge decides who has the right to possession at a hearing. ORC 5321.15 bars landlords from shutting off utilities or otherwise excluding a tenant to force them out without court process; only a sheriff or bailiff enforcing a court-issued writ may carry out a physical removal. Ohio sets no statewide rent grace period, so lease terms and payment history govern how a three-day notice gets triggered."
why_it_matters: "A three-day notice only starts the clock to file in court, not a deadline to physically remove a tenant, and skipping the court process to force someone out, by changing locks or shutting off utilities, exposes a landlord to liability under ORC 5321.15."
