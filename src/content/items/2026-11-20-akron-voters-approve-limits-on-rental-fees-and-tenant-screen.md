title: "Akron voters approve limits on rental fees and tenant screening"
summary: "Akron voters approved two charter amendments on the November ballot, backed by the tenant organizing group Freedom BLOC after two to three years of organizing. Issue 11 passed with about 58% of the vote and bans rental application fees while requiring landlords to let new tenants pay security deposits in installments rather than a lump sum. Issue 12 passed with nearly 51% and bars landlords from refusing to rent to someone based on past criminal history, with exceptions for sex-offense and arson convictions."
detail: "Freedom BLOC (Black Led Organizing Collaborative), which also supports a half-dozen building-based tenant unions in Akron, spent two to three years organizing before qualifying Issue 11 and Issue 12 for the ballot through petition signatures. Rev. Raymond Greene Jr., the group's executive director, said the work centered on educating tenants about who controls decisions over their living standards. Organizer and attorney Imokhai Okolo said residents repeatedly raised rent caps, but state law preempts local rent control, so the campaign focused on barriers to accessing rental housing instead: application fees, lump-sum deposits, and criminal-history screening. Issue 11 requires landlords to offer an installment option for security deposits instead of requiring it all upfront. Issue 12 prohibits refusing an applicant over a past conviction, except for sex-offense or arson convictions. Both measures passed, with Issue 11 the wider margin at about 58% and Issue 12 narrower at nearly 51%, according to Shelterforce."
why_it_matters: "These fee and screening rules apply only inside Akron for now, but since Ohio law blocks local rent control, expect similar ballot or council pushes on application fees, deposit installment plans, and criminal-history screening limits to surface in other Ohio cities, Columbus included, using the same legal workaround Akron organizers just proved out."
