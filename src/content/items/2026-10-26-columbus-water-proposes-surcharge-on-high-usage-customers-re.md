title: "Columbus Water Proposes Surcharge on High Usage Customers, Residents Get Small Break"
summary: "Columbus Water and Power submitted a proposed rate structure on Oct. 26 that would add a $1.62 per hundred cubic feet surcharge for roughly 426 commercial customers whose water use spikes well above normal, generating an estimated $6 million a year. The city says the change would make standard residential and small business water rates about 1.6% lower than they would otherwise be, though that would only partly offset Columbus's planned 18% water rate increase set for 2027. A public hearing is scheduled for Nov. 12, ahead of City Council votes in November."
detail: "Columbus Water and Power submitted a proposed high demand water rate structure to City Council on Oct. 26, aimed at commercial customers, including data centers, whose water use regularly spikes well above typical levels. Deputy Director Jon Lee said the surcharge is meant to recover the added system capacity needed to serve customers who create unusually high peak demand on the city's treatment, pumping, storage and distribution system. Under the proposal, commercial customers using at least 100 hundred cubic feet, about 74,800 gallons, a month and hitting a peak demand factor of twice their typical usage would face a $1.62 per hundred cubic feet surcharge. About 426 customers would qualify, generating an estimated $6 million a year.\n\nCity officials say the goal is to keep high demand customers from shifting costs onto average households, and the proposal would make standard water rates about 1.6% lower than they would otherwise be. That offset is small next to Columbus's planned 18% water rate increase in 2027, and the city has not said residents will see any savings from this change specifically. The department also wants new customers to help pay for upcoming projects like the Home Road water treatment plant rather than spreading that cost across existing ratepayers. A public hearing is set for Nov. 12, with City Council votes expected Nov. 16 and Nov. 23."
