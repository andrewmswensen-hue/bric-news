title: "Hamilton County home values jump 27%, tax bills to follow in 2027"
summary: "Hamilton County's state-mandated triennial reappraisal found home values rose 27 percent on average, Auditor Jessica Miranda said, a smaller increase than the prior reappraisal's 34 percent. The higher values will likely raise property tax bills for the 2026 tax year, payable in 2027, once the state finalizes tax rates in mid-December. A 2025 law, House Bill 186, caps the tax-rate increase from inflation for homeowners in districts at the 20-mill floor, though Miranda said it does not fully offset the impact."
detail: "Hamilton County Auditor Jessica Miranda's office completed its state-mandated triennial mass appraisal of roughly 350,000 parcels, finding home values rose 27 percent on average countywide. Miranda said strong home sale prices drove the increase, which is smaller than the 34 percent average jump recorded in the prior full reappraisal. The new values will likely raise property tax bills for the 2026 tax year, payable in 2027, but the exact amount will not be known until the Ohio Department of Taxation finalizes tax rates in mid-December; the auditor's office then calculates bills, which are certified and sent to the county treasurer for mailing. Miranda pointed to House Bill 186, passed in 2025, which created inflation cap credits limiting the tax rate increase to the rate of inflation, rather than the real estate market's faster growth, for homeowners in school districts already at the 20-mill floor; she said the relief does not go far enough. Property owners who want to challenge their new valuation can file a complaint with the county Board of Revision between January and March. Homeowners are expected to know their new tax bills by early next year at the latest."
why_it_matters: "Franklin County runs on the same state-mandated triennial reappraisal cycle as Hamilton County, so a Columbus-area investor should expect a comparable jump in assessed value and tax bills on the local schedule, and should plan to use the January-to-March Board of Revision window if a valuation looks out of line with recent comparable sales."
