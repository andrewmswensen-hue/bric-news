Under IRS Notice 2026-11, 100% bonus depreciation applies to appliances, carpet and fencing placed in service after January 19, 2025, though the rental building itself does not qualify. detail: "RL Property Management's year-end guide splits a rental owner's remaining 2026 tax moves into two groups: those that must happen by December 31 and elections made later when filing. On the calendar-year side, 100% bonus depreciation under IRS Notice 2026-11 applies to property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025, but only to shorter-life assets such as appliances and carpet (5-year property) and fences (15-year property); the rental building itself is 27.5-year property and the building's structural systems, including a furnace, follow the same 27.5-year schedule. A unit that is rent-ready and listed by December 31 is treated as placed in service in 2026 even if a tenant does not move in until January, since the IRS standard is ready and available for rent, not occupied.\n\nThe guide also walks through three IRS safe harbors landlords can use before year-end: the de minimis election, which lets owners deduct items costing up to $2,500 per invoice rather than depreciate them; a routine maintenance safe harbor; and a small-taxpayer safe harbor for buildings with an unadjusted basis under $1 million, capping deductible repairs at the lesser of 2% of basis or $10,000 for the year. Separately, the Form 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC reporting threshold for payments made directly by an owner to a contractor rose from $600 to $2,000 starting with 2026 payments, so the guide recommends collecting W-9s now from anyone paid directly. According to the guide, Ohio does not fully follow the federal bonus depreciation rule: under Ohio Revised Code 5747.01, the state adds back five-sixths of the federal deduction in the year it is taken, then returns one-fifth of that add-back in each of the following five years."
why_it_matters: "A Columbus owner who gets a vacant unit rent-ready and listed by December 31 can start depreciating eligible appliances and flooring for 2026 even if the new tenant doesn't move in until January, and the 1099 threshold moving to $2,000 means some direct payments to handymen this year may not require a form that last year's did."
