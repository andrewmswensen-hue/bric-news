title: "FHFA opens VantageScore to all lenders, eyes fewer credit pulls"
summary: "The Federal Housing Finance Agency directed Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to let every approved lender use VantageScore 4.0 for conventional mortgages, ending a rollout limited to 50 companies. Classic FICO remains an option. FHFA Director Bill Pulte said the agency is also studying whether to drop the requirement that lenders pull credit reports from all three bureaus, a change that could target the rising cost of a tri-merge report. An industry group says the average credit-report expense on a conventional mortgage has risen from about $50 in 2022 to roughly $540 in 2026."
detail: "FHFA had allowed only 50 lenders to choose between Classic FICO and VantageScore 4.0 when selling loans to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. Pulte's directive opens that choice to every approved lender immediately, though individual lenders still need to complete GSE and vendor requirements before delivering VantageScore-evaluated loans. VantageScore says its model was the sole credit score used on more than 9% of GSE-securitized mortgages between May and August. Separately, Pulte said FHFA is considering moving from a tri-merge credit report, which pulls data from Equifax, Experian and TransUnion, to a bi-merge approach using only one bureau. The Community Home Lenders of America, which pushed for wider lender choice, said the change should pressure credit costs down. Reducing the number of required reports could cut mortgage origination expenses by more than switching credit-score models alone, since bureau, reseller and platform fees stack on top of the score price. Classic FICO remains fully approved for GSE loans."
why_it_matters: "A Columbus investor buying or refinancing a rental with a Fannie or Freddie loan can now ask any lender, not just the original 50, to run VantageScore instead of Classic FICO, and a future move to a single-bureau credit pull could cut a few hundred dollars from closing costs."
