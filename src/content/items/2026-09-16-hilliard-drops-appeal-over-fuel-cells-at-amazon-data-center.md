title: "Hilliard drops appeal over fuel cells at Amazon data center"
summary: "The city of Hilliard withdrew its administrative appeal of an Ohio EPA permit for a 72.9-megawatt natural-gas fuel cell facility at an Amazon Web Services data center near Interstate 270, City Council President Emily Cole said. Cole said the withdrawal is not an endorsement, noting state law gives local communities limited authority over such facilities. Hilliard had filed the appeal with the Environmental Review Appeals Commission in 2025 after residents raised health concerns about fuel cell emissions, and had sought additional air quality studies."
detail: "Hilliard filed its administrative appeal with the state's Environmental Review Appeals Commission in 2025, seeking two additional studies on air quality impacts after residents near the Amazon Web Services data center campus off Scioto Darby Road raised health concerns about emissions from the proposed 72.9-megawatt solid oxide fuel cell facility. City Council President Emily Cole said the city is withdrawing that appeal.\n\nCole said the decision reflects the limits of local authority rather than a change in the city's position. State law gives municipalities little say over permitting for facilities like fuel cells once the state EPA has issued a permit, and Cole said the city concluded further appeal was unlikely to succeed even though it believed pursuing available options was the right call for residents. The studies Hilliard requested had indicated air quality impacts were expected to stay below established health and safety thresholds.\n\nWith the appeal withdrawn, the fuel cell facility can proceed toward operation. The city said it plans to conduct its own air quality testing once the fuel cells are running, rather than relying solely on the developer's pre-operation studies."
why_it_matters: "Hilliard's experience shows that once Ohio EPA issues an air permit for data center power infrastructure, a host city has little practical ability to block it through appeal, which matters for any investor weighing property near a planned data center power facility elsewhere in Franklin or Licking counties."
