title: "Ohio posted the nation's biggest affordability drop since 2020"
summary: "A National Housing Conference report found Ohio had the largest five-year loss of homeownership affordability in the country between 2020 and 2025, with home prices up 121% over that span while incomes lagged, according to The Center Square. The report, built on the NHC's Paycheck to Paycheck database and median income data across 390 metro areas, found the share of employed Ohioans who could afford a home with a 10% down payment fell nearly 41 percentage points."
detail: "The National Housing Conference compared its Paycheck to Paycheck database, which estimates the salary needed to buy or rent in 390 metropolitan areas, against median income data to track affordability from 2020 to 2025. Ohio's home prices rose 121% over that period while the share of employed residents who could afford a home with a standard 10% down payment fell by nearly 41 percentage points, the steepest such drop of any state, according to The Center Square. The report attributed the gap mainly to home prices and rents outpacing wages rather than to interest rates alone, noting nationwide living costs rose about 25% since 2020 while wages rose about 24.8%. For renters, the income needed statewide to afford a two-bedroom apartment climbed 39% over the five years, and Cincinnati's increase of 49% was the steepest among the Ohio metros the report cited. Julia Wright of the Cuyahoga Metropolitan Housing Authority said the shift is pushing stability further out of reach for households that previously could manage. The report's release comes as both major candidates for Ohio governor have made housing costs part of their campaigns."
