title: "Lawsuit Seeks to Block HUD's Dec. 14 Mixed-Status Housing Rule"
summary: "Three Los Angeles County residents and two tenant groups sued HUD and Secretary Scott Turner on Nov. 17 to block a rule, set to take effect Dec. 14, that ends indefinite prorated assistance for mixed-status households in HUD-assisted housing. The suit, filed in U.S. District Court for the Central District of California, asks a judge to delay the effective date while the case proceeds. HUD figures cited in the complaint show about 79,300 people, including roughly 36,000 citizen children, live in mixed-status households receiving HUD assistance."
detail: "Public Counsel, Kendall Brill & Kelly LLP and Bird Marella LLP filed suit Nov. 17 in the U.S. District Court for the Central District of California against HUD and Secretary Scott Turner. The plaintiffs are three Los Angeles County residents and two tenant organizations, People Organized for Westside Renewal and Union de Vecinos.\n\nThe rule, published in the Federal Register on Nov. 12, ends HUD's practice of letting mixed-status households receive indefinite prorated assistance based only on eligible members. It also removes a \"do not contend\" option that let ineligible members decline to claim status, and generally requires every household member to document citizenship or an eligible immigration status. Households that keep an ineligible member could lose assistance for at least two years, and housing authorities and some property owners must report residents found to be in the country unlawfully to the Department of Homeland Security.\n\nThe complaint names two plaintiff families, including a U.S. citizen with cerebral palsy who has lived in public housing since age 1, arguing they face separation or loss of housing if the rule takes effect Dec. 14. HUD estimated maintaining current assistance levels under the rule would cost $311 million to $385 million more a year, according to court filings cited by Newsweek."
why_it_matters: "A pending request to delay the rule means Columbus-area landlords with Section 8 or public-housing tenants from mixed-status families may not see the Dec. 14 documentation deadline move until the court rules on the injunction request."
