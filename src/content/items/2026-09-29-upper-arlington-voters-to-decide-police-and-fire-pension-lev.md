title: "Upper Arlington Voters to Decide Police and Fire Pension Levy"
summary: "Upper Arlington's Issue 34 on the November ballot would renew the city's expiring 0.89-mill police and fire pension levy and add 0.24 mills, for 1.13 mills total, raising about $2.2 million a year toward the city's state-mandated police and fire pension contribution. The owner of a $100,000 home would pay about $26 a year, $8.50 more than now, after Ohio's rollback provision on the renewal share. A $500,000 home would pay about $130 a year, $42 more. If voters reject the levy, the city says it would lose roughly $2.2 million in General Fund support."
detail: "Upper Arlington's Issue 34, on the Nov. 2 ballot, asks voters to renew the city's existing 0.89-mill police and fire pension levy, which expires at the end of tax year 2026, and add 0.24 mills, for a combined 1.13 mills. The levy would run five years starting tax year 2027, payable beginning in 2028, and is projected to raise about $2.2 million a year. Ohio law requires the city to fund 19.5 percent of police gross wages and 24 percent of fire gross wages into the state's police and fire pension fund; Upper Arlington has met that obligation through voted property tax levies for 50 years, via 10 five-year renewals. Because the renewal portion qualifies for Ohio's tax rollback, the city estimates homeowners will actually pay $23.71 per $100,000 of home value, or $118.55 for a $500,000 home, versus the full $26 and $130 figures before rollback. The police and fire divisions together account for about half the city's workforce and general budget. The pension levy is part of the roughly 8 percent of a resident's total property tax bill that goes to the city; most of an Upper Arlington property tax bill funds the schools, library, and Franklin County levies. If the levy fails, the city says it would need to cover the pension obligation out of General Fund operations, a loss of about $2.2 million."
why_it_matters: "An Upper Arlington landlord's property tax bill rises by about $8.50 per $100,000 of value if Issue 34 passes in November, a fixed cost increase separate from whatever the county auditor's broader reappraisal does to the underlying valuation."
