detail: "The National Association of Home Builders' monthly Housing Market Index weakened further in November. The index of current sales conditions fell six points to 28, sales expectations over the next six months slipped two points to 30, and the gauge of prospective buyer traffic dropped two points to 16, its lowest reading in 15 years outside the early pandemic months. NAHB Chairman Bill Owens, a home builder and remodeler from Worthington, Ohio, said stepped-up immigration enforcement is keeping legal workers off job sites and deepening labor shortages, while higher fuel prices are pushing subcontractors to raise rates. NAHB Chief Economist Robert Dietz pointed to tariff-driven material costs, a shortage of construction workers, and mortgage rates above 7 percent as additional pressures on supply and demand. Public homebuilders described similar conditions on recent earnings calls: Century Communities said incentives reached about 13 percent of sales price in the third quarter and are expected to rise further, while PulteGroup reported community traffic down 14 percent year over year. The median new-home sales price in September was about $400,000, down nearly 4 percent from a year earlier, as builders lean on rate buydowns and discounts to keep sales moving."
