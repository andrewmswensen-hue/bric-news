summary: "Columbus City Council held its third hearing this year on a policy package addressing data center growth, including an ordinance that would direct Columbus Water and Power to develop new rates or fees for high-load, high-volume water users such as data centers, according to WSYX. If it passes a second reading later this month, large-scale developments that require new water infrastructure would be paid for by the high-volume users themselves rather than residential and small business ratepayers, according to council leaders."
detail: "Columbus City Council President Shannon Hardin and Councilmember Christopher Wyche held a news conference and a Public Utilities and Sustainability Committee hearing on a proposed data center policy package, the third such hearing this year, according to WSYX. The centerpiece is an ordinance, which received a first reading at Monday's council meeting, directing Columbus Water and Power to develop a new set of rates, charges or fees specifically for high-load, high-volume water users, including data centers. The broader package is also expected to address efficiency requirements for high-volume users and electronic waste disposal. Council members described competing pressure from both sides of the debate: some residents want data centers banned outright, while industry representatives point to indirect job creation tied to data center construction. If the ordinance passes a second reading later this month, the stated goal is that large-scale developments requiring new water infrastructure would be funded by the high-volume users that create the demand, rather than being spread across residential and small business water rates."
why_it_matters: "If this ordinance passes, a Columbus landlord's own water rate should not move because of data center demand, since the point of the new fee structure is to keep that infrastructure cost off residential and small business bills."
