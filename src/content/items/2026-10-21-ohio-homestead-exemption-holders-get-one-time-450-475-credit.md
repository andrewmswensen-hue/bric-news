detail: "Ohio homeowners already enrolled in the state's homestead exemption program will receive a one-time additional property tax credit of about $450 to $475, under House Bill 479, which Gov. Mike DeWine signed into law in June, according to a Richland County Auditor announcement reported by the Mansfield News Journal. The homestead exemption primarily serves senior citizens and homeowners with qualifying disabilities who meet state age or disability and income requirements. Lawmakers added the one-time credit to House Bill 479 to offset rising property tax and housing costs for seniors and residents with disabilities, funding it through surplus state revenue rather than a new tax. Eligible homeowners do not need to file a new application: the credit will be applied automatically. It will show up as a second Homestead Exemption line item on tax year 2026 property tax bills, which county auditors mail in February 2027. The announcement specifies this is a one-time credit, not a permanent change to the Homestead Exemption's annual reduction, so the extra relief will not recur on future bills unless lawmakers act again. An estimated 710,000 Ohio homeowners qualify."
