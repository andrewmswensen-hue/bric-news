detail: "The Public Utilities Commission of Ohio approved the acquisition of AES Corporation, parent company of Dayton-area utility AES Ohio, by a consortium led by BlackRock and Swedish private equity firm EQT. The approval moves the deal closer to completion, though it still requires sign-off from the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission, where the Independent Market Monitor for the PJM grid has raised questions about whether BlackRock's existing utility holdings are fully reflected in the companies' market-power analysis. AES Ohio is separately seeking a $143 million rate increase from PUCO; discussions between BlackRock's predecessor fund and AES executives on the acquisition began more than a year before that rate request was filed. Publicly traded utilities have generated roughly 9 percent annual returns over the past decade, while BlackRock's infrastructure fund backing the deal targets gross annual returns up to 20 percent, prompting the Private Equity Stakeholder Project to ask where the gap between those returns and historical utility earnings would come from. In a similar case in Minnesota, an administrative law judge recommended rejecting BlackRock's acquisition of Minnesota Power's parent company, citing rate-shock risk. The AES consortium also includes Qatar's sovereign wealth fund, and EQT separately backs EdgeConneX, a data center developer active in Ohio alongside new gas-fired power generation, giving the investor group simultaneous interests in data centers, power generation, and utility ownership."
why_it_matters: "AES Ohio serves the Dayton area rather than Columbus, but PUCO's approval of a private equity buyer targeting returns well above what regulated utilities have historically earned sets a regulatory precedent that AEP Ohio's own owners could point to if they pursue a similar sale."
