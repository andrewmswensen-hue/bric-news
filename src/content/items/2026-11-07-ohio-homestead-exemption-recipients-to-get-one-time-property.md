detail: "House Bill 479, signed into law in June 2026, allocates $350 million for a one-time reduction on 2027 property tax bills for Ohio homeowners currently receiving the Homestead Exemption, according to the Sentinel-Tribune. The state estimates about 710,000 homeowners statewide will qualify, with the credit expected to run $450 to $500 per home, a figure the state will finalize based on the total number of eligible recipients. Homeowners do not need to apply separately; the credit will be applied automatically to qualifying bills.\n\nOhio currently offers two Homestead Exemption tiers. The standard exemption shields $29,000 of a home's market value from taxation for seniors 65 and older, surviving spouses of past recipients, or permanently and totally disabled homeowners with household income at or below $41,000 for 2026 applications. The enhanced exemption shields $58,000 of value for 100 percent disabled veterans and surviving spouses of first responders killed in the line of duty, with no age or income limit. Wood County Auditor Matthew Oestreich said the bill is a first step toward broader property tax relief and that the one-time credit will bring his county's homeowners about $3.4 million in combined savings."
