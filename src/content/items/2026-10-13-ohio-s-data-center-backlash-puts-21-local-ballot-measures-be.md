detail: "A growing backlash against data center development has produced a record number of local ballot measures across Ohio, Inside Climate News reports. Ten communities, including Granville, Pataskala, Conneaut, Defiance, Oregon, Piqua, Sunbury, Upper Sandusky, Trenton, and Urbana, will vote Nov. 3 on local bans or restrictions on data centers, part of about 40 such measures nationwide this cycle and the most of any state, according to Ballotpedia. Opponents, organized through a group called Conserve Ohio, are also gathering signatures for a statewide ballot measure that could reach voters in 2027.\n\nThe debate centers partly on electricity and water use: data centers draw heavily on both, and critics argue nearby residents could see rising utility bills and strained water supplies, while developers such as Amazon Web Services and Meta dispute some of those claims. The Ohio General Assembly created a Select Committee on Data Centers last spring, which has met five times to gather input from local officials, developers, and residents, but lawmakers have not yet passed a statewide pause.\n\nBoth major candidates for Ohio governor, Democrat Amy Acton and Republican Vivek Ramaswamy, have said they support some version of a moratorium, though they differ on the details, including incentive rollbacks and consumer electricity protections."
