detail: "South Western City Schools voters in Grove City will decide Nov. 3 whether to approve Issue 6, a 2.26-mill bond issue to replace the district's two high schools, Grove City and Westland, both opened in 1970, according to NBC4 WCMH-TV. The district says the measure would not raise its overall property tax rate because the new levy offsets collections for existing bond debt that is being paid off. If voters reject Issue 6, the district estimates property taxes would instead decrease by about $79 a year for every $100,000 of a property's appraised value, as that old debt retires without being replaced. Passage would let the district draw roughly $113 million the state has committed through the Ohio Facilities Construction Commission toward a project the district puts at about $226 million total, split roughly evenly between state and local funding. Bond proceeds can only fund construction and renovation, not salaries or operations. Grove City Council passed a resolution supporting Issue 6 on Sept. 21. The district says 175 juniors were waitlisted for career and technical programs at the South Western Career Academy during the 2025-26 school year, part of its rationale for added space. The district last placed a bond issue on the ballot in 2018. If the local share isn't approved, the state funding would go to another Ohio district instead."
why_it_matters: "For a rental property in the South Western district assessed at $200,000, the Nov. 3 outcome is the difference between a flat bond tax line and roughly $158 a year less in property taxes if the levy fails, a number landlords in Grove City and Westland should pencil into 2027 budgets either way."
