title: "HUD loosens Section 18 rule to open public housing to private capital"
summary: "HUD updated its Section 18 rule in late August to make it easier for public housing authorities to convert units to Section 8 vouchers and draw in private financing for repairs and redevelopment. The change widens which buildings count as functionally obsolete, letting more public housing built before 1950 qualify for demolition or redevelopment, and raises the portfolio-size threshold for using the rule from 50 units to 75. Mixed-finance properties can also use it once their 15-year low-income housing tax credit period ends. The nation's public housing carries a $170 billion repair backlog."
detail: "Section 18 of the United States Housing Act lets public housing authorities transition units out of the traditional public housing funding model and into a Section 8 voucher structure, which opens the door to private developers and lenders for building upgrades that federal operating and capital funds do not cover. HUD's August update loosens the criteria in three ways, according to Tanya Dempsey, co-CEO of the municipal advisory firm CSG Advisors.\n\nFirst, it expands the definition of a functionally obsolete building, a category that can now include any of the more than 270,000 public housing units built before 1950 that lack features such as elevators. Second, it raises the size limit for agencies that can reposition public housing into vouchers from 50 units to 75, letting more small public housing authorities use the tool. Third, it allows mixed-finance properties, built with a combination of low-income housing tax credits and federal subsidy, to use Section 18 once their 15-year tax credit compliance period ends.\n\nHUD estimates the nation's public housing stock carries a $170 billion backlog of capital needs, and the agency is betting the rule change will draw private developers, tax credit investors and government-backed lenders into repairing and redeveloping aging public housing."
