title: "Meijer plans three new Central Ohio stores, part of $1 billion Obetz project"
summary: "Meijer is planning three new Central Ohio supercenters, in Jerome Township, Violet Township and Obetz, continuing its expansion in the region, according to WhatNow. In Violet Township, the retailer plans a store at Pickerington Road and Refugee Road. In Obetz, Meijer will anchor part of the $1 billion Buckstone development after previously conducting site due diligence."
detail: "Meijer, based in Walker, Michigan, operates more than 500 stores across the Midwest and continues expanding its Central Ohio footprint with three new planned locations, according to WhatNow.\n\nIn Violet Township, the company plans a signature supercenter at the intersection of Pickerington Road and Refugee Road, adding a grocery and general merchandise option in that part of the county. In Obetz, the city has announced Meijer will be part of the roughly $1 billion Buckstone development, a large-scale mixed project for the community; Meijer had previously said it was conducting due diligence on the site before committing.\n\nIn Jerome Township near Dublin, Meijer plans a store spanning approximately 160,000 square feet on a 29.5-acre site west of Hyland Croy Road and north of Post Road, bordered by Route 33 to the east and near the recently opened Coughlin Kia of Dublin. Jerome Township trustees approved rezoning for the property in March; the site had previously been rezoned in 2007 for a large-scale retail development described at the time as a 'Target-style center,' and township officials said the Meijer project is generally consistent with that prior plan, though the 2026 rezoning was needed to reconfigure the site. The property also has room for additional outlot development, subject to future township approval."
why_it_matters: "Three new supercenters anchoring growth corridors in Violet Township, Obetz and Jerome Township is the kind of retail commitment that tends to pull rooftops and rents up around it, and the Jerome Township site's rezoning history, approved for large-scale retail as far back as 2007, shows how long some of these Dublin-area parcels have been positioned for exactly this kind of development."
