detail: "Granville village residents will vote Nov. 3 on a charter amendment banning construction of any data center with a peak electrical load above 25 megawatts inside the village. Resident Tracee Laing initiated the measure and collected 168 signatures to qualify it for the ballot, telling village council that residents are concerned about data centers' utility consumption, emissions and noise. Laing said Licking County already has 49 data centers. The ballot language was drafted by Conserve Ohio, a statewide group organizing similar bans; Granville is one of three Licking County communities, along with Sunbury and Pataskala, voting on data center restrictions this cycle, and one of 21 Ohio communities among the 39 data center measures on ballots nationwide this year, according to Ballotpedia. A Bowling Green State University poll from September found 75% of likely voters opposed a data center in their own community. Concerns in the area trace partly to Intel's 2022 announcement of a $28 billion manufacturing plant in Licking County and the nearby New Albany data center cluster. Only Granville village residents, not township residents outside the village limits, will vote on the data center ban; both will vote separately on a township greenspace preservation levy renewal."
why_it_matters: "A ban like Granville's would block new data center construction but leaves zoning for housing and small commercial development untouched, so Columbus-area investors watching Licking County should track whether Sunbury's and Pataskala's parallel measures pass too, since a cluster of bans could redirect data center and related infrastructure investment toward communities that haven't restricted it."
