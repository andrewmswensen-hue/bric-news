detail: "Circleville City Council voted on September 1 to annex 369 acres for Scioto Preserve, a planned development spanning more than 314 acres that would add over 1,200 single-family homes and apartments, along with a clubhouse, pool, playgrounds, and walking paths, according to planning documents. City officials estimate the project could grow Circleville's population by as much as 10%. Residents including David White and Kindra Nye have raised concerns about traffic, school crowding, and strain on emergency services and utilities, and say the developer has discussed tax increment financing and abatements without answering their questions about how new infrastructure and school costs would be covered. The Circleville City School District Board of Education passed a resolution stating it will not support residential tax abatements for the project unless the district is fully compensated for added enrollment, warning that uncompensated growth could lead to new levies and additional burden on existing property taxpayers. Developers have said they plan to negotiate tax incentives with the city during rezoning. A resident group is collecting signatures to force a public vote on the annexation and needs to submit them by October 1; if the petition falls short, the city's planning and zoning commission is scheduled to review the development's site plan on October 7."
