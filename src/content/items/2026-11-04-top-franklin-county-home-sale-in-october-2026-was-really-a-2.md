summary: "The priciest Franklin County property sale in October 2026 was 2088 London-Groveport Road in Grove City, which sold for $2,626,800 on October 16 to Epcon Harris LLC, built mostly of about 34 acres of farmland-classified parcels rather than home value, according to Columbus Navigator. detail: "The most expensive residential sale recorded in Franklin County in October 2026 was less a house sale than a land deal, Columbus Navigator reported. 2088 London-Groveport Road in Grove City sold for $2,626,800 on October 16 to Epcon Harris LLC, which now owns 85 nearby parcels; the sale covered roughly 34 acres across three parcels, nearly all of it land the county auditor classifies as farmland. No house sale reached $3 million this time, down from a $4 million sale that topped September's list.\n\nThree of the remaining top five sales were resales of existing homes, two in New Albany and one in Upper Arlington, that last changed hands between 2017 and 2020 for $1.075 million to $1.295 million and sold again in October for between $2.15 million and $2.4 million. A separate $3.5 million sale, an 81-acre farm with a 1905 house in Madison Township, was left off the ranking because the county classifies it as a farm sale rather than a home sale.\n\nThe Franklin County Auditor's weekly sales reports, which cover October 1 through 29, show about 1,450 homes sold countywide at a median price near $303,000. Parcel records show two additional October sales at $2 million or more, for properties on Hard Road and Walnut Street, that never appeared in a weekly report."
why_it_matters: "The Grove City sale shows raw farmland near the city pricing like future development land rather than current agricultural use, useful context for investors comparing per-acre land offers in that corridor. It is also a reminder that the auditor's weekly sales reports can miss large sales for weeks, so comps for big parcels are worth checking against parcel records directly."
