title: "HUD Proposes Work Requirements and Time Limits for Housing Assistance"
summary: "The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development unveiled a proposed rule that would let local public housing authorities require up to 40 hours of weekly work and set time limits of two years or more for able-bodied adults receiving federal housing assistance. HUD Secretary Scott Turner announced the proposal alongside the Housing Authority of Champaign County, Illinois and the Vermilion Housing Authority, the first two agencies to join a new voluntary coalition backing work requirements. Seniors, children and people with disabilities would be exempt."
detail: "HUD's proposed rule would give local public housing authorities the option, not the obligation, to require up to 40 hours of weekly work and impose time limits of two years or more on able-bodied adults who receive public housing or voucher assistance. Seniors, children and people with disabilities would not be affected. HUD cited federal data showing nearly half of non-elderly, able-bodied assisted households had no working adult in 2024, and that the average stay in major rental assistance programs has grown to eight or nine years, with about half of able-bodied voucher recipients staying more than 15 years. Alongside the rule, HUD launched a \"Work and Dignity Coalition\" for housing authorities that want to adopt work-focused policies. The Housing Authority of Champaign County and the Vermilion Housing Authority became the first two members. HUD estimated the change could generate up to $51 million in additional income nationally. The proposal now enters the federal rulemaking process, which requires a public comment period before any final rule takes effect. No timeline was given for when a final rule might be adopted."
why_it_matters: "If finalized, adoption is up to each local housing authority, so Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority would separately decide whether to add work requirements for its voucher holders, which could eventually change turnover and income stability among tenants using vouchers at Columbus-area rentals."
