title: "Ohio budget language exempts megaproject township zoning from referendum"
summary: "A provision in Ohio's biennial budget exempts township zoning tied to a state-designated megaproject from citizen referendum. According to a report by Inkstick Media published by Farm and Dairy, emails obtained through a public records request show the amendment language came from a registered lobbyist for the defense manufacturer Anduril, which is building a plant in Madison Township in Pickaway County. The lobbyist emailed an aide to state Rep. Brian Stewart, who chairs the House Finance Committee, on April 3, 2025. The House passed a budget containing the provision six days later."
detail: "Ross Larue and Paul Loar, farmers in Madison Township in Pickaway County, gathered signatures in 2022 and again in 2025 to put township rezonings to a referendum. Both attempts failed after developer objections and signature disqualifications by the county board of elections, which told Inkstick Media that developer pressure did not drive its review. Farm and Dairy published the Inkstick Media report. Emails released in May under a public records request show that on April 3, 2025, Levi Gross, a registered lobbyist for Anduril, wrote to an aide for state Rep. Brian Stewart describing an amendment exempting township zoning connected to a megaproject from referendum. Gross wrote that citizen referendums posed a risk to the Anduril project, which required rezoning. Six days later the House passed its first version of House Bill 96, the biennial budget, with language on page 811 exempting zoning for property involved in a megaproject from referendum. Stewart represents Pickaway County and chairs the House Finance Committee, where the budget is marked up. Stewart, his aide, and Anduril did not respond to requests for comment, according to the report. Anduril announced the 5 million square foot plant, called Arsenal-1, in January 2025. The rezoning at issue covered 170 acres moving from rural and residential to business."
why_it_matters: "A township referendum has been the tool Ohio residents used to undo a rezoning after trustees approved it. Where a project carries the state's megaproject designation, that route is closed, which raises the odds a large industrial rezoning south of Columbus closes on its stated schedule and shortens the period when nearby land pricing is still contested."
