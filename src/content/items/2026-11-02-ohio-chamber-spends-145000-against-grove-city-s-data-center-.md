title: "Ohio Chamber Spends $145,000 Against Grove City's Data Center Ballot Measure"
summary: "Grove City voters decide Issue 28, the Community Consent Amendment requiring a public vote on large developments, on Nov. 3. Campaign finance filings with the Franklin County Board of Elections show the anti-Issue 28 group Save Grove City has raised $145,000 from eight donors, with help from the Ohio Chamber of Commerce, compared with about $3,500 from 34 donors for the pro-Issue 28 group Protect Grove City, according to the Statehouse News Bureau."
detail: "Grove City's Nov. 3 vote on Issue 28, the Community Consent Amendment requiring voter approval before large developments can proceed, is one of 21 data center-related ballot measures across 18 Ohio municipalities this election, the most of any state, according to the Statehouse News Bureau. Ohio has more than 200 data center projects planned or under construction, the fifth-highest total nationally.\n\nThe measure grew out of a 310-acre data center proposal from developer Headwaters Site Development, which Grove City halted with a one-year moratorium after residents including farm owner Patricia Spillman organized as Protect Grove City. Preliminary Franklin County Board of Elections filings show Protect Grove City has raised just over $3,500 from 34 donors, a grassroots effort. The opposing group, Save Grove City, has raised $145,000 from eight donors; the Ohio Chamber of Commerce helped establish the committee. Save Grove City volunteer Cassie Ferguson argues the amendment's thresholds, 50 acres, 20 megawatts of power, or 500,000 gallons of water a day, are broad enough to delay unrelated projects like new school construction. Grove City Mayor Ike Stage also opposes the measure. Protect Grove City's Jen Belt said the group wrote the amendment broadly on purpose, after seeing other Ohio communities that targeted data centers directly face lawsuits from developers."
why_it_matters: "The lopsided spending, about $145,000 from an industry-backed group against $3,500 from local organizers, shows how much the data center industry will spend to defeat a citizen-vote requirement in just one suburb, a sign investors watching similar measures elsewhere in Central Ohio should expect well-funded opposition campaigns too."
