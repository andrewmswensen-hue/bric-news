title: "HUD proposes rule letting housing authorities set work requirements, time limits"
summary: "HUD unveiled a proposed rule that would let local public housing agencies impose work requirements of up to 40 hours a week and time limits of two years or more on able-bodied adults receiving federal housing assistance. HUD Secretary Scott Turner announced it in Champaign, Illinois, where two housing authorities became the first to join a new HUD coalition. Seniors, children and people with disabilities would not be affected. The rule must go through a public comment period before it can take effect."
detail: "HUD Secretary Scott Turner announced a proposed rule in Champaign, Illinois, according to Chambana Today, carried by WIXY. The rule would give public housing agencies flexibility to require up to 40 hours per week of work and to set time limits of two years or more for able-bodied adults receiving federal housing assistance. Turner said seniors, children and people with disabilities would not be affected.\n\nThe proposal requires a public comment period before it can take effect. HUD also launched a Work and Dignity Coalition to encourage housing authorities to adopt work-focused policies. The Housing Authority of Champaign County and the Vermilion Housing Authority were the first two agencies to join.\n\nTurner cited federal data that nearly half of non-elderly, able-bodied assisted households had no working adult in 2024. The report also cites HUD figures that the average stay in major rental assistance programs has grown to eight or nine years, and that roughly half of able-bodied voucher recipients stay more than 15 years.\n\nThe rule would be optional for local agencies rather than a national mandate, based on the description in the source. It does not spell out how a requirement would work for households using housing choice vouchers with private landlords, and no effective date is given."
why_it_matters: "Landlords who rent to Section 8 voucher holders would be affected only if the local housing authority chooses to adopt the flexibility, so the decision would likely sit with the Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority. Any resulting change in voucher tenure or household income would show up in tenant retention for voucher units."
