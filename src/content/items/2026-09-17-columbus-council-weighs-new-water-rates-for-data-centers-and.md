title: "Columbus Council weighs new water rates for data centers and large users"
summary: "Columbus City Council heard proposals Wednesday to curb data center effects on utility costs without a moratorium. One would create new water rates or fees for high-volume users, and it had its first reading this week with a final vote possible at the next meeting. Other items cover decommissioning rules, a water conservation plan, and limits on on-site power generation. Council President Shannon Hardin said large users should cover the infrastructure their demand requires."
detail: "WOSU reports that Columbus City Council's Public Utilities and Sustainability Committee, chaired by Christopher Wyche, held a public hearing Wednesday on regulations aimed at data centers and other large developments. It was the third such hearing in a year without a moratorium proposal, despite public demand for one.\n\nThe measures under consideration include new water rates, charges, or fees for high-load, high-volume users, rules for decommissioning large data centers, a water conservation plan, and limits on environmental effects from on-site power generation. The water rate legislation had its first reading this week and could get a final vote at the next council meeting.\n\nCouncil President Shannon Hardin said charging only for water volume misses the infrastructure a large user requires, and that if demand forces new construction, those users should pay for it. Wyche said many data centers are outside city limits but draw from Columbus's water system. The city is seeking bids on a fourth water treatment plant expected to cost billions of dollars. A Bowling Green State University poll cited in the article found 78 percent of 1,000 likely voters favor a temporary ban on new data centers. Most speakers asked for a moratorium, while union representatives spoke in support of the projects."
why_it_matters: "Water and sewer costs on Columbus-served rentals depend on how the city recovers the cost of a treatment plant, and this rate structure is the first decision on who pays for that capacity."
