title: "Columbus Adds No-Mow Buffer Rule for Apartment Retention Ponds"
summary: "Columbus City Council on Monday approved new safety rules for retention ponds at apartment complexes, requiring a 15-foot no-mow buffer around the water to preserve visibility. The ordinance also requires written safety notices be posted in common areas and delivered to tenants. It follows the city's first retention pond law in 2024, which required warning signs, life preservers, and tenant notices. Ten children have drowned in Columbus retention ponds since 2019, including three this year."
detail: "Columbus City Council approved new retention pond safety requirements for apartment complexes, adding to the city's 2024 ordinance after renewed concern over child drownings. The new rule requires property owners to preserve existing vegetation within 15 feet of a pond's edge, banning mowing or cutting in that buffer to keep the water visible, though it does not require new landscaping. Safety notices must now be delivered to tenants as a standalone written document and posted in a common area, and if the city declares a pond a public nuisance, the notice must explain the reasoning.\n\nThe 2024 law already required apartment owners to warn tenants, post signage, and maintain life preservers at retention ponds, and gave the city authority to declare dangerous ponds public nuisances. According to WSYX, Columbus Building and Zoning Services has identified, mapped, and inspected more than 100 retention ponds at apartment complexes since 2024 but has not used the public nuisance process. Ten children have drowned in Columbus retention ponds since 2019, including three this year: 2-year-old Georgina Villar in July and 2-year-old Beeny Mbo in September, whose complex was found in compliance with existing rules at the time of her death.\n\nThe ordinance applies to multi-unit residential properties with retention ponds on site."
why_it_matters: "Any Columbus landlord with a retention pond on an apartment property now has to keep a 15-foot no-mow strip around it and hand tenants a standalone written safety notice, on top of the signage and life-preserver rules already in place since 2024."
