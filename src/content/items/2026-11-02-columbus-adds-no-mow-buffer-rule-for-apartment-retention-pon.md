title: "Columbus adds no-mow buffer rule for apartment retention ponds"
summary: "Columbus City Council approved new safety requirements Monday for retention ponds at apartment complexes, including a 15-foot no-mow buffer around the water, according to WSYX. Ten children have drowned in Columbus retention ponds since 2019, including three this year. The city has mapped and inspected more than 100 retention ponds at apartment complexes since a 2024 ordinance required warning signs, life preservers, and tenant safety notices, but has not yet declared any pond a public nuisance under that law. The new rules also require written safety notices posted in common areas."
detail: "Columbus City Council passed emergency legislation Monday adding a 15-foot no-mow buffer around retention ponds at apartment complexes, sponsored by Council President Pro Tem Rob Dorans, WSYX reported. The buffer is meant to create a natural barrier between children and the water by preserving existing vegetation rather than requiring new landscaping. The ordinance also requires owners to deliver written safety notices to tenants as a standalone document and post them in a common area, and to explain the city's reasoning if a pond is declared a public nuisance. The rules build on a 2024 ordinance that required warning signs, life preservers, and tenant notices and gave the city authority to declare dangerous ponds a public nuisance, a step Columbus Building and Zoning Services has not yet taken despite inspecting more than 100 apartment retention ponds. The changes follow the deaths of two toddlers in separate ponds this year, including one at a complex that code enforcement had confirmed was in compliance with existing signage and buoy requirements. Dorans said he plans to introduce legislation requiring permanent physical barriers before the end of the year."
why_it_matters: "Owners of apartment complexes with retention ponds now have a specific landscaping rule to follow immediately (the 15-foot no-mow buffer) and should expect a second round of legislation on permanent barriers before year-end, since compliance with the 2024 signage rules alone did not prevent a death this year."
