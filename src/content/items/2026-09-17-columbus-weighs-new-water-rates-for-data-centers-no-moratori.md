summary: "Columbus City Council is considering a package of regulations aimed at data centers and other high-volume water users, including a new rate structure that would charge them for the infrastructure their demand requires, rather than pursuing a moratorium on new construction. detail: "Columbus City Council is weighing a package of regulations for data centers and other large water users rather than a construction moratorium, despite a recent Bowling Green State University poll finding 78 percent of likely voters favor a temporary ban. Council member Christopher Wyche, who chairs the Public Utilities and Sustainability Committee, said a moratorium would be largely symbolic since most data centers tapping Columbus water sit outside city limits, and said a regulatory framework could instead be a model other Ohio municipalities adopt.\n\nThe proposals include a new rate structure for high-load, high-volume water users, a decommissioning plan for large data centers, a water conservation plan, and rules addressing on-site power generation. The rate ordinance received its first reading this week and could reach a final vote at the next council meeting. Council President Shannon Hardin said the goal is ensuring high-volume users cover the infrastructure costs their demand creates, including a planned fourth water treatment plant expected to cost billions of dollars, rather than spreading those costs across residential and small business ratepayers."
why_it_matters: "If Columbus finalizes rate-based charges for high-volume water users this fall, it sets a template other Central Ohio municipalities may copy, meaning landlords in areas near planned data center campuses should watch for similar utility rate structures reaching their own water bills."
