title: "Columbus sets 70-degree floor for rental heat as winter approaches"
summary: "Ohio law (ORC 5321.04) requires landlords to supply reasonable heat at all times, and Columbus City Code sets a number: heating that can hold habitable rooms and bathrooms at 70 degrees Fahrenheit when it is zero degrees outside, according to RL Property Management, BRIC's editorial partner. A tenant paying the gas bill does not shift furnace repairs to the tenant; landlords remain responsible for equipment they supply."
detail: "RL Property Management, BRIC's disclosed editorial partner, published a guide to Ohio's rental heat requirements as the 2026 heating season begins. Ohio Revised Code 5321.04 requires landlords to provide reasonable heat at all times, with narrow exceptions, and to keep any heating equipment they supply in good working order, even when the tenant's name is on the gas bill. Columbus City Code 4523.05 sets the specific standard inside city limits: heating facilities must be able to hold habitable rooms and bathrooms at 70 degrees Fahrenheit when it is zero degrees outside, and the occupant must keep the heat running enough to prevent frozen pipes when the occupant controls it. The code also bans unvented space heaters and open-flame radiant heaters, and sets clearance requirements for other room heaters: 12 inches at the rear and sides, 24 inches at the top and front. If a furnace fails in freezing weather, a tenant who is current on rent can give written notice and, if the landlord does not fix it within a reasonable time or 30 days, deposit rent with the court clerk under ORC 5321.07. The city can also issue emergency vacate orders and bill the owner for relocation costs when the heating failure was the owner's responsibility, up to three months of temporary housing or three times HUD fair market rent for a permanent move, and housing code violations carry fines up to $1,000 per offense for an individual and up to $5,000 for an organization, with each day counting as a separate offense. Under the city's rental registry, five or more heating violation notices within 12 months can push a property of up to 100 units into conditional registry status. The guide cites a January 2025 case in which Columbus sued the owners of a Far East Side apartment complex over units with little or no heat, and notes that suburbs like Gahanna, Westerville, and Dublin set their own property maintenance codes, which can differ from Columbus on both temperature and enforcement."
