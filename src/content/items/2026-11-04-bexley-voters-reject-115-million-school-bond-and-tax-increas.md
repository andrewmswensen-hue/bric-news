title: "Bexley Voters Reject $115 Million School Bond and Tax Increase"
summary: "Bexley City Schools voters rejected a combined bond issue and income tax increase meant to fund a $115 million construction and renovation project, according to unofficial Franklin County results. The combined measure, Issue 5, failed 53.5% to 46.5%. It paired a roughly $30 million bond, costing $72 per $100,000 of appraised home value over 30 years, with a 0.5 percentage point income tax increase, from 0.75% to 1.25%, projected to raise $85 million. The district says it is the only central Ohio school system that has not upgraded its facilities in more than 25 years."
detail: "Bexley City Schools asked voters on Nov. 3 to approve Issue 5, a combined bond and permanent income tax levy to fund the first phase of a long-range facilities plan, including a new middle school, renovations at other buildings, and maintenance upgrades at Carlton Smith Field. Unofficial Franklin County results show voters rejected the combined measure 53.5% to 46.5%, with all precincts reporting. The bond portion would have raised nearly $30 million, at a cost of 2.05 mills, or $72 per year for every $100,000 of a home's appraised value under the Franklin County Auditor's valuation, over 30 years. The accompanying income tax increase, from the district's current 0.75% to 1.25%, would have taken effect Jan. 1, 2027, and was projected to generate $85 million for capital improvements. Bexley City Schools said the property tax portion would have been offset by an expiring bond payment set to roll off in 2027, meaning the measure was not expected to raise the district's overall property tax burden on its own. The district did not respond to a request for comment after the results were reported. Bexley City Schools says it is the only district in central Ohio that has not updated its facilities in more than 25 years."
why_it_matters: "Bexley property owners avoid a new income tax increase for now, but the rejected bond means the district's aging-facilities question, and whatever levy follows it, is still ahead of Bexley landlords and homeowners."
