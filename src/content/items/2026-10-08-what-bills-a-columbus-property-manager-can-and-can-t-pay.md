summary: "RL Property Management outlined which bills it pays on an owner's behalf and which stay with the landlord. The Columbus firm pays repair and maintenance invoices, vendor and turnover costs, and utilities during vacancy, drawing from rent or a maintenance reserve of $350 to $1,500 per unit depending on plan. Four bills remain the owner's responsibility: the mortgage, property insurance, property taxes, and HOA or COA dues. Ohio law, ORC 4735.18(A)(27), requires brokerages managing rental property to hold rent and deposits in a separate trust account."
detail: "RL Property Management, a Columbus brokerage and the site's disclosed editorial partner, published a breakdown of which bills a property manager handles directly and which the owner keeps paying.\n\nUnder RLPM's plans, in house repairs run $84 an hour plus a $15 trip charge and materials. Outside vendor work carries a 15% project management fee on costs up to $3,000, capped at $450 per project. Turnover work between residents, covering paint, cleaning, and flooring, carries a tiered fee: 15% on turns up to $15,000, 10% from $15,001 to $25,000, and 7.5% above that. The company also keeps utilities running on vacant units at the owner's expense.\n\nFour bills stay with the property owner under every RLPM plan: the mortgage, property insurance, property taxes, and HOA or COA dues. RLPM notes many mortgage servicers already escrow for taxes and insurance, and that notices for all four bills go directly to the owner of record. In Franklin County, 2025 tax year bills were due February 28 and July 20, 2026, and Ohio Revised Code 323.121 adds a 10% penalty for late payment, half waived if paid within ten days.\n\nRepair approval limits, the dollar threshold a manager can spend before calling the owner first, differ by plan tier: $1,500 on the Passive plan, $750 on Standard, and $350 on Premium. Ohio Revised Code 4735.18(A)(27) requires any brokerage managing rental property to hold rents and security deposits in a separate trust account from the company's own operating funds.\n\nBecause owners pay the four excluded bills directly, those payments will not appear on a manager's year end statement, so owners need to track mortgage interest, taxes, insurance, and HOA dues themselves for tax filing."
