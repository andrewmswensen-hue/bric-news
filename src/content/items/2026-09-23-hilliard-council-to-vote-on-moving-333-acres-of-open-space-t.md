title: "Hilliard council to vote on moving 333 acres of open space to Metro Parks"
summary: "Hilliard City Council is scheduled to vote Sept. 28 on transferring about 333 acres of conservation land, tied to the Hill Farm developments, to Columbus and Franklin County Metro Parks. The transfer would expand Homestead Metro Park to 376 acres in the Big Darby Creek watershed. Hilliard requires 70 percent open space in new developments there."
detail: "According to NBC4, Hilliard City Council is expected to approve on Sept. 28 a plan to transfer roughly 333 acres of conservation land from the city to Columbus and Franklin County Metro Parks. The proposal was presented Sept. 14. The land would expand Homestead Metro Park to 376 acres and bring Metro Parks' preserved land in the Big Darby Creek watershed above 10,000 acres.\n\nThe legislation would amend Hilliard's agreements with M/I Homes to allow conservation land within the Hill Farm and Hill Farm II developments, including the headwaters of Clover Groff Run, to move from the developer to Metro Parks. Council approved Hill Farm II in March 2025. That 300-home extension sets aside 186 of its 265 acres as open space. Council separately authorized a transfer of about 35 acres from Clover Meadows in June. The wider expansion also draws on land assembled through agreements involving Fischer Homes, Pulte Homes and Rockford Homes.\n\nHilliard adopted a policy in 2023 requiring 70 percent open space in new developments in the area, compared with the 50 percent standard followed by other Big Darby Accord communities. Metro Parks would maintain and patrol some regional trails, including about 2.4 miles of the Heritage Trail, and plans to restore wetlands, prairies and forests. No timeline was given."
why_it_matters: "The 70 percent open space rule in Hilliard's Darby-area developments shows how much of a parcel there can actually be built on, which matters for anyone underwriting land or new-build lots in that part of Franklin County. The park expansion also fixes a large block of acreage as permanent open space."
