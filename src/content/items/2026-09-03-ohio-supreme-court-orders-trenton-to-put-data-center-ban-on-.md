title: "Ohio Supreme Court orders Trenton to put data center ban on the ballot"
summary: "The Ohio Supreme Court ruled unanimously Sept. 1 that Trenton City Council must place a citizen petition banning large data centers on a special election ballot, rejecting the council's argument that petitioners needed more signatures than state law requires. The court held that charter amendment petitions need signatures equal to 10% of votes cast in the city's last municipal election, 128 in Trenton's case, not 10% of registered voters, which would have required 820."
detail: "The Butler County Board of Elections certified 336 valid signatures on the petition, well above the 128 the court found necessary. Trenton City Council had voted 4-3 in early August to reject the petition, arguing the threshold should be based on total registered voters rather than turnout in the last election, but the court found council had a mandatory constitutional duty to certify it once the signature count was met. A special election must now be scheduled within 60 to 120 days as required under the Ohio Constitution. The ruling does not affect the under-construction 250-megawatt AWS campus, but a second proposed 18-building, roughly 600-acre AWS project in neighboring townships remains pending a separate annexation request into Trenton, with a public hearing before Butler County commissioners scheduled for Sept. 15. If that land is annexed, whether the new charter amendment would apply could depend on the timing of annexation relative to the special election."
why_it_matters: "For Central Ohio investors watching how suburbs handle large-scale industrial and data center proposals, this ruling sets a low signature bar for forcing a citywide vote on a zoning-adjacent ballot measure even over a council's objection, a precedent other Ohio communities fighting similar projects can now cite."
