title: "Test wells near Alexandria, Granville could supply 9.3M gallons a day"
summary: "Test wells drilled near Alexandria and Granville to find water sources for Intel's New Albany chip plant found the potential to produce up to 9.3 million gallons of water a day, the Reporting Project reported. The state funded the testing, but with Intel's production start now delayed to 2031 and Columbus already committed to supplying the plant's roughly 5 million gallons a day, New Albany and Granville officials say there is no rush to tap the new sources."
detail: "Test wells drilled at two sites near Alexandria and one near Granville, in the Raccoon Creek valley, found the potential to produce up to 9.3 million gallons of water a day combined, according to a report by Eagon & Associates cited by the Reporting Project. The State of Ohio funded the testing as part of its commitment to secure water for Intel's New Albany chip plant, but with Intel's production start delayed to 2031 and the City of Columbus already positioned to supply the plant's roughly 5 million gallons a day, New Albany and Granville officials say there is no immediate need to develop the new sources. Granville Village Manager Herb Koehler said the village supports 'responsible regional water development' but wants controls to protect its aquifer and well field before any water is drawn. One constraint on the Granville-area site: it sits near the former Granville Solvents property, a 15-year Superfund cleanup site, where contaminated groundwater could migrate toward Granville's public well field if a new well changed the direction of groundwater flow. New Albany, Granville, St. Albans Township, Alexandria, Licking County, and Ohio EPA jointly reviewed the testing, and New Albany officials say they are still evaluating the results before deciding on next steps."
