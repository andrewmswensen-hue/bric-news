title: "Ohio smoke and CO alarm rules for rentals sit in the fire code, not ORC 3781.104"
summary: "Ohio rentals need working smoke alarms under the Ohio Fire Code (OAC 1301:7-7-11), and carbon monoxide alarms when a unit meets any of four conditions in section 915. Existing buildings had a January 1, 2019 deadline for CO alarms. The statute many landlord guides cite, ORC 3781.104, applies only to buildings over 75 feet tall. Columbus requires hard-wired detectors in one- to three-family buildings built after April 26, 1978, and the owner installs them."
detail: "According to RL Property Management, the section of Ohio law most landlord content cites for smoke detectors, ORC 3781.104, covers apartment buildings and condominiums over 75 feet tall. The rules that reach a typical Central Ohio duplex or single-family rental are in the Ohio Fire Code, OAC 1301:7-7-11.\n\nRentals fall under Group R occupancies and need single-station smoke alarms. Interconnection inside a unit and hard-wiring with battery backup are required, with exceptions for existing buildings where no construction is taking place. Renovation therefore changes the answer.\n\nCarbon monoxide alarms are triggered by any one of four conditions: a fuel-burning appliance or fireplace in the unit, service by a fuel-burning forced-air furnace, a fuel-burning appliance elsewhere in the building with communicating openings, or an attached private garage. Existing buildings had until January 1, 2019 to install CO alarms, and battery-only units are permitted there.\n\nORC 5321.04(A)(1) and (A)(2) turn these code rules into a landlord duty to comply with safety codes and keep the premises fit and habitable. The source says a violation can be treated as negligence per se in Ohio. Columbus splits responsibility in writing: the owner installs under CCC 4529.08, and in one- to three-family dwellings the occupant handles operation and maintenance under CCC 4529.11. The 10-year replacement guideline comes from NFPA 72, manufacturers and the U.S. Fire Administration rather than Ohio law. The source describes its content as informational and not legal advice."
why_it_matters: "A landlord who cites the wrong statute can miss the trigger that applies, such as a gas water heater in a shared basement that puts CO alarms in every unit. In Columbus, the hard-wiring rule for one- to three-family buildings built after April 26, 1978 is the detail to check first."
