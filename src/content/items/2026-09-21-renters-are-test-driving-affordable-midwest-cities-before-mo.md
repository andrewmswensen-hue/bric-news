detail: "Zillow's August 2026 housing report tracked where rental searches are coming from outside a given metro area, treating an increase in outside interest as a leading indicator of future arrivals since renters tend to research a market before moving. Buffalo led with a 4.2 percent year-over-year increase in outside searches, followed by Houston at 3.4 percent and New Orleans at 3 percent. Zillow chief economist Mischa Fisher said renters are increasingly finding affordable mid-sized cities rather than following the traditional Sunbelt migration pattern.\n\nTypical asking rent across Zillow's most-searched cities remained below the national figure of $1,948 a month, which requires a household income of about $77,919 to afford comfortably. Among local, not just out-of-town, renter interest, Cincinnati posted the strongest annual increase at 8.1 percent, followed by Jacksonville at 4.6 percent and Columbus at 3.2 percent.\n\nAccording to Hamilton Project data cited in the piece, large institutional investors own the highest share of single-family rentals in 20 markets concentrated in the Southeast and Southwest, leaving many Midwest and Northeast markets, including Columbus, comparatively more open to individually owned rental portfolios, though the article notes competition from other small investors in these same affordable markets is increasing quickly."
