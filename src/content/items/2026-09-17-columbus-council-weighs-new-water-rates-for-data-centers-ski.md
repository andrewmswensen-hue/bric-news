detail: "Columbus City Council member Christopher Wyche, who chairs the Public Utilities and Sustainability Committee, held a third public hearing in the past year on data center regulation without introducing the moratorium many residents have demanded. A Bowling Green State University poll of 1,000 likely voters found 78 percent favor a temporary ban on new data center construction pending more information, including majorities across party lines. Wyche said a moratorium would be largely symbolic since most data centers tapping Columbus utilities sit outside city limits, and said the city instead wants to build a regulatory framework other Ohio municipalities could copy. Proposals discussed include new water rates or fees for high-load users, a decommissioning plan for large data centers, a water conservation plan, and rules addressing on-site \"behind the meter\" power generation. Council President Shannon Hardin said the point is ensuring large users cover the cost of the infrastructure their demand requires, including a planned fourth water treatment plant expected to cost billions of dollars. Building trades union members who spoke at the hearing supported continued data center construction for the jobs it provides, while most public speakers, including a Franklin Township resident citing a similar fight in Pickaway County, asked for an outright pause. The new water-rate proposal for high-volume users had its first reading this week and could reach a final council vote at the next meeting."
