title: "242-unit apartment tower proposed for Arena District parking lot"
summary: "HP Land Development, an affiliate of building-supply company Hamilton Parker, filed plans with Columbus's Downtown Commission for One Twenty Vine, a seven-story, 242-unit building at 120 Vine St., replacing a surface parking lot one block from Nationwide Arena. The project is a partnership with Nationwide Realty Investors, developer of the surrounding Arena District, and would connect by skywalk to the neighboring Kilbourne Garage. The building has no retail and no public parking; residents would use enclosed first-floor parking and the adjacent garage."
detail: "The application combines nine parcels and portions of public right-of-way on the block bounded by Vine, Spruce, Armstrong and Kilbourne streets into a single site, listed as 120 Vine St. Plans filed with the city show two seven-story wings joined by an enclosed bridge over the garage entrance, with a leasing office and fitness center on the ground floor and an internal courtyard featuring a pool, gas grills and a gas fireplace.\n\nThe site currently operates as paid public parking, and the new building would not replace that public parking capacity: its ground-floor garage is for residents only, though a planned skywalk would connect the third floor to the neighboring Kilbourne Garage, which also serves the adjacent Two Twenty Vine apartments and the 500 Neil Avenue office building.\n\nHP Land Development, tied to local building-supply company Hamilton Parker, has owned the block for years and is partnering with Nationwide Realty Investors, the master developer of the Arena District, on the project. The same partnership previously built the neighboring Two Twenty Vine, a 124-unit building. The Columbus Architectural Studio designed both.\n\nThe Downtown Commission is scheduled to review the design September 22 at 8:30 a.m. If approved, the project still needs building permits before construction can begin, and the timeline for breaking ground has not been announced."
why_it_matters: "A 242-unit addition one block from Nationwide Arena removes a surface parking lot from downtown's supply without adding replacement public parking, which is worth tracking for any investor holding or evaluating property near North Market or the convention center where street parking is already tight."
