title: "Butler County property tax collections dip after state and local homeowner relief"
summary: "Butler County collected $659.7 million in property taxes this year, down from $664.9 million last year, as tax relief from a $3.8 billion state package and about $20 million in local rollbacks took effect. The delinquency rate fell to 3.14 percent. The county auditor estimates an average 20 percent value increase in its current reassessment, and a state lawmaker says new limits will make the resulting tax increases track inflation."
detail: "Butler County Treasurer Mike McNamara reported $659.7 million in property tax collections this year, compared with $664.9 million last year, according to the Journal-News. Total billing was $681 million, and the delinquency rate came in at 3.14 percent, down from 3.2 percent last year. McNamara read the lower delinquency rate as a sign that people are paying their taxes.\n\nThe article says it is hard to isolate the effect of relief measures because new construction and levies also move the totals. The state passed a $3.8 billion property tax reform package last year, which includes credits for residents in school districts at the 20-mill floor. Butler County Auditor Nancy Nix put those credits at about $17.5 million locally. County commissioners added roughly $20 million in relief by rolling back part of inside millage, about $32 per $100,000 of home value, plus targeted help for nearly 19,000 homeowners who qualify for the Ohio homestead exemption.\n\nNix is now in the six-year reassessment and estimates an average 20 percent increase in values, still under negotiation with the state tax department. State Rep. David Thomas told the Journal-News that the reforms cap increases in inside millage at the rate of inflation and raise the owner-occupied rollback to 15.38 percent over four years, so unvoted tax bills would rise with inflation rather than with values."
why_it_matters: "The state reforms lean toward owner-occupied homes through the larger rollback, which rental property does not receive. Investors in Franklin, Delaware and Licking counties should read local reappraisal notices with that difference in mind, since a value jump can pass through to a rental owner's bill more fully than to a neighbor who lives in the home."
