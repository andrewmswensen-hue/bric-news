detail: "The Office of the Ohio Consumers' Counsel (OCC), the state agency that represents residential utility customers in rate cases and regulatory proceedings, is marking 50 years since the General Assembly created it in 1976, according to WOSU. Director Maureen Willis said the office's role has stayed constant even as the issues it handles have changed, from early fights over winter utility disconnections, which led to rules requiring advance notice before shutoffs, to today's disputes over data center electricity demand.\n\nAmong the OCC's more recent efforts, Willis cited its work on submetering, the practice in which an apartment owner or other property manager buys utility service in bulk and resells electric, gas or water usage back to residents. Willis said submetering operators had functioned outside the consumer protections that apply to regulated utilities. This year, according to WOSU, the Ohio Supreme Court ruled that submetering company Nationwide Energy Partners qualifies as a public utility and must be regulated as one.\n\nWillis said the OCC's staff was cut in half in 2012 and remains reduced, and the office now works with outside lawyers, experts and other consumer groups to extend its reach. She said rising data center electricity demand is one of the biggest utility issues facing Ohio consumers today."
