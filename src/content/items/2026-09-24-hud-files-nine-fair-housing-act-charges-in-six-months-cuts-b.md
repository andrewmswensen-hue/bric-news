summary: "The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development filed nine Fair Housing Act discrimination charges over the past six months, the agency said. Four charges involve alleged discrimination against families with children, three involve alleged disability discrimination, and one involves alleged sexual harassment. HUD's Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity also said it cut its backlog of pending complaints by 46 percent since September 1, 2025, attributing the earlier backlog to case-closure delays under the prior administration."
detail: "HUD announced nine Fair Housing Act discrimination charges filed in the past six months. Four charges allege discriminatory rental practices or lease terms against families with children, three allege that housing providers failed to make reasonable accommodations for tenants with disabilities, and one alleges sexual harassment. HUD's Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity, which also enforces the Americans with Disabilities Act, Title VI of the Civil Rights Act, and the Violence Against Women Act, said it has reduced its backlog of pending Fair Housing Act complaints by 46 percent since September 1, 2025.\n\nHUD Secretary Scott Turner said the agency is clearing a case backlog he attributed to the prior administration, citing a 2024 HUD Office of Inspector General report that found roughly 70 percent of cases were not closed within the 100-day timeline the law requires. Assistant Secretary for Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity Craig Trainor said the office is enforcing the Fair Housing Act, which bars discrimination in housing sale or rental based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, and disability. The announcement did not name the housing providers involved in the nine charges or specify their locations. Anyone who believes they have experienced housing discrimination can file a complaint through HUD's website."
