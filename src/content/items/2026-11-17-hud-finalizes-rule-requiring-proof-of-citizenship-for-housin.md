title: "HUD finalizes rule requiring proof of citizenship for housing aid"
summary: "HUD published a final rule in the Federal Register this week closing a long-standing exception that let applicants for federal housing assistance self-attest citizenship without documentation. Starting Dec. 14, all household members in HUD-supported housing, including current tenants in mixed-status households, must verify citizenship or lawful immigration status with documents such as a passport, birth certificate, or residency paperwork. Housing authorities will offer only temporary prorated assistance while verification is pending, ending indefinite partial subsidies."
detail: "The rule, published in the Federal Register, revises enforcement regulations under Section 214 of the Housing and Community Development Act of 1980. Previously, applicants could self-attest U.S. citizenship without supporting documents, and some noncitizens could decline to confirm status under a \"do not contend\" option. The new rule eliminates that option: citizens must now produce a passport, birth certificate, naturalization certificate, or similar proof on request, and noncitizens must document lawful residency. The verification requirement applies to every household member regardless of age, including tenants in households with mixed immigration status, and reaches both new applicants and current residents of HUD-supported housing. Housing authorities will grant only temporary prorated assistance while a household's status is being verified, rather than the indefinite partial subsidies previously allowed, which HUD says will free up units for waitlisted applicants. The rule takes effect Dec. 14. HUD Secretary Scott Turner said in a statement the change stops ineligible residents from drawing resources meant for citizens and vulnerable groups, framing it as part of the administration's broader push against waste, fraud, and abuse in federal assistance programs. Advocacy groups and local governments, including in Los Angeles and Portland, have filed or announced lawsuits arguing the rule will break up mixed-status families who currently hold vouchers."
why_it_matters: "Landlords who accept Section 8 or other HUD-assisted tenants in Columbus could see rent payments shrink or pause for mixed-status households while local housing authorities re-verify every member's status before the Dec. 14 deadline, since only prorated aid is available during that review."
