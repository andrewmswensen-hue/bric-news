summary: "RentRedi co-founder Ed Barone told BiggerPockets that late-payment rates on the platform, which serves close to 200,000 landlords and renters, swing as much as 4x by state, from around 5% in Utah and Hawaii to 20% in states like Mississippi. Barone said a landlord with a handful of units should treat a market's baseline late-payment rate as normal rather than a verdict on any one tenant, and build a consistent system instead: default autopay at lease signing, reminders sent before the due date, and a late fee charged the same amount every time."
detail: "BiggerPockets spoke with Ed Barone, co-founder of the rent-payment platform RentRedi, which serves close to 200,000 landlords and renters, about what its data shows small landlords misjudge. Barone said late-payment rates vary as much as 4x by state, running around 5% in states like Utah and Hawaii and as high as 20% in states like Mississippi. His point for small landlords: a late payment in a high-baseline market does not necessarily reflect a problem tenant or a landlord being too lenient, since some of that gap tracks to regional norms rather than individual behavior. The recommended fix does not change based on the local rate. Barone recommends three consistent practices: setting autopay as the default at lease signing, sending payment reminders before the due date rather than after, and charging a late fee that appears in the written lease and is applied the same amount every time it is triggered rather than inconsistently. He said inconsistent fee enforcement, charging some months and not others, is a common mistake among small landlords that undercuts the fee's effectiveness and can create legal exposure if challenged."
why_it_matters: "A Columbus landlord whose late-payment rate looks high next to national averages should check it against Ohio's regional baseline before assuming a tenant screening problem, and should audit whether their own late fee is actually charged every time it's triggered in the lease, since inconsistent enforcement is the specific mistake Barone flags as most common."
