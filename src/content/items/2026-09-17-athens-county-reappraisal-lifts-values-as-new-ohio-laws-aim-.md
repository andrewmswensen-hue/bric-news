title: "Athens County reappraisal lifts values as new Ohio laws aim to soften tax bumps"
summary: "Athens County homeowners are receiving 2026 reappraisal notices, and most values are up from the last reappraisal three years ago. A WOUB analysis of 170 single-family sales found homes sold for 19 percent more on average than the new appraised value. Higher bills start next year, and the county auditor says a further increase is likely at the next reappraisal unless prices fall."
detail: "According to WOUB, Athens County homeowners should now have letters from the county auditor with updated values from the 2026 reappraisal. For most homes the value is up from the last reappraisal three years ago, in many cases by tens of thousands of dollars. Higher property taxes based on the new values begin next year.\n\nWOUB analyzed 170 single-family sales from January through August and found the median sale price was $271,250. On average, homes sold for 19 percent more than the new appraised value, and nearly a third sold for more than $100,000 above their 2023 appraised value. County Auditor Jill Davidson said the office is working from sales that are two or three years old, does not inspect interiors, and prefers to stay under market value given economic volatility. She also cautioned that a homeowner who challenges a value that is already below the purchase price could see it move up to the sale price.\n\nThe article explains that Ohio property taxes are based on 35 percent of appraised value, then multiplied by the tax rate built from levies. It also describes the 20-mill floor for school operating levies, which stops levy rates from falling further as values rise, and notes that state lawmakers have passed measures meant to moderate the increases. Because appraisals trail sales, WOUB reports another large increase is likely at the next reappraisal if prices hold."
why_it_matters: "Columbus-area owners face the same mechanics: county values trail sale prices, taxes are figured on 35 percent of appraised value, and a school district at the 20-mill floor passes value gains straight into the bill. Landlords in Franklin, Delaware and Licking counties can use the Athens numbers as a preview of how a lagging reappraisal shows up in an operating budget."
