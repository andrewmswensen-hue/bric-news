title: "HUD proposes work requirements and time limits for housing vouchers"
summary: "The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development unveiled a proposed rule that would let local housing authorities require up to 40 hours of work per week and impose time limits of two years or more on able-bodied adults receiving federal rental assistance. HUD Secretary Scott Turner said seniors, children and people with disabilities would be exempt. The Housing Authority of Champaign County and the Vermilion Housing Authority, both in Illinois, became the first agencies to join HUD's new Work and Dignity Coalition. The rule must go through a public comment period before taking effect."
detail: "HUD's proposed rule would give local public housing agencies discretion to attach work requirements and time limits to Section 8 Housing Choice Vouchers and public housing, rather than mandating a single national standard. Agencies that opt in could require up to 40 hours per week of work-related activity and cap assistance at two years or more for able-bodied, non-elderly adults without disabilities.\n\nHUD cited federal data showing nearly half of non-elderly, able-bodied assisted households had no working adult in 2024, and said the average stay in major rental assistance programs has grown to eight or nine years, with about half of able-bodied voucher holders remaining more than 15 years. The agency estimated the changes could add up to $51 million in participant income nationally.\n\nThe rule would not force every housing authority to adopt work requirements immediately; it gives agencies the option, then goes through a federal public comment period before HUD finalizes it. HUD also launched a Work and Dignity Coalition to encourage adoption, with two Illinois agencies as founding members.\n\nIf finalized, the option would reach any Ohio public housing authority that chooses to use it, potentially changing how long some voucher holders stay eligible and how landlords participating in the voucher program see tenant turnover in those units."
why_it_matters: "If Ohio's public housing authorities adopt these optional time limits, landlords with voucher tenants could see more turnover in units currently occupied by long-term Section 8 holders, since HUD says some tenants have held vouchers for more than 15 years."
