title: "Grove City places data center ballot measure before voters Nov. 3"
summary: "Grove City Council voted unanimously Sept. 3 to place a resident-led charter amendment, the Community Consent Amendment, on the Nov. 3 ballot, after rejecting a shorter version sought by the business community. The measure would require a citywide vote before any project larger than 50 acres, using more than 20 megawatts of electricity, or using more than 500,000 gallons of water a day can proceed, a direct response to a proposed 300-acre Stream Data Centers campus near Rensch and Beatty roads."
detail: "The grassroots group Protect Grove City, led in part by resident Jen Belt, gathered the signatures needed to force the amendment to a council vote and to Franklin County Board of Elections certification for the ballot. The group says the amendment is not limited to data centers but would apply to any large-scale heavy industrial development, giving residents living near a proposed site direct approval power that currently rests with local government. The process began in April 2026 when Stream Data Centers proposed the 300-acre campus, and organizers say the fight is not over even with the ballot measure secured, since Grove City's business community has pushed for a narrower version limiting the vote requirement. If approved by voters, the amendment would apply citywide, not only to the Rensch and Beatty roads site."
why_it_matters: "For an investor evaluating land near a planned industrial or data center site in a Franklin County suburb, Grove City's vote shows a resident petition can force a citywide referendum requirement on any large future development, not just data centers, once it clears the local charter amendment process."
