detail: "Circleville City Council voted Sept. 1 to annex 369 acres for Scioto Preserve, a planned development of more than 1,200 single-family homes and apartments spanning over 314 acres, with open space, a clubhouse, pool, playgrounds, and walking paths. The annexation could grow the city's population by as much as 10 percent. Residents including David White and Kindra Nye say the project moved faster than the city explained, and worry about traffic, overcrowded schools, and strain on emergency services and utilities. They have organized as Neighbors for Responsible Growth-Scioto Preserve and are collecting signatures to place the annexation on the November ballot, with a deadline of Oct. 1 to qualify. The Circleville City School District Board of Education passed a resolution last month stating it will not support residential tax abatements for the project unless the district is fully compensated for added students, warning that uncompensated growth could lead to new levies. Developers have said publicly they intend to negotiate tax incentives with the city during the rezoning process. City officials had not responded to requests for comment as of the story's publication. If the petition does not gather enough valid signatures, the city's planning and zoning commission will take up the development's site plan on Oct. 7."
