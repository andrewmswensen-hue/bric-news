detail: "A full-time Ohio worker needs to earn at least $23.82 an hour to afford a \"modest\" two-bedroom apartment without spending more than 30% of income on rent and utilities, according to the 2026 Out of Reach report from the National Low Income Housing Coalition and the Coalition on Homelessness and Housing in Ohio (COHHIO). That statewide Housing Wage is up from $22.51 a year earlier, a roughly 5% increase, and has risen 169% since 2020, when the gap between the Housing Wage and the average renter wage was $1.57 an hour.\n\nThe Housing Wage varies by metro area: $27.50 an hour in Columbus, $26.02 in Cincinnati, $24.60 in Cleveland and $24.48 in Dayton, with Union County recording the state's highest figure at $27.56. Ohio's statewide Fair Market Rent, the benchmark HUD uses to calculate the wage, is $1,238 a month for a two-bedroom unit and $996 for a one-bedroom.\n\nThe average Ohio renter earns $19.59 an hour, $4.23 below the statewide two-bedroom threshold, according to the report, which also estimates a shortage of 266,000 affordable and available rental units statewide. COHHIO executive director Amy Riegel said in a statement that renters' income gains in the early 2020s have since been outpaced by rent increases."
