title: "Ohio Supreme Court keeps Wilmington data center ordinance off the ballot"
summary: "The Ohio Supreme Court ruled Friday that a proposed Wilmington ordinance regulating data centers will not appear on the Nov. 3 ballot. The court said the measure exceeded municipal authority because it created a private right for residents to sue data center operators over alleged zoning violations. The Clinton County Board of Elections had voted in August not to certify the initiative. Justice Patrick F. Fischer dissented."
detail: "A proposed Wilmington ordinance on data centers will not go before voters on Nov. 3 after the Ohio Supreme Court upheld the Clinton County Board of Elections, according to WKRC. The elections board had verified enough valid signatures, but voted unanimously in August not to certify the initiative after the city law director raised concerns about one provision.\n\nThat provision would have let affected residents sue data center operators over alleged zoning violations. A court could then have imposed daily fines, suspended a certificate of occupancy, or revoked the conditional use of the property. The ordinance would also have set standards for noise, lighting, emissions, generator testing, water use, wastewater discharge, and heat generation.\n\nThe Supreme Court said municipalities cannot create a new private cause of action through a ballot initiative and denied the request from supporters to order the measure onto the ballot. Justice Patrick F. Fischer dissented, writing that courts, not election boards, should decide the legality of a proposed ordinance after voters approve it."
why_it_matters: "The ruling sets a limit on what a citizen-written zoning initiative can contain in Ohio, which matters for anyone tracking land use fights as data center measures reach ballots in other communities."
