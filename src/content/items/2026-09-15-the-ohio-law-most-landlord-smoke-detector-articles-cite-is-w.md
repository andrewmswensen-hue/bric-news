title: "The Ohio law most landlord smoke detector articles cite is wrong"
summary: "Ohio rentals must have working smoke alarms under the Ohio Fire Code, not the building-height statute, ORC 3781.104, many landlord guides cite, which applies only to buildings over 75 feet tall. Carbon monoxide alarms are required when a unit meets any of four conditions in fire code section 915, including a fuel-burning forced-air furnace or an attached garage, each triggering the requirement on its own. ORC 5321.04 turns those code provisions into a landlord duty, and a violation can count as negligence per se in court."
detail: "The citation most landlord content uses for Ohio smoke detector law, ORC 3781.104, took effect in 1981 and applies only to apartment buildings and condominiums over 75 feet tall, not a single-family rental in Westerville or a four-unit building in Clintonville. The rules that actually reach most Ohio rentals live in the Ohio Fire Code, OAC 1301:7-7-11, which requires working smoke alarms in rental dwelling units.\n\nCarbon monoxide alarms are required only when a unit meets at least one of four separate conditions listed in fire code section 915. A fuel-burning forced-air furnace triggers the requirement on its own, as does an attached private garage, meaning a unit can require a CO alarm for either reason independent of the other. Existing buildings faced a January 1, 2019 deadline to comply.\n\nORC 5321.04(A)(1) and (A)(2) convert those fire code requirements into an enforceable landlord duty: comply with applicable building, housing, health and safety codes, and keep the property in a fit and habitable condition. Because of that statute, a missing or non-working detector can support a finding of negligence per se in an Ohio courtroom, meaning the code violation itself can establish the landlord breached its duty of care.\n\nColumbus adds its own layer. City code requires hard-wired detectors, not battery-only units, in one- to three-family buildings built after April 26, 1978, and splits responsibility in writing: the owner installs the detector under CCC 4529.08, while in a one- to three-family dwelling the occupant is responsible for operation and maintenance under CCC 4529.11. Battery-only alarms remain permitted under the state fire code in older existing buildings where no construction is underway."
why_it_matters: "An Ohio landlord who cites ORC 3781.104 in a lease addendum or eviction filing is citing a law that covers only buildings over 75 feet tall, so relying on it to document compliance in a typical Columbus rental could undercut the landlord's own negligence per se defense if a detector-related claim goes to court."
