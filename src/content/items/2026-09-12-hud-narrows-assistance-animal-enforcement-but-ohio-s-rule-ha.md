title: "HUD narrows assistance animal enforcement, but Ohio's rule hasn't changed"
summary: "A partner explainer from RL Property Management lays out where a May 22, 2026 HUD enforcement memo leaves Ohio landlords on assistance animal requests. The memo narrowed federal enforcement to animals individually trained to perform a task tied to a disability, but it changed HUD's own enforcement posture, not the Fair Housing Act itself, private lawsuits, or Ohio's separate civil rights rule. Ohio Administrative Code 4112-5-07 still protects any animal that aids a person with a disability, with no training requirement, and still bars charging extra fees for one."
detail: "The shift traces back to three federal actions over eighteen months, the explainer says. On September 17, 2025, HUD withdrew eight fair housing guidance documents, including the framework providers had used since 2020 to evaluate assistance animal requests. A Federal Register notice formalized that withdrawal on April 6, 2026. Then, on May 22, 2026, HUD Assistant Secretary for Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity Craig Trainor issued a memo narrowing the agency's own enforcement to animals individually trained to perform a task tied to a disability. That memo changed HUD's enforcement posture, not the underlying Fair Housing Act statute, and it cannot bind a court hearing a private lawsuit. Ohio Administrative Code 4112-5-07(C) separately protects any \"animal assistant,\" defined as any animal that aids a person with a disability, without using the word \"trained,\" and it still bars landlords from charging extra fees for one. As of September 2026, the Ohio Civil Rights Commission, a HUD-funded fair housing agency that applies Ohio's own standard, had published no position on the federal change. Direct threat and substantial property damage remain valid grounds for denial under both federal and Ohio law, but each requires evidence specific to the animal in question rather than a blanket policy. For an Ohio owner, the explainer frames the practical line this way: a trained service animal is an accommodation rather than a pet, so pet fees do not apply to it, while an emotional support animal now sits in genuinely contested territory, since HUD stopped enforcing on untrained ESAs in May 2026 while Ohio's own civil rights rule, protecting any animal that aids a person with a disability and barring extra charges for one, did not change alongside it. Owner-trained animals count under the standard HUD adopted, since there is no certification requirement."
why_it_matters: "A Columbus landlord who starts denying emotional support animal requests solely because HUD stopped requiring training documentation could still run afoul of Ohio Administrative Code 4112-5-07, which protects any animal that aids a person with a disability regardless of training, so a categorical denial policy carries legal risk under state law even after the federal enforcement shift."
