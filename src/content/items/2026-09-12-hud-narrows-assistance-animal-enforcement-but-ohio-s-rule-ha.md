title: "HUD narrows assistance animal enforcement, but Ohio's rule has not moved"
summary: "A HUD memo issued May 22, 2026, narrowed federal enforcement of assistance animal requests under the Fair Housing Act to animals individually trained to perform a task tied to a disability, but it did not amend the underlying statute and cannot bind Ohio courts. Ohio Administrative Code 4112-5-07 still protects any animal assistant that aids a person with a disability and bars extra pet charges for one, without requiring training."
detail: "HUD's shift did not happen in a single step. On September 17, 2025, HUD withdrew eight fair housing guidance documents, including FHEO Notice 2020-01, the framework providers had used since January 2020 to evaluate assistance animal requests. HUD followed with additional actions in April and May 2026, culminating in a May 22, 2026 memo that narrows the agency's own enforcement posture to animals individually trained to perform a task tied to a disability, effectively separating trained service animals from untrained emotional support animals in HUD's enforcement priorities.\n\nThat change reaches HUD enforcement only. It does not amend the Fair Housing Act statute, does not bind private lawsuits, and does not touch Ohio Administrative Code 4112-5-07(C), which protects any animal assistant, defined as any animal that aids a person with a disability, without using the word trained, and separately bars charging extra fees for one. The Ohio Civil Rights Commission enforces that state standard and, as of September 2026, has not published guidance addressing the gap between it and HUD's narrower federal posture.\n\nThe result is contested territory specifically around untrained emotional support animals: HUD's own enforcement has stepped back, but Ohio's civil rights rule has not changed, and an owner could still face a state fair housing claim over how a request was handled. Owner-trained animals qualify under the standard HUD adopted, since no certification is required. Denying a request categorically, without an individualized review, remains the riskiest response under either standard, and direct threat or substantial physical damage still require case-specific evidence to justify a denial."
why_it_matters: "Until the Ohio Civil Rights Commission states its own position, a Columbus owner who denies an emotional support animal request based only on HUD's narrower federal stance is relying on a standard Ohio's civil rights rule has not adopted, which keeps that owner exposed to a state fair housing complaint even where a HUD complaint would now fail."
