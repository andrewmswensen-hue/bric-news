title: "Treasury plans to exempt build-to-rent from investor home-buying ban"
summary: "The U.S. Treasury Department proposed carving build-to-rent communities out of the ROAD Act's ban on large investors buying single-family homes, which takes effect January 7, 2027 and otherwise blocks investors who own 350 or more homes from acquiring more. Under the proposed framework, a purpose-built rental community sold to an institutional buyer within 18 months of its certificate of occupancy would be exempt, as long as the homes are for rent to individual tenants rather than resale or land banking."
detail: "The Road to Housing Act's final version bars investors owning 350 or more single-family homes from buying additional ones starting January 7, 2027, but a coalition of rental housing groups had pushed Treasury to exempt build-to-rent developments since the law passed, warning that financing for new build-to-rent projects was drying up under the threat of a broad ban. Treasury's proposed rulemaking would grant that exemption: a build-to-rent community that has never been owner-occupied and is sold to an institutional buyer within 18 months of receiving its certificate of occupancy would fall outside the investor ban, provided the homes are rented to individual tenants and not held for resale, land banking, or speculative appreciation. The exemption covers communities that mix renter-occupied and owner-occupied homes, which matters because many homebuilders sell part of a completed community to a large investor for rental use while selling the rest to individual buyers. The 18-month window creates a hard deadline: a builder who delivers a build-to-rent project in 2027 and holds it for several years before selling to a large investor would fall outside the exemption, and the buyer would not be eligible to purchase it once that investor already owns 350 or more homes. The proposal follows HUD's move last week to make build-to-rent communities eligible for financing insured through its FHA multifamily mortgage programs, which together signal the Trump administration is treating institutional investment in purpose built rental housing differently from investor purchases of existing single-family homes."
why_it_matters: "A Columbus-area investor looking to buy into a local build-to-rent project, or sell one to an institutional buyer, should track the January 2027 effective date and the 18-month certificate-of-occupancy window, since missing it could make a planned sale to a large investor illegal under the ban."
