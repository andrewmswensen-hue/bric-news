title: "Columbus proposes water surcharge for its highest-usage customers"
summary: "Columbus Water and Power submitted a proposed rate structure Monday that would add a surcharge for roughly 426 commercial customers whose water use spikes well above typical levels, following an Oct. 26 deadline set by city council, according to WSYX. Customers using at least 100 hundred cubic feet a month, about 15 times an average single-family home's usage, and peaking at twice their normal demand would pay $1.62 per hundred cubic feet, raising an estimated $6 million."
detail: "Columbus Water and Power proposed the new rate structure after an Oct. 26 deadline set by the city's Public Utilities and Sustainability Committee, which has been examining how to keep rising infrastructure costs from falling on typical households and small businesses. The surcharge would apply to commercial customers using at least 100 hundred cubic feet (74,800 gallons) a month with a peak-demand factor of twice their normal usage, a threshold the utility's deputy director Jon Lee said is meant to recover the treatment, pumping, storage and distribution capacity the system must maintain to handle those peaks. About 426 customers would currently qualify, generating an estimated $6 million a year at the proposed $1.62 per hundred cubic feet rate, according to WSYX. The proposal does not identify which customers would be affected or set a separate electricity rate for high-demand users; Columbus Councilman Christopher Wyche said the city wants flexibility to work with customers before applying the surcharge. Revenue from the new charge would let standard water rates run about 1.6% lower than they otherwise would, partly offsetting the city's planned 18% water rate increase set for 2027. A public hearing is scheduled for Nov. 12, with council votes expected in two readings on Nov. 16 and Nov. 23."
why_it_matters: "The 18% water rate increase coming in 2027 is happening regardless of this surcharge, which only shaves about 1.6% off standard rates, so budget utility pass-throughs or CAM charges for next year around the full increase, not the offset."
