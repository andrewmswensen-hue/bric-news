detail: "Hilliard City Council voted 7-0 on Oct. 26 to cancel a 12 year, 50% property tax abatement that had applied to Ten Pin Alley since 2017, after the bowling alley's current operator failed to meet a payroll commitment tied to the deal. Under the original Community Reinvestment Area agreement, the business's then owner, Purdy Bowl LLC, committed to a $7 million renovation, retaining 30 jobs, adding 20 new jobs and reaching a combined payroll of $1.13 million in exchange for the abatement, which applies only to the added value from improvements, not the full property.\n\nHilliard's Tax Incentive Review Council found in August that current operator Lucky Strike Entertainment was more than $339,000 short of the payroll requirement with no plans to add staff, after the city had already given the business a one year grace period in 2024 when Lucky Strike took over. The property has received $1,072,700 in abatements since the agreement began. The council canceled the abatement without further deliberation."
why_it_matters: "This is the enforcement mechanism behind every Community Reinvestment Area deal made visible: Hilliard tracked a payroll target for eight years and pulled the abatement the year the numbers came up short, the same scrutiny any investor holding a CRA agreement elsewhere in Franklin County should expect."
