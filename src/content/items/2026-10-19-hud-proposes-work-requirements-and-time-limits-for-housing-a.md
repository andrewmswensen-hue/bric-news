title: "HUD proposes work requirements and time limits for housing aid"
summary: "The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development proposed a rule on Oct. 16 letting local public housing authorities require up to 40 hours of work per week and set two-year-plus time limits for able bodied adults on federal rental assistance. HUD Secretary Scott Turner announced the rule in Champaign, Illinois, where the Housing Authority of Champaign County and the Vermilion Housing Authority became the first agencies to join HUD's new Work and Dignity Coalition. Seniors, children and people with disabilities are exempt."
detail: "HUD unveiled the proposed rule Oct. 16 at an event in Champaign, Illinois, where Secretary Scott Turner and Assistant Secretary Benjamin Hobbs framed it as a shift toward work based housing assistance. The rule would give local public housing authorities the option, not a mandate, to require able bodied adults to work up to 40 hours a week and to cap assistance at two years or longer, with specifics left to each authority. Seniors, minors and people with disabilities are excluded from any work requirement. HUD, citing federal data, said nearly half of non elderly, able bodied assisted households had no working adult in 2024, and that average tenure in major rental assistance programs has grown to eight or nine years, with about half of able bodied Section 8 recipients staying more than 15 years. The Housing Authority of Champaign County and the Vermilion Housing Authority were named the first two agencies nationally to join HUD's new Work and Dignity Coalition, which encourages local agencies to adopt similar work focused policies voluntarily. The rule must go through a federal public comment period before any housing authority could put it into effect."
why_it_matters: "Because the rule is opt-in for local public housing authorities, Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority would have to adopt it before any Section 8 work requirement applies here, but landlords who accept vouchers should expect new eligibility paperwork and possible tenant turnover if CMHA opts in."
