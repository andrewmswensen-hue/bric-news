title: "Ohio Supreme Court Backs Elections Board in Wilmington Data Center Ballot Fight"
summary: "The Ohio Supreme Court ruled 6-1 that the Clinton County Board of Elections properly excluded a citizen initiative from the November ballot that would have added data center zoning rules, including a right for residents to sue over violations, to Wilmington's code. The justices found the city lacked legal authority to create that private right of action. The initiative was a response to a proposed $4 billion Amazon data center; organizers say they will revise the language and resubmit it. The ruling leaves the board's gatekeeping power over petition initiatives intact."
detail: "The Ohio Supreme Court ruled 6-1 in favor of the Clinton County Board of Elections in a dispute over a citizen petition to add data center zoning regulations to Wilmington's municipal code. The board had rejected the initiative before residents appealed to the court. The proposed ordinance would have set rules on noise, setbacks from homes, wastewater chemical documentation, and generator use for data centers, and would have let residents sue a data center over zoning violations. The majority found Wilmington lacked legal authority to create that private right to sue, and under Ohio law a petition cannot be altered after it is filed, so the flawed section could not simply be removed. Justice Patrick Fischer dissented, writing that boards of elections lack authority to decide the question the majority resolved. The initiative responded to a proposed $4 billion Amazon data center in Wilmington; organizers, who said they gathered enough signatures with help from legal counsel and a certified planner, plan to revise the ordinance language and restart signature gathering for a future ballot. The ruling does not change Wilmington's existing zoning code, but it confirms that boards of elections can screen local zoning initiatives for legal defects, including private enforcement provisions, before voters see them."
why_it_matters: "The ruling confirms that an Ohio board of elections can keep a zoning initiative off the ballot if it includes a private right to sue, a limit any Columbus-area group drafting a citizen petition to add enforcement teeth to local zoning or land use rules now has to design around."
