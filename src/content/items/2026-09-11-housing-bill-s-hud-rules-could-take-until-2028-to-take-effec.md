summary: "A Bipartisan Policy Center and National Association of Affordable Housing Lenders panel detailed why the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, the first major federal housing law in decades, will take years to implement. detail: "The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act became law this year, but panelists at a Bipartisan Policy Center session said most of its provisions will take one to several years to reach renters, buyers or housing providers. HUD accounts for about 70% of the law's 125 identified federal implementation actions and must write nine new rules, a process that historically runs 18 months to two years, pushing some rules into early 2028. HUD's staffing has fallen to about 5,800 employees from 8,800 in fiscal year 2024.\n\nCongress has not funded roughly a dozen new programs the law authorized. The House's fiscal year 2027 transportation and housing appropriations bill, passed before ROAD became law, does not fund the ROAD provisions, and the Senate's bill remains pending.\n\nOne change likely to move fastest: the law raised the cap on bank public welfare investments, which fund community development projects in low- and moderate-income areas, from 15% to 20% of a bank's capital and surplus. A slower but more consequential change would let HUD drop the permanent chassis requirement from its manufactured housing definition, opening the door to multi-story manufactured housing in denser markets, once HUD completes a new rulemaking and local zoning catches up."
why_it_matters: "Ohio manufactured housing communities and multifamily lenders should not expect the law's supply-side changes, including the manufactured housing redesign rule and updated HOME program limits, to show up in practice before 2027 or 2028, since HUD's own rulemaking queue is the bottleneck."
