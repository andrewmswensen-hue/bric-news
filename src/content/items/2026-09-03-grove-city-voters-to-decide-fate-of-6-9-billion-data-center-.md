title: "Grove City voters to decide fate of $6.9 billion data center project"
summary: "Grove City's November ballot will include a citizen-initiated charter amendment that would require voter approval for any development of 50 or more acres, 20 megawatts of power use, or 500,000 gallons of daily water use, a measure aimed at a proposed 300-acre, $6.9 billion data center campus, according to the Times of India, citing Axios. The opposition group Protect Grove City collected more than 2,000 signatures after city council enacted a one-year moratorium on data center projects in response to resident backlash over the proposed campus."
detail: "The proposed data center campus would sit next to a 345-kilovolt electrical substation, a location developers said made the site well suited for a power-intensive facility. Opponents have raised concerns about electricity demand, water use, noise, backup generators, traffic and the long-term character of Grove City, and pushed the city council to enact a one-year moratorium covering all data center projects, including the $6.9 billion campus.\n\nProtect Grove City then drafted a charter amendment requiring a public referendum for any development of 50 or more acres, 20 megawatts of power use, or 500,000 gallons of daily water use, with the developer responsible for the cost of the ballot measure. The group gathered more than 2,000 signatures, enough to force city council to place the amendment on the November ballot.\n\nCity and regional economic development leaders oppose the measure. Grove City Council President Ted Berry told Axios the amendment 'would literally kill the city.' Former U.S. Rep. Steve Stivers, now president and CEO of the Ohio Chamber of Commerce, argued the measure's reach extends well beyond data centers to any large development. The vote comes as Ohio communities increasingly use ballot measures and moratoriums to slow data center construction, echoing a similar fight over a charter amendment the Ohio Supreme Court ordered onto the ballot in Trenton, Ohio, this month."
why_it_matters: "The referendum threshold in this amendment, 50 acres or 20 megawatts, would apply to any large development in Grove City, not just data centers, so an investor planning a sizable multifamily or industrial project there after November would need to clear a public vote first if the measure passes."
