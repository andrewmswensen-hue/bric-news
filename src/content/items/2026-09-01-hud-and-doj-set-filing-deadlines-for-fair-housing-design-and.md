title: "HUD and DOJ set filing deadlines for Fair Housing design and construction claims"
summary: "HUD and the Justice Department jointly rescinded 2013 guidance that let accessibility design and construction complaints under the Fair Housing Act be filed indefinitely against whoever owned a building, including owners who did not build it. Under the replacement framework the clock starts at the certificate of occupancy: one year to file an administrative complaint with HUD, two years for a private suit in federal court. HUD said the prior reading drove more than $112 million in accessibility retrofits over five years for owners seeking FHA refinancing."
detail: "The Department of Housing and Urban Development and the Department of Justice rescinded a 2013 policy that treated a builder's deviation from HUD accessibility guidelines as an ongoing violation, exposing later owners to claims years after construction. According to Fox News, which reported the action first, the replacement guidance ties the limitations period to the date a building receives its certificate of occupancy. Administrative complaints to HUD must be filed within one year of that date, and private suits in federal court within two years. After those windows close, owners are no longer subject to building-wide retroactive liability for original design flaws, though individual residents may still request reasonable modifications for their own units under the Fair Housing Act. HUD said internal data showed owners spent more than $112 million on accessibility retrofits over the last five years in order to qualify for FHA refinancing, and that one inspection firm found nearly $49 million in required fixes across roughly 500 refinance deals since 2019, an average above $100,000 per property. HUD Secretary Scott Turner said the earlier guidance created liability with no basis in law. Assistant Secretary Craig Trainor said Congress wrote a one-year limit into the statute in 1988. HUD also pointed to a Ninth Circuit ruling holding that a failure to properly design a building ends when construction is finished and occupancy is certified."
why_it_matters: "For a Columbus multifamily owner refinancing with FHA-insured debt, accessibility deficiencies left by the original builder were a common reason a deal stalled or came with a six-figure retrofit bill. Under the new deadlines that exposure closes two years after the certificate of occupancy, which shifts the diligence question on an older building from what the builder did to when the building was finished."
