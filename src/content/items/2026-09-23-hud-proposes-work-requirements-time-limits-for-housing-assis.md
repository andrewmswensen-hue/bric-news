title: "HUD Proposes Work Requirements, Time Limits for Housing Assistance"
summary: "HUD unveiled a proposed rule Sept. 19 that would let local public housing authorities require up to 40 hours a week of work and impose time limits of two years or more on able-bodied adults receiving federal housing assistance, including Section 8 vouchers. Seniors, children and people with disabilities would be exempt. HUD Secretary Scott Turner said the rule targets long stays in subsidized housing, citing federal data showing nearly half of non-elderly, able-bodied assisted households had no working adult in 2024. The rule must clear a public comment period before taking effect."
detail: "HUD Secretary Scott Turner announced the proposed rule at an event in Champaign, Illinois, framing it as a self-sufficiency measure rather than a benefit cut. Under the proposal, public housing agencies would gain the option, not the mandate, to require up to 40 hours a week of work and to cap assistance at two years or more for able-bodied adults, while preserving current protections for elderly, disabled and family households with children. Turner cited federal data showing the average stay in major rental assistance programs has grown to nearly eight or nine years, with roughly half of able-bodied Section 8 voucher recipients staying more than 15 years. HUD simultaneously launched a \"Work and Dignity Coalition\" to encourage local housing authorities to adopt work-focused policies voluntarily. The Housing Authority of Champaign County and the Vermilion Housing Authority became the first two agencies in the country to join, saying they already run self-sufficiency programs under existing federal flexibility. The rule is not final. It must go through a public comment period, and because it grants authority rather than requiring its use, actual implementation would depend on each local housing authority's own board deciding whether to adopt work requirements for its voucher and public housing programs."
why_it_matters: "A Columbus landlord who accepts housing choice vouchers would not see an immediate change: the rule gives the Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority the option to adopt work requirements, not a mandate, so any effect on tenant eligibility and turnover depends on a decision CMHA's board has not yet made."
