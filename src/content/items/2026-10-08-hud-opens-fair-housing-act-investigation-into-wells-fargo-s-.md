summary: "The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development has opened an investigation into Wells Fargo, alleging the bank's marketing campaigns and lending programs aimed at increasing Black homeownership may have violated the Fair Housing Act by offering different loan products or terms based on race, according to Scotsman Guide. HUD's Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity cited Wells Fargo's own public reports promoting diversity, equity and inclusion programs as the basis for the probe, in a letter sent to CEO Charlie Scharf. Wells Fargo had no immediate comment."
detail: "HUD has launched a civil rights investigation into Wells Fargo, alleging the bank's marketing and lending programs aimed at boosting Black homeownership amounted to race-based lending in violation of the Fair Housing Act, Scotsman Guide reported. Craig Trainor, assistant secretary leading HUD's Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity, wrote to Wells Fargo Chairman and CEO Charlie Scharf that the bank's repeated public promotion of what HUD called explicit race-based lending policies prompted the agency's Office of Special Investigations to open the inquiry.\n\nHUD traces the allegations back to 2017, describing a pattern it calls a deliberate campaign to implement a racialized home loan policy by offering different products or terms based on race. The agency pointed to Wells Fargo's own annual reports celebrating diversity, equity and inclusion programs, the kind of programs President Trump has moved to ban across federal hiring, contracting and spending through executive orders.\n\nA Wells Fargo spokesperson told Scotsman Guide the bank had no immediate comment. The bank has scaled back its mortgage origination footprint in recent years but remains a major holder of mortgage-backed securities, with Ginnie Mae data showing nearly $280 billion in holdings as of the second quarter of 2026, roughly double JPMorgan Chase's position."
why_it_matters: "HUD's renewed enforcement push is a reminder that fair housing law cuts both ways: a housing provider's marketing, screening, or pricing criteria can draw federal scrutiny whether it disadvantages or singles out applicants by race, which matters for any landlord running targeted outreach or promotions."
