summary: "Delaware County commissioners renewed the county's 0.5-mill property tax rollback for a fifth consecutive year. The renewal provides close to $7.1 million in tax relief to county property owners in the coming tax year. Rollbacks over the prior four years delivered more than $24 million, bringing the running total to roughly $32 million. Board President Jeff Benton attributed the renewal to the county's fiscal management while continuing investment in roads, sewers and facilities. Commissioner Gary Merrell described the action as the county reducing its inside-millage collections."
detail: "Delaware County first enacted the 0.5-mill rollback four years ago and has now renewed it for a fifth year. Spectrum News reported the renewal delivers nearly $7.1 million in relief to Delaware County property owners during the upcoming tax year, on top of more than $24 million provided over the previous four years, for a cumulative figure approaching $32 million. Board of Commissioners President Jeff Benton said fiscal management made the fifth consecutive rollback possible while the county continues to plan and invest in road, sewer and facility improvements and maintain service levels. Commissioner Barb Lewis cited the cost of living in supporting the reduction. Commissioner Gary Merrell framed the action in mechanical terms: the county is reducing its inside-millage collections, the portion of property tax levied without a vote, and he noted this comes on top of other reductions approved over the past thirty years. The rollback applies to the county's own millage and does not change millage levied by schools, townships, municipalities or other taxing authorities within Delaware County."
why_it_matters: "The relief comes out of the county's inside millage, which is the slice of a Delaware County tax bill that is not voted on, so it lands on every parcel regardless of school district or municipality. At roughly $7.1 million countywide it is a modest per-parcel effect, but it is one of the few line items moving down while valuations move up."
