detail: "Property insurance costs reached another record in the second quarter of 2026, according to the ICE Mortgage Monitor. The average mortgage holder with a single-family home now pays $209 a month for coverage, up 8.7% from a year earlier and up nearly 80% since the start of 2020. Insurance now represents 9.6% of the average monthly mortgage payment, a share lenders must account for when calculating a borrower's debt-to-income ratio. The report found homeowners who switched private insurance carriers in the past year cut their premiums by an average of 6.6% while also increasing coverage limits by 7.3% and lowering deductibles. Those who stayed with their existing carrier instead saw premiums rise 10.4%, a gap ICE put at about $440 a year, or roughly $37 a month. Premium growth has slowed from a peak of 15.1% at the end of 2024, but increases remain uneven by market: Greenville, South Carolina and Honolulu posted the largest annual jumps, while Miami and New Orleans, the two most expensive insurance markets, saw smaller increases despite already high costs. ICE said higher coverage limits, not just insurer repricing, now account for most of the nationwide increase."
