title: "Pataskala voters to decide charter ban on large data centers Nov. 3"
summary: "Pataskala and Granville residents vote Nov. 3 on charter amendments banning data centers over 25 megawatts. In Pataskala, the measure follows City Council's unanimous August rejection of a 200-megawatt Aligned Data Centers proposal for the city's corporate park; the developer has asked a judge to review that denial. Conserve Ohio backed the petition drives in both communities. An attorney for the group says Ohio law does not clearly resolve what happens if a court overturns the council's rejection after voters approve the charter ban."
detail: "Pataskala and Granville voters will decide Nov. 3 whether to amend their municipal charters to prohibit construction of data centers drawing more than 25 megawatts of power. Neither community currently has a data center, but Pataskala is fighting a specific proposal: Aligned Data Centers sought to build a 200-megawatt facility in the city's corporate park, and City Council voted unanimously in August to reject the site plan. Aligned Data Centers asked a Licking County court in September to review that denial. At-large council member Tim Hickin said residents have mixed views and should weigh both the financial upside and the power, water, and noise drawbacks before voting. Charter committee member Leatrice Guttentag said she believes most residents oppose the project outright. Both ballot measures were organized through Conserve Ohio, the grassroots group that has backed similar petitions across Licking County after a statewide ban effort fell short of signature requirements. Conserve Ohio attorney Nick Owens said Ohio law offers little precedent on how a charter ban would interact with a developer's vested property rights if the pending court appeal overturns the council's denial after the charter amendment passes, calling it a legal question a court would ultimately have to settle."
why_it_matters: "If the charter amendment passes and the pending court appeal later overturns Pataskala's council denial, investors with land near the proposed site should expect the resulting legal fight over vested property rights to decide whether that 200-megawatt project, and the land values tied to it, can still move forward."
