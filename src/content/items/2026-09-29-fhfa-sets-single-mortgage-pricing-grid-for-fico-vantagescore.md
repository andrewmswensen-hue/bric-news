detail: "In September, FHFA let Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac approved lenders choose either Classic FICO or VantageScore 4.0 for eligible conventional loans, but required a 20-point downward adjustment to VantageScore results so pricing matched FICO's scale. The pricing grid FHFA Director Bill Pulte released this week removes that 20-point adjustment, so both scoring models now plug into the same loan-level price adjustment (LLPA) grid without a correction factor. Lenders must still apply one scoring model consistently across every borrower on a given loan; Classic FICO remains the required model for loans underwritten manually rather than through automated systems. FICO 10T, a newer version of the FICO model, has not been approved for Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac delivery, though the Federal Housing Administration plans to begin using it for underwriting starting January 2027, and the GSEs have released historical data to prepare for an eventual switch. Because VantageScore 4.0 has historically produced higher scores than Classic FICO for the same borrowers, removing the adjustment raises an open question about how the two models will price loans differently once lenders can choose between them under the same grid."
why_it_matters: "Columbus investors financing a rental purchase through a conventional Fannie or Freddie loan may now see different pricing depending on whether their lender pulls a Classic FICO or a VantageScore 4.0 score, since the adjustment that used to equalize the two is gone."
