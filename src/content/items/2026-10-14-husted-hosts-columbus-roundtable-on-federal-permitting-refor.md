title: "Husted hosts Columbus roundtable on federal permitting reform"
summary: "U.S. Sen. Jon Husted (R-Ohio) hosted Senate Majority Whip John Barrasso (R-Wyo.) in Columbus for a roundtable with Ohio energy, manufacturing and business leaders on federal permitting reform. Participants discussed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, which would set one year deadlines for environmental assessments and two year deadlines for full environmental impact statements, limit when lawsuits can be filed against permits, and require data centers using 20 megawatts or more to cover their own energy costs."
detail: "Sen. Jon Husted's office said the Columbus roundtable brought together state lawmakers, regulators, and Ohio energy, manufacturing and business leaders to discuss the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, a Senate bill meant to speed up federal permitting for energy and infrastructure projects. Under current law, a federal environmental review can take up to a decade, according to the release. The bill would require environmental assessments within one year and full environmental impact statements within two years, limit legal challenges under the National Environmental Policy Act to claims filed within 150 days, and restrict when federal agencies can pull or suspend an issued permit. It would also apply a version of Husted's own Ratepayer Protection Act, requiring data centers that use 20 megawatts or more of power to cover their own energy costs rather than passing those costs to other ratepayers, while leaving Ohio in charge of siting transmission lines and setting data center rates. The bill is expected to get a Senate vote after the chamber returns from its October work period. Husted sits on the Senate Environment and Public Works Committee."
why_it_matters: "If the bill passes, requiring large data centers to pay their own power costs could help hold down the electric rate increases Columbus-area landlords have been watching as data center demand grows on the local grid, since it blocks utilities from spreading those costs to other ratepayers."
