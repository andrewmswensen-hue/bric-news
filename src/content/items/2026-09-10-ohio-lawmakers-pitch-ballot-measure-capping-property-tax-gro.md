detail: "State Reps. Jeff LaRe, a Republican from Violet Township, and Kevin Miller, a Republican from Newark, are pitching a constitutional amendment that would cap how quickly a home's taxable value can rise for tax purposes, according to the Columbus Dispatch. Under the proposal, increases in a home's appraised value would be tied to inflation and capped at 3 percent a year. Any value growth beyond that cap would be exempt from taxation for as long as the owner, or the owner's spouse, continues living in the home. Because the exemption depends on continued owner occupancy, the proposal as described would not extend to a non owner occupied rental property. The pitch follows a broader wave of Ohio property tax activity this year: five other companion bills already took effect adjusting valuation growth caps, the state's 20 mill floor calculation, and homestead exemption eligibility, and a separate citizen campaign is gathering signatures to abolish property taxes outright. As a constitutional amendment, the LaRe-Miller proposal would need to qualify for the ballot and be approved by Ohio voters before taking effect, and no timeline for that vote has been set."
