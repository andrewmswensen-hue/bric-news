title: "Worthington group submits 3,976 signatures to force zoning vote"
summary: "Democracy Before Developers, a Worthington citizen group, submitted 3,976 petition signatures on September 21 seeking to put Worthington City Council's July 20 rezoning vote to a public referendum. The rezoning allows a $52.4 million, 248-unit housing development on roughly 20 acres at 445 E. Dublin Granville Road, land owned by nonprofit I Am Boundless. Ohio law requires 3,017 valid signatures in Worthington to qualify a referendum; the group needs the Franklin County Board of Elections to validate the signatures before the measure can appear on the November 2027 ballot."
detail: "Worthington City Council voted July 20 to rezone about 20 acres at 445 E. Dublin Granville Road, land owned by disability nonprofit I Am Boundless, to allow a 248-unit, $52.4 million housing development. Citizen group Democracy Before Developers opposed the rezoning and launched a petition drive on July 23 seeking a public referendum on the council's decision. The group submitted 3,976 signatures to the city on September 21, well above the 3,017 required under Ohio's referendum threshold of 35 percent of the votes cast in Worthington's last gubernatorial election.\n\nThe city will hold the signatures for 10 days before forwarding them to the Franklin County Board of Elections, which validates signatures and certifies the ballot issue. Campaign coordinator Marty Shumway estimated 85 to 90 percent of the signatures will prove valid. I Am Boundless plans to sell part of its land to developer Elford Development and had agreed to expand its workforce by 65 to 80 jobs as part of the deal; the nonprofit's CEO said a successful referendum would delay the project and raise its cost. If certified, the referendum would appear on the November 2027 ballot."
why_it_matters: "If the Franklin County Board of Elections certifies this referendum, it puts an already-approved 248-unit rezoning back in front of voters more than a year out, a preview of how a single organized petition group can suspend a Central Ohio development that has already cleared council and stall it into 2027."
