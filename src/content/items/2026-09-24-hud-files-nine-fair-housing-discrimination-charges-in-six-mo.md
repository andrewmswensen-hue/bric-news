summary: "HUD announced it filed nine Fair Housing Act discrimination charges over the past six months, four involving alleged discrimination against families with children, three involving disability discrimination and one involving sexual harassment. The agency's Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity said it has cut its backlog of pending complaints by 46 percent since Sept. 1, 2025, which HUD attributed to delays under the prior administration. A 2024 HUD inspector general report found about 70 percent of cases were not closed within the legally required 100-day window."
detail: "HUD said its Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity filed nine charges under the Fair Housing Act over the past six months: four alleging discriminatory rental practices or lease terms against families with children, three alleging landlords failed to make reasonable accommodations for tenants with disabilities, and one alleging sexual harassment. The agency said it has reduced its backlog of pending Fair Housing Act complaints by 46 percent since Sept. 1, 2025. HUD attributed the prior backlog to the Biden administration, citing a 2024 report from HUD's Office of Inspector General that found about 70 percent of complaints were not resolved within the 100-day timeline required by law. HUD Secretary Scott Turner said the agency is clearing what he called an \"indefensible\" case backlog while delivering relief to complainants. Assistant Secretary for Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity Craig Trainor said the office is enforcing the Fair Housing Act alongside other civil rights laws, including the Americans with Disabilities Act and the Violence Against Women Act. The Fair Housing Act prohibits discrimination in the sale or rental of housing based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status and disability. People who believe they experienced housing discrimination can file a complaint through HUD's website."
why_it_matters: "The four charges over lease terms and rental practices toward families with children are the category most likely to touch an Ohio landlord's own leasing decisions, since a faster-moving HUD complaint process means a familial-status dispute over an occupancy limit or lease clause could reach an enforcement charge well inside the 100-day window instead of sitting in backlog for months."
