detail: "Zillow's August 2026 housing report tracked where renters search for listings outside their home metro, a leading indicator the company says predicts future moves. Buffalo drew the most outside interest, up 4.2 percent year over year, followed by Houston (up 3.4 percent) and New Orleans (up 3 percent). In several established relocation destinations, a majority of rental page views already come from outsiders: Raleigh led at 59 percent, followed by Hartford at 55.1 percent, New Orleans at 53.7 percent, Salt Lake City at 51.9 percent, Nashville at 51.7 percent and Providence at 51.5 percent. Typical rents in the most-searched cities undercut the national figure of $1,948: Buffalo at $1,449, Houston at $1,643, Dallas at $1,659 and New Orleans at $1,598. Local renter interest, a separate measure, rose most in Cincinnati (up 8.1 percent year over year), Jacksonville (up 4.6 percent) and Columbus (up 3.2 percent). A companion Realtor.com summer report found that in only five of its top 20 metros do household incomes still cover housing costs at or below the standard 30 percent affordability threshold: Peoria, Illinois; Akron, Youngstown-Warren and Canton-Massillon, Ohio; and Rockford, Illinois. Zillow's chief economist, Mischa Fisher, said rental demand tends to run ahead of actual moves, calling the search data a preview rather than a recap of migration patterns."
why_it_matters: "Columbus's 3.2 percent rise in local renter search interest lands it among the metros gaining tenant demand as coastal and Sunbelt markets price out renters, but the Realtor.com affordability data suggests Akron, Youngstown and Canton, not Columbus, are where incomes most comfortably cover the 30 percent cost threshold today, a distinction worth weighing before assuming rising search volume alone signals room to raise Columbus rents."
