detail: "The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development proposed a rule that would give local public housing authorities the option to impose work requirements and time limits on able-bodied adults receiving federal housing assistance, including public housing and Section 8 housing choice vouchers. HUD Secretary Scott Turner said the proposal would let agencies require up to 40 hours of work per week and set assistance time limits of two years or longer, while exempting seniors, children and people with disabilities.\n\nHUD cited federal data showing nearly half of non-elderly, able-bodied households receiving assistance in 2024 did not include a working adult, and that the average length of stay in major rental assistance programs has grown to eight or nine years. The agency estimates the change could increase national income for assisted households by up to $51 million.\n\nAlongside the proposal, HUD launched a \"Work and Dignity Coalition\" inviting public housing agencies to adopt work-focused policies. The Housing Authority of Champaign County and the Vermilion Housing Authority in Illinois were the first two agencies to join.\n\nThe rule now enters the federal rulemaking process and must go through a public comment period before HUD can finalize it. No timeline for that process was announced."
