title: "HUD extends FHA multifamily financing to build-to-rent communities"
summary: "The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development issued Mortgagee Letter 2026-12, making build-to-rent (BTR) communities eligible for two FHA multifamily insurance programs previously reserved for traditional apartment buildings. BTR properties with at least five single-family, duplex, triplex or manufactured housing units can now use Section 221(d)(4) for new construction, which carries a fixed rate and a combined term of up to 43 years, or Section 223(f) for acquisition and refinancing of properties built within the last three years, with terms up to 35 years."
detail: "HUD's Mortgagee Letter 2026-12 opens two FHA multifamily insurance programs to build-to-rent (BTR) developments, which it previously limited to buildings of five or more attached units. A qualifying BTR community must be a single rental development of at least five single-family homes, duplexes, triplexes or manufactured homes. Section 221(d)(4) covers new construction or substantial rehabilitation, with an interest-only period of up to three years that converts to a fixed-rate loan with a combined term of up to 43 years. Section 223(f) covers acquisition or refinancing of BTR properties with a certificate of occupancy less than three years old, with fixed terms up to 35 years. According to HousingWire, former FHA Commissioner Frank Cassidy said the underwriting rules predate BTR as a recognized asset class and needed updating to include it. The change builds on the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, which raised FHA multifamily loan limits and dropped an earlier Senate provision that would have required many BTR homes to be sold within seven years of construction. An analyst with John Burns Research and Consulting told HousingWire the effect on new supply could take two to three years to appear, since FHA approval is slow and the construction loan requires prevailing wage labor."
why_it_matters: "A Columbus-area investor developing a build-to-rent community of five or more homes can now apply for FHA's Section 221(d)(4) construction loan, which locks a fixed rate through a combined term of up to 43 years instead of relying only on a short-term construction or bridge loan."
