title: "HOA foreclosures rose 40 percent in two years as associations chase delinquent dues"
summary: "Foreclosure activity tied to homeowners association collections reached 6,376 properties in the first quarter of 2026, a 40 percent increase from two years earlier, according to ATTOM data reported by the Wall Street Journal. Associations are pursuing collections harder because their own costs are climbing: general liability insurance rose for 91 percent of community associations, with some premiums more than doubling."
detail: "The figure covers the full range of foreclosure activity, from default notices through completed sales, where the action was initiated by a homeowners association rather than a mortgage holder.\n\nThe driver is financial pressure on the associations themselves. Costs for maintaining common areas, staffing and grounds have climbed, and the Foundation for Community Association Research found that general liability insurance rose for 91 percent of associations, with some premiums increasing more than 100 percent. The Journal cited a Long Island association whose annual premium went from $60,000 to $360,000. Brian Fox of Benutech, which tracks association delinquency, told the Journal that associations are being pushed into more aggressive collections to avoid their own insolvency.\n\nThe legal mechanism that makes this consequential is super-lien status, available in about half of states, which allows an association lien to take priority over other recorded instruments including the first mortgage. In many jurisdictions the process also sits outside standard judicial foreclosure, so the procedural protections an owner would expect in a mortgage foreclosure may not apply. A default notice sent to a stale address can start the clock without the owner seeing it."
