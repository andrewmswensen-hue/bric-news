detail: "Federal Housing Administration leadership is working to bring more lenders into the FHA loan program, HousingWire reports from a recent industry conference. A senior FHA official said his team has reviewed HUD's sub-regulatory handbook, mortgagee letters, and loan origination and servicing cost structures to identify what has limited lender participation. He pointed to the revival of a 2019 memorandum of understanding between HUD and the Department of Justice that limits the use of the False Claims Act against lenders over defects in FHA-insured mortgages, aiming to give lenders clearer rules before they take on FHA business.\n\nSeparately, Ginnie Mae's president, who is also leading FHA on an interim basis, discussed a provision in the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act that removes the steel-chassis requirement for manufactured homes. He said the change should blur the line between site-built and factory-built housing and could accelerate manufactured housing supply at a cost of roughly $100 to $120 per square foot. He added that investors have shown a willingness to pay more for manufactured housing loans given their lower prepayment rates, and Ginnie Mae is working to expand related investor disclosures."
why_it_matters: "Removing the steel-chassis requirement could make manufactured and modular housing cheaper to build, at roughly $100 to $120 per square foot, giving Columbus-area investors a lower-cost infill or workforce-housing option as land and construction costs climb."
