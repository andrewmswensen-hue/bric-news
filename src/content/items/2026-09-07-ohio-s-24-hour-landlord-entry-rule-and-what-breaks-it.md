title: "Ohio's 24-hour landlord entry rule, and what breaks it"
summary: "Ohio law presumes 24 hours is reasonable notice before a landlord enters an occupied rental, under Ohio Revised Code 5321.04(A)(8), RL Property Management explains. Entry must also happen at a reasonable time for a legitimate purpose, such as a repair, inspection, or showing, with emergencies as the exception. A landlord who enters improperly can face actual damages, an injunction, attorney's fees, or a tenant who terminates the lease under ORC 5321.04(B)."
detail: "Ohio Revised Code 5321.04(A)(8) sets the standard for landlord entry into an occupied rental: the landlord must give reasonable notice and enter at a reasonable time for a legitimate purpose, such as an inspection, a repair, an agreed service, or a showing, except in an emergency or when notice is impracticable. The statute puts a number on 'reasonable': 24 hours is presumed reasonable in the absence of evidence to the contrary, though a landlord giving less notice can still justify it case by case. Tenants carry a matching duty under ORC 5321.05(B) not to unreasonably withhold consent to a landlord's entry for repairs, inspections, or showings. The remedies for a violation, laid out in ORC 5321.04(B), are broader than many landlords expect: a tenant can recover actual damages, obtain an injunction, recover attorney's fees, or terminate the rental agreement outright, meaning an improper entry can cost an owner a paying tenant and an unplanned vacancy. A related question RL Property Management says owners keep asking involves the Columbus rental registry, adopted in 2026 under City Code Chapter 4515, with initial registration running October 1 through December 31 at $15 per unit. The registry authorizes a 'Preventative Education Inspection' on a three-year cycle, but that inspection covers exteriors, common areas, and shared mechanical systems, not the interior of occupied units, unless a property lands in conditional status under a separate provision. RL Property Management, which manages roughly 740 units in central Ohio, says it handles routine entry through scheduled, written notice tied to a fixed inspection scope rather than ad hoc visits."
why_it_matters: "An owner who assumes the new Columbus rental registry gives inspectors a right to walk through occupied units is wrong, and treating it that way risks a separate ORC 5321.04 entry violation on top of confusing tenants about the registry's actual scope."
