title: "Piqua, Urbana, Wilmington voters curb data center projects via ballot"
summary: "Voters in Piqua, Urbana and Wilmington passed ballot measures this week limiting or reversing data center development, according to WYSO. Piqua banned data centers larger than 25 megawatts; Urbana banned those over 7.5 megawatts, complicating a lawsuit over Thor Equities' proposed Urbana Technology Hub; and Wilmington voters reversed a February zoning change that rezoned 545 acres near U.S. 68 from rural residential to light industrial, land residents link to a proposed Amazon data center campus. The results follow months of resident-led petition drives across the Miami Valley."
detail: "Three Miami Valley communities voted this week to restrict data center development after grassroots petition campaigns forced the measures onto the ballot, WYSO reported. In Piqua, voters approved a charter amendment banning data centers larger than 25 megawatts of peak power demand, a year after city commissioners used an emergency vote to approve a $1 billion Meta data center on a 600-acre city-owned tract; three Piqua commissioners were also recalled. Urbana voters banned data centers over 7.5 megawatts, complicating a pending settlement in Thor Equities' federal lawsuit over its proposed 460,000-square-foot Urbana Technology Hub, which the company has offered to resolve with $50 million in community payments; Urbana council could face more than $100 million in damages if it continues fighting the suit instead of settling. Wilmington had no outright ban on its ballot, but voters overwhelmingly reversed a February council decision that rezoned 545 acres near U.S. 68 from rural residential to light industrial, land residents believe is tied to a proposed Amazon data center campus. An Ohio Supreme Court ruling had already blocked a stricter Wilmington measure from reaching this ballot over an enforcement clause; organizers say they plan to refile it."
why_it_matters: "Central Ohio has its own data center pipeline competing for land, power and local goodwill, and these votes show Ohio communities increasingly using charter amendments and zoning referendums, not just council votes, to block or delay large projects near residential areas."
