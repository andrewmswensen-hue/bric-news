detail: "The data comes from RentRedi, a landlord and tenant rent payment platform used by close to 200,000 landlords and renters, according to BiggerPockets. Late payment rates on the platform vary by as much as four times from state to state, from around 5 percent in states such as Utah and Hawaii to as much as 20 percent in states such as Mississippi. RentRedi co-founder Ed Barone said the variation tracks regional conditions rather than tenant quality on its own, meaning a landlord in a market with a 20 percent baseline late rate is not necessarily managing worse tenants than one in a 5 percent market. The article recommends a specific operating fix regardless of market: setting autopay as the lease default, sending rent reminders before the due date rather than after, and charging a late fee consistently every month it applies rather than only sometimes, since inconsistent enforcement is what typically gets a late fee clause challenged. It also flags Texas Property Code 92.019 as an example of a state statute that voids a late fee entirely unless the lease spells out the specific dollar amount or calculation method, along with the date the fee is triggered."
why_it_matters: "The specific statute cited is Texas law, but the underlying lesson, that a late fee only holds up if the lease states the exact dollar amount and trigger date rather than a vague catch-all clause, applies to any Ohio lease as well."
