title: "FHFA Unifies Fannie, Freddie Pricing Grid for FICO and VantageScore"
summary: "The Federal Housing Finance Agency has removed a 20-point downward adjustment that had applied to VantageScore 4.0 scores on the shared loan pricing grid Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac lenders use. Lenders can already choose either Classic FICO or VantageScore 4.0 for eligible conventional loans, but must use the same model for every borrower on a loan. detail: "The Federal Housing Finance Agency has removed a 20-point downward adjustment that had been applied to VantageScore 4.0 scores when lenders use the model to underwrite Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac loans. The adjustment had been built into the shared loan-level price adjustment grid to account for differences between VantageScore 4.0 and Classic FICO. Lenders approved by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac have been able to choose either scoring model for eligible conventional loans since early September, but must apply the same model to every borrower on a given loan. Classic FICO remains available and is still required for manually underwritten loans. FICO 10T has not yet been approved for GSE delivery, though the FHA plans to begin using it for underwriting in January 2027. Historical data shows VantageScore 4.0 can produce higher scores than Classic FICO for the same borrower, so removing the adjustment changes how that gap carries through to mortgage pricing. United Wholesale Mortgage reported that about 25 percent of its borrowers currently get a more favorable result under VantageScore, a share it expects to reach 40 percent by the end of September. Rocket Mortgage said it will make VantageScore 4.0 its preferred scoring model for all eligible loans, after testing both models on 1.4 million credit reports and finding that some borrowers who used VantageScore saved an average of $1,600 at closing."
