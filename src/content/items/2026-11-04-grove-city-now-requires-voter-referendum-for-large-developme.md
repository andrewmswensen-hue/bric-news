detail: "Grove City voters approved a charter amendment Tuesday that requires a citywide referendum before any large-scale development can move forward, NBC4 reported. The measure, put on the ballot by the group Protect Grove City after the city rejected a data center proposal and enacted a moratorium, requires a public vote for any project covering more than 50 acres, using more than 500,000 gallons of water a day, or drawing more than 20 megawatts of power. It also adds new lighting and noise restrictions. Grove City Mayor Ike Stage said the approval process for qualifying projects will now stretch from two to four months to 18 to 22 months, and the city listed more than a dozen properties the new rule could affect, including Mount Carmel's 61-acre campus and the mental health nonprofit Buckeye Ranch. Stage said he expects the city to face lawsuits over the amendment and that officials are hiring a law firm to review next steps, including creating a city auditor position the charter change requires. The vote was one of five Central Ohio ballot measures passed this week curbing data center development."
