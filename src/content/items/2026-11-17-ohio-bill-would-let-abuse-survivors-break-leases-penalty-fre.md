summary: "State Rep. Melanie Miller testified before the House Judiciary Committee on House Bill 855, the Ohio Safe Homes Act, sponsored jointly with Rep. Michele Grim. The bill would let tenants who are victims of rape, domestic violence, dating violence, abuse or a sexually oriented offense terminate a rental agreement without early termination penalties, and would let qualifying tenants request replacement locks with required documentation."
detail: "Rep. Melanie Miller (R-Ashland) gave sponsor testimony before the House Judiciary Committee on House Bill 855, the Ohio Safe Homes Act, which she sponsors jointly with Rep. Michele Grim (D-Toledo). The bill would let tenants who are victims of rape, attempted rape, domestic violence, dating violence, abuse or a sexually oriented offense terminate their rental agreement without early termination penalties.\n\nQualifying tenants could also request replacement locks for their unit to help protect their safety, provided they submit required documentation to the landlord. The bill lays out acceptable forms of documentation, lock replacement procedures and limits on installation costs that can be passed to the tenant. It includes a protection for property owners: a tax credit for landlords whose tenants terminate a lease under the bill's provisions, while preserving landlords' existing right to recover property damages.\n\nMiller said no one should have to choose between personal safety and the financial burden of leaving an abusive situation. House Bill 855 awaits further consideration in the House Judiciary Committee; no vote has been scheduled."
why_it_matters: "If House Bill 855 becomes law, Columbus-area landlords would need to accept lease termination notices from qualifying survivors without charging an early termination fee, though the bill also offers landlords a tax credit tied to those lease breaks."
