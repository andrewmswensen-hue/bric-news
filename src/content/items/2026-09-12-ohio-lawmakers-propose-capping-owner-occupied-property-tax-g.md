title: "Ohio lawmakers propose capping owner-occupied property tax growth at 3%"
summary: "Two Republican state lawmakers, Kevin Miller of Newark and Jeff LaRe of Violet Township, introduced a constitutional amendment capping annual growth in the taxable value of an owner-occupied home at 3%, regardless of appraised value. The cap would not apply to non-owner-occupied property, including rentals. Miller said the goal is to stop unvoted tax increases from rising appraisals while giving local governments an inflationary adjustment. The amendment needs voter approval and is not expected on a ballot this year, with sponsors aiming for May."
detail: "State Reps. Kevin Miller (R-Newark) and Jeff LaRe (R-Violet Township) filed a joint resolution for a constitutional amendment that would limit growth in the taxable value of an owner-occupied residence to 3% per year, no matter how much the county auditor's appraisal rises. Miller said the change targets what he calls unvoted tax increases, since a home's taxable value, and the tax bill built on it, can jump sharply at reappraisal without any ballot vote by residents. Local governments would still get a small inflationary adjustment but would lose the additional revenue from appraisal growth above 3% unless voters approve a new levy.\n\nThe cap applies only to owner-occupied homes, not to rental property, so a landlord's taxable value would continue rising with the full appraisal under current law. Miller and LaRe are aiming for a May ballot, but the resolution would need to clear the General Assembly first, likely in the new session starting in January. It is the 15th constitutional amendment Ohio legislators have proposed this General Assembly. A separate volunteer effort to abolish property taxes outright, Ax Ohio Tax, missed the deadline for this fall's ballot and is now aiming for November 2027."
why_it_matters: "Because the proposed 3% cap would apply only to owner-occupied homes, a Columbus landlord's non-owner-occupied rental parcels would keep absorbing the full appraisal increase at every reappraisal, which could widen the tax gap between an investor's rental and the identical house next door if an owner occupies it."
