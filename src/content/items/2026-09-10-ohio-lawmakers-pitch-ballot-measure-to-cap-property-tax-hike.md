title: "Ohio Lawmakers Pitch Ballot Measure to Cap Property Tax Hikes"
summary: "Two Ohio state representatives, Jeff LaRe (R-Violet Township) and Kevin Miller (R-Newark), are pitching a constitutional amendment that would limit how much a home's appraised value can rise for tax purposes. The proposal would tie annual valuation increases to inflation and cap them at 3%. Any increase above that cap would be exempt from taxation as long as the owner, or their spouse, continues living in the home. Because it is a constitutional amendment, the measure would need to go before Ohio voters as a statewide ballot issue."
detail: "State Reps. Jeff LaRe, a Violet Township Republican, and Kevin Miller, a Newark Republican, are pitching a constitutional amendment aimed at limiting how fast property tax bills can climb as home values rise. Under the proposal, increases in a home's appraised value would be tied to the rate of inflation and capped at 3% per year for tax purposes. Any portion of a valuation increase above that 3% cap would be exempt from taxation, but only for as long as the property's owner, or their spouse, continues to live there. The cap would not apply if the property changes hands, since the exemption is tied to continuous occupancy by the same owner or their spouse. Because a constitutional amendment requires voter approval in Ohio, the measure would need to appear on a statewide ballot before it could take effect."
why_it_matters: "If enacted, this cap applies only to a homesteaded owner-occupant's tax bill, not to a landlord's non-owner-occupied rental parcels, so investment property owners would keep facing full valuation-driven tax growth even as owner-occupied neighbors get relief."
