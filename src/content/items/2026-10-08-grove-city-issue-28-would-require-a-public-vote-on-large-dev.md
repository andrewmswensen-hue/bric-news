detail: "Grove City residents will vote in November on Issue 28, a citizen-initiated charter amendment that would require a public referendum before the city could approve qualifying large-scale developments, according to NBC4. The measure defines a qualifying project as one taking up 50 or more acres, drawing more than 20 megawatts of power at peak capacity, or consuming more than 500,000 gallons of water per day, thresholds that target data centers but would sweep in other large projects that meet them.\n\nThe push came from Protect Grove City, a group of residents who organized after working to stop a proposed 300-acre data center in the city. Organizer Jen Belt said the effort to stop that project took everything residents had and that Issue 28 is meant to guarantee residents a formal vote on similar projects going forward rather than leaving the decision to council alone. Grove City Council passed a resolution in August opposing the measure.\n\nIf voters approve Issue 28, any future project clearing those acreage, power, or water thresholds would need to go to a citywide vote before the city could sign off, adding a new approval step and timeline uncertainty to large-scale development proposals in Grove City."
why_it_matters: "A developer or investor assembling a large parcel in Grove City, residential or otherwise, would need to clear a citywide vote on top of normal zoning and planning approval if Issue 28 passes, which adds real timeline and political risk to any project near the 50-acre, 20-megawatt, or 500,000-gallon thresholds."
