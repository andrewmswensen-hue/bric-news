title: "No Winter Eviction Ban in Ohio, But Franklin County Process Shifts a Bit"
summary: "Ohio law contains no winter or holiday ban on evictions, and a Franklin County filing can proceed through the cold months, according to RL Property Management. The bailiff still gives a tenant five days after a tag before set-out, including weekends and holidays, and crews must bring tarps to cover belongings in bad weather. Franklin County Municipal Court eviction filing fees rose to $149 for possession only or $186 with money damages as of Aug. 1, 2026. Columbus's Pay to Stay ordinance still lets a tenant stop a nonpayment case by paying rent and reasonable late fees before judgment."
detail: "Ohio's eviction statutes, Revised Code Chapters 1923 and 5321, contain no winter, cold-weather, or holiday moratorium, according to RL Property Management (RLPM), a Columbus-area property manager. The three-day notice, seven-day summons period, and 10-day writ execution timeline run the same in January as in June. What changes in winter is logistics rather than law. The Franklin County Municipal Court Clerk's cost schedule, effective Aug. 1, 2026, sets eviction filing at $149 for possession only or $186 for possession plus money damages, with a $35 writ fee and a $45 set-out fee. Once a bailiff tags a unit, the tenant has five days to leave, including weekends and holidays, and the set-out must happen by the 10th day. Crews handling a cold-weather set-out must bring plastic sheeting to cover a tenant's belongings, which stay outside for 48 hours, and the bailiff can cancel the set-out if the crew or supplies fall short. Inside Columbus, the city's Pay to Stay ordinance (Columbus City Code 4551.07) still applies regardless of season: a tenant who pays all past-due rent plus reasonable late fees before a landlord files, or that amount plus court costs after filing but before judgment, can defeat a nonpayment case. Late fees above the greater of $50 or 10% of monthly rent count as unreasonable for that purpose. RLPM estimates that waiting three months to file on a $1,500 monthly rental costs about $4,500 in lost rent, against a filing cost starting at $149, and points to payment plans, Pay to Stay tenders, or cash-for-keys agreements as options before a formal filing."
why_it_matters: "A landlord who waits for spring on a nonpaying $1,500 rental loses roughly $4,500 in rent over three months, far more than the $149 to $186 it costs to file in Franklin County today, and the math only gets worse if the unit then sits vacant through a slow winter re-lease."
