title: "Toledo refers non-compliant landlords to court under lead-safe law"
summary: "Toledo is using a new $7 million U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development grant to speed up lead paint remediation in older rental housing as it enforces its 2016 lead-safe ordinance, which requires rental properties and home daycares built before 1978 to pass lead inspections. More than 80% of Toledo's housing stock predates the 1978 federal ban on lead-based paint."
detail: "Toledo passed its first lead-safe ordinance in 2016, requiring rental properties and home daycares built before 1978, when lead-based paint was banned for residential use, to obtain lead inspections. More than 80% of the city's housing stock predates that ban. An updated version of the law took effect last year, adding an enforcement mechanism that refers non-compliant landlords to housing court; lead safe coordinator Monica Smith said the city has made a few hundred such referrals and has seen a significant share of those landlords come into compliance afterward. The city is now using a new $7 million grant from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development to help property owners pay for lead paint remediation, since many cannot afford the repairs themselves, according to lead grant program manager Jerry Culkowski. The city prioritizes homes where children already have elevated blood lead levels. According to the Ohio Department of Public Safety's Office of Criminal Justice Services and the CDC, about three in five Ohio toddlers on Medicaid go without a required blood lead screening, even though Ohio's lead exposure risk ranks among the nation's highest. The nonprofit Toledo Lead Resource Center is training lead abatement workers, risk assessors and painters to address a workforce shortage that is slowing remediation citywide, according to its executive director, Candace Buckley."
why_it_matters: "Toledo's enforcement model, pre-1978 rental inspections referred to housing court rather than punished with fines, is the kind of lead-safe ordinance landlords with older Columbus-area housing stock should expect to see proposed locally, since Ohio's toddler lead-screening rate lags badly even as exposure risk ranks among the nation's highest."
