title: "Avon Lake petition would put TIF and rezoning of a power plant site to a public vote"
summary: "Residents in Avon Lake filed a proposed charter amendment with 1,148 signatures that would require voter approval before the city could pass any ordinance granting tax increment financing for the former Avon Lake Power Plant site, or rezoning it into the Lakefront Mixed-Use Overlay District. The filing exceeds the 484 valid signatures required, and the Lorain County Board of Elections must verify them. Backers want the measure on the November 3 ballot. The redevelopment would require a 30-year TIF at 100 percent, and the overlay zoning would permit up to 60 residential units per acre."
detail: "The proposed amendment was filed by William Zimmerman, attorney Gerald W. Phillips and Beth Wallace. It would bar either type of ordinance from taking effect unless voters approved it at a general or regular election held at least 90 days after passage.\n\nAvon Lake City Council has already approved an interim development agreement and a purchase-sale agreement with Avon Lake Environmental Redevelopment Group, and narrowly approved the Lakefront Mixed-Use Overlay District on a 4 to 3 vote. The developer has said the project needs a 30-year tax increment financing arrangement at 100 percent to proceed. The zoning would allow up to 60 residential units per acre with a total cap of 1,200 units.\n\nPhillips has said he will ask the Ohio Supreme Court to intervene if the measure does not reach the November 3 ballot.\n\nAvon Lake sits in Lorain County, west of Cleveland. The mechanism is what carries beyond that county line: a charter amendment subjecting TIF approvals and a specific rezoning to a referendum requirement."
why_it_matters: "Tax increment financing and overlay rezoning are the two tools Central Ohio suburbs lean on hardest to move large redevelopment sites, so a charter amendment that forces a public vote on both becomes a template residents can file elsewhere in Ohio if it survives review."
