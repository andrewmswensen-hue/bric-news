title: "Trump order directs FHFA to cut refinancing and insurance costs"
summary: "President Trump signed an executive order on October 22 directing the Federal Housing Finance Agency to consider cheaper title options, reduced documentation, and automatic cancellation of private mortgage insurance for eligible borrowers. The order also pushes FHFA toward a nationwide title acceptance program that could let Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac skip traditional title insurance on some refinances, and creates a task force on manufactured housing. Industry groups including the Mortgage Bankers Association welcomed the move but noted implementation depends on FHFA rulemaking."
detail: "The October 22 executive order, titled Decreasing Housing Costs for the American People, directs FHFA to consider a range of changes aimed at the fees that make refinancing expensive even when mortgage rates improve. The most developed provision would expand an existing title acceptance pilot into a nationwide program, letting Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac accept automated title reviews instead of a traditional lender's title insurance policy on eligible refinances, with any savings required to pass through to borrowers. FHFA is separately directed to consider waiving appraisals and other documentation for limited cash-out refinances that lower a borrower's payment, and to consider cutting loan-level price adjustments on those transactions. On mortgage insurance, the order sets up a pilot letting Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac use automated valuation models to find homeowners with enough equity to qualify for private mortgage insurance cancellation without applying, though this applies only to mortgages originated after the pilot is established, not to existing loans. The order also creates a Task Force on Manufactured Housing Innovation, led by HUD and FHFA, with 180 days to recommend standardized inspections and streamlined permitting for factory built homes. Separately, it directs the Agriculture and Veterans Affairs departments to consider rate cuts on loans they issue directly, not on privately originated VA or USDA guaranteed loans. None of these changes are immediate: each is a directive for FHFA or the agencies to consider, not a waiver or fee change in effect today. The Mortgage Bankers Association and the Community Home Lenders of America both welcomed the order, while flagging that the actual savings borrowers see depends on how FHFA and the GSEs implement the provisions."
why_it_matters: "If FHFA follows through, a Columbus investor refinancing a rental with 20% or more equity could skip a traditional title insurance policy, and an investor who used a conventional loan with PMI could see automatic cancellation reviews instead of having to request it, though none of that applies yet to loans already on the books."
