title: "Worthington Citizen Group Forces Zoning Vote With 3,976 Signatures"
summary: "A citizen group called Democracy Before Developers submitted 3,976 petition signatures to put Worthington City Council's July 20 rezoning of roughly 20 acres at 445 E. Dublin Granville Road to a public vote, aiming for the November 2027 ballot. The rezoning would allow a $52.4 million, 248-unit housing development on land owned by disability nonprofit I Am Boundless, which plans to sell part of the site to developer Elford Development. Ohio law required signatures equal to 35 percent of votes cast in Worthington's last gubernatorial race, or 3,017 here."
detail: "The petition targets Worthington City Council's July 20 vote to rezone about 20 acres at 445 E. Dublin Granville Road, clearing the way for a $52.4 million, 248-unit housing development. The land is owned by I Am Boundless, a disability services nonprofit that agreed to sell part of it to developer Elford Development while retaining jobs and adding 65 to 80 positions tied to the project. Campaign coordinator Marty Shumway said the petition drive, which ran July 23 to Sept. 21, drew broad participation citywide after an uncertain start. Worthington will hold the signatures for 10 days before sending them to the Franklin County Board of Elections, which will validate them and certify the ballot issue if the petition meets Ohio's referendum requirements; Shumway estimated 85 to 90 percent of the signatures will be ruled valid. The Worthington Chamber of Commerce, the group Building Worthington's Future, several council members and I Am Boundless have defended the July rezoning throughout the petition campaign. I Am Boundless President and CEO Patrick Maynard has said that if the measure reaches the November 2027 ballot and is certified, it would delay the housing project further and raise costs for the developer, and would delay public access to walking trails the developer had planned as part of the site."
why_it_matters: "If the referendum qualifies, the 248-unit Dublin Granville Road project would not break ground on its original schedule, which is the kind of timeline risk a Worthington-area investor underwriting nearby land or an adjacent property's future competition should build into a 2027, not 2026, delivery assumption."
