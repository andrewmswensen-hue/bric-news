title: "Columbus Considers New Peak Water Rate for Data Centers"
summary: "Columbus Water and Power is considering a new peaking rate that would charge high-volume industrial customers, including data centers, chip fabrication, brewing and food processing plants, for the extra pipe capacity they require, according to WCMH. The fee would not affect residential customers. The utility says large industrial users often need infrastructure sized for millions of gallons a day even though they do not draw that volume constantly, and the rate would recover that cost from the users who require it."
detail: "Columbus Water and Power is studying a new peaking rate aimed at its highest-volume industrial customers, including data centers, chip fabrication plants, and brewing and food processing facilities, according to WCMH. Utility director Kristen Atha said these customers often require large pipe infrastructure, in some cases a 12-inch line, to deliver water on demand even though they do not draw the full volume at all times, and the city currently charges every customer the same standard rate regardless of that infrastructure cost. The proposed peaking rate would apply only to those high-use industrial accounts and would not change residential water bills, according to deputy utility director Jon Lee, who said the utility recovers its costs without building in profit. Any new rate still has to go through the city's Utility Advisory Board before reaching City Council for a vote. Council has legislation on first reading directing Columbus Water and Power to bring a specific peaking rate proposal to council before the end of October, following a public hearing on the broader data center policy package."
why_it_matters: "The city has said explicitly that this fee will not touch residential water bills, so a typical rental property owner should not expect a rate change here, though it is the same underlying cost pressure driving the city's separate fourth water plant delay."
