title: "Athens County reappraisal lags sales by 19% as new Ohio laws may soften next bump"
summary: "Athens County homeowners are receiving 2026 reappraisal values that mostly rise from three years ago, but WOUB reports the new appraisals sit well below what homes sell for. In a review of 170 single-family sales through August, homes sold for an average of 19 percent more than the 2026 appraised value, and the median price was $271,250. Ohio taxes 35 percent of appraised value. Higher taxes based on the new values begin next year, and new state laws may moderate the next reappraisal's increase."
detail: "WOUB reports that Athens County's 2026 reappraisal raised values for most homes, in many cases by tens of thousands of dollars, and that higher tax bills based on the new values begin next year. County Auditor Jill Davidson said appraisals rely on sales from two or three years earlier and on exterior views only, so they trail the market. She said she prefers a margin of caution because of economic volatility.\n\nWOUB analyzed 170 open-market single-family sales from January through August. The median price was $271,250, and homes sold for an average of 19 percent more than the 2026 appraised value. Nearly a third sold for more than $100,000 above their 2023 appraisal.\n\nThe article explains that Ohio property tax is based on 35 percent of appraised value. On a $300,000 appraisal, $105,000 is taxable. That figure is divided by 1,000 and multiplied by each levy's millage, then credits and reductions are applied. A 3-mill levy, for example, would produce $315 a year on that home.\n\nAuditors reappraise every three years, and WOUB reports that unless prices fall, owners face another large increase at the next cycle, although recent state laws may temper it. Davidson also cautioned that challenging a valuation can backfire when a recent purchase price exceeds the appraised value.\n\nThe story is about Athens County, not Central Ohio, and it does not give Franklin, Delaware or Licking County figures."
