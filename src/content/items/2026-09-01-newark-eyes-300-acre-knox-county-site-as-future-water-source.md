title: "Newark eyes 300-acre Knox County site as future water source"
summary: "The City of Newark is evaluating a 300-acre property in Morgan Township, Knox County, just north of Utica, as a potential well field or reservoir to supplement its water supply. Water Administrator Brandon Fox told The Reporting Project there is no immediate need, but city council is set to vote Sept. 8 on an 18-month, $180,000 to $200,000 contract giving Newark first right to buy the land if a geological study finds it viable. Newark currently draws from the North Fork of the Licking River and treats 7 to 8 million gallons a day against a 15-million-gallon daily capacity."
detail: "Fox said a reservoir on the site could release water into the North Fork of the Licking River, from which Newark already draws downstream, while a well field could supply water directly to the Newark Water Plant. The city frames the site as a potential regional asset that could also benefit Utica and nearby townships, and says the land's location could support future recreational use such as a park or walking path. Fox, who previously worked on Columbus's Del-Co reservoir project in Delaware County, said Newark's search follows a similar due-diligence process on a smaller scale. If a future decision is made to build a reservoir or drill wells, the city would need to issue water bonds repaid through water department billing revenue. The proposal will be presented to the full Newark City Council on Sept. 8, and the city plans additional public meetings on the search for future water sources."
why_it_matters: "For an investor holding or considering rental property in Licking County, a multi-year water-source study this far in advance signals the city expects to need more capacity eventually, the kind of infrastructure planning that tends to precede new development approvals rather than follow them."
