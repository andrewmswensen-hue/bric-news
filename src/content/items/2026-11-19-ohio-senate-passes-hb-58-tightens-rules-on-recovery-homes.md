title: "Ohio Senate passes HB 58, tightens rules on recovery homes"
summary: "The Ohio Senate unanimously passed House Bill 58 on November 18, sending it to Gov. Mike DeWine, who is expected to sign it. The bill requires recovery homes, transitional housing for people in addiction recovery, to get certification directly from the state rather than from an industry trade group, requires state officials to investigate complaints within three days, and gives local prosecutors more power to seek court orders shutting down homes that violate state law."
detail: "House Bill 58 previously cleared the Ohio House unanimously in February and passed the Senate without opposition on November 18, sending it to Gov. Mike DeWine's desk. DeWine is expected to sign it after issuing an executive order in August that already imposes many of the bill's measures on a temporary basis; the bill would make those requirements permanent.\n\nThe bill ends the option for recovery homes to get certified through an industry trade group instead of the state, requires state officials to investigate a complaint about a recovery home within three days, and gives local prosecutors expanded authority to seek a court order shutting down a home found to violate state law. The bill is a priority for officials in Scioto County, an epicenter of the opioid epidemic that now has the fourth-most recovery home operators in the state despite ranking 35th in population. Local officials have said they have struggled to regulate the fast-growing industry, and some operators have been found offering substandard conditions that prosecutors could not easily shut down under the prior rules."
why_it_matters: "An investor who owns or is weighing a property to lease as a recovery home will need direct state certification rather than trade-group certification once DeWine signs this, and local prosecutors will have clearer authority to shut down a noncompliant operation faster than before."
