detail: "A federal judge has dismissed a lawsuit filed by two Philadelphia landlords who sought to block renter protection laws passed by Philadelphia City Council earlier this year. U.S. District Judge Wendy Beetlestone ruled Friday that the landlords' constitutional challenge, which argued the laws violated property rights under the Fifth and 14th Amendments, rested on speculation rather than factual allegations. She wrote that the laws serve legitimate government interests, including reducing displacement and promoting housing stability.\n\nThe laws are part of Philadelphia's Safe Healthy Homes Act. They expand good cause eviction protections to all renters regardless of lease term, require landlords to state a legally defined reason before ending or not renewing a lease, and bar landlords from retaliating against tenants who report code violations or join a tenant association. They also let tenants sue for back rent collected without a valid rental license.\n\nThe ruling was entered without prejudice, meaning the landlords can amend and refile their complaint. One of the plaintiffs, landlord Seth Floyd, said he intends to keep fighting the laws. The case tests how far a city can go in regulating landlord-tenant relationships without running into property rights claims, according to WHYY."
