summary: "The Federal Housing Finance Agency may announce as soon as Oct. 12 that Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac will accept credit data from two of the three national credit bureaus instead of requiring all three, National Mortgage Professional reported. FHFA Director Bill Pulte is expected to address the change at the Mortgage Bankers Association's annual convention in Chicago. The Mortgage Bankers Association supports the shift, telling FHFA that members faced credit-reporting cost increases of 40% to 50% heading into 2026."
detail: "The Federal Housing Finance Agency is weighing a long-debated change to how Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac verify a borrower's credit: moving from the current tri-merge requirement, which pulls files from Equifax, Experian and TransUnion, to a bi-merge model using only two of the three. According to National Mortgage Professional, FHFA Director Bill Pulte could announce the change as soon as Oct. 12 at the Mortgage Bankers Association's annual convention in Chicago, where he is scheduled to appear alongside MBA President and CEO Bob Broeksmit.\n\nThe Mortgage Bankers Association has pushed for the switch, telling FHFA in a December 2025 letter that members faced credit-reporting cost increases of 40% to 50% heading into 2026 under the three-bureau system. The Consumer Data Industry Association, which represents the credit bureaus, opposes dropping a bureau, arguing it could change loan outcomes: the group cites a TransUnion estimate that about 300,000 borrowers could see a different eligibility result under a reduced-bureau approach compared with tri-merge. FHFA first floated bi-merge reporting in 2022 but delayed implementation in January 2025 pending industry feedback. The Federal Housing Administration has said it will keep requiring tri-merge reports for FHA-backed loans even as the two frameworks diverge."
why_it_matters: "A Columbus investor financing a rental purchase through a conforming Fannie or Freddie loan could see lower credit-report costs and a faster underwriting file if FHFA finalizes bi-merge, though an FHA-backed purchase would still require all three bureau reports."
