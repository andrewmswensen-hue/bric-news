summary: "Mayfield Heights voters rejected a zoning change that would have allowed a 61-home townhome development on Parkland Boulevard, with 52.59% voting to revert the Planned Unit Development zoning the city council had approved in May. The referendum reached the ballot as Issue 107 after the Ohio Supreme Court ordered it onto the Nov. 3 election following a challenge from nearby residents and business owners. Developer Sommers Development Group said it is still working with the city to find a legal way to pursue construction despite the vote, according to the News-Herald."
detail: "Mayfield Heights City Council voted in May to rezone land along Parkland Boulevard from a Planned Unit Development district to allow a 61-home townhome project proposed by Sommers Development Group. Nearby residents and business owners opposed the change and took it to the Ohio Supreme Court, which ordered the rezoning onto the Nov. 3 ballot as Issue 107. Voters rejected it, with 3,353 votes (52.59%) in favor of reverting the zoning to office and headquarters use only, against 3,023 votes to let the rezoning stand, according to the Cuyahoga County Board of Elections.\n\nSommers Development Group principal Greg Sommers said the company still intends to build, calling the project the highest and best use of the property and saying it is working with the mayor, city council and legal counsel on multiple avenues to proceed despite the referendum result. The outcome tests how much a citizen zoning referendum actually blocks a project once a council has approved it, a question that matters anywhere a developer is relying on a council-approved rezoning that opponents could still put to a public vote."
