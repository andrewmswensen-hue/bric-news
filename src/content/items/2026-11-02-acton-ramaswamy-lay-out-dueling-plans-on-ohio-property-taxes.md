title: "Acton, Ramaswamy Lay Out Dueling Plans on Ohio Property Taxes"
summary: "Ohio governor candidates Amy Acton and Vivek Ramaswamy outlined contrasting property tax plans to The Columbus Dispatch ahead of the Nov. 3 election. Ramaswamy would roll property taxes back to pre-pandemic levels, a cut think tank Innovation Ohio estimates would cost local governments and schools several billion dollars in revenue. Acton would instead expand the homestead exemption and add a rebate of up to $1,000 for homeowners with income under $132,000 whose bills rose more than 4% in a year."
detail: "With Ohio's governor's race reaching its final day before the Nov. 3 election, The Columbus Dispatch asked Republican Vivek Ramaswamy and Democrat Amy Acton how they would handle the state's property tax backlash, which intensified after 2023 reappraisals pushed many bills higher. Ramaswamy, who floated eliminating property taxes entirely during his campaign launch, has scaled that back to a plan that rolls rates back to their level at the end of the COVID-19 pandemic, while protecting new construction and honoring existing local government debt. His campaign calls it the largest property tax cut in state history; Innovation Ohio, a progressive think tank, estimates it would strip several billion dollars from local services including K-12 schools, police and fire departments.\n\nActon does not want to eliminate property taxes. She would expand Ohio's homestead exemption, which currently lets qualifying seniors and people with disabilities exempt the first $29,000 of a home's value, and add a rebate of up to $1,000 for homeowners with household income under $132,000 and home values at or below $350,000 whose bills rose more than 4% in a year. On data centers, both candidates said they would impose conditions rather than bans: Acton wants a conditional moratorium requiring full utility cost coverage, union labor and brownfield siting, while Ramaswamy wants new projects to cover neighbors' electricity bills and lower their property taxes."
why_it_matters: "Whichever plan becomes law would change a landlord's carrying costs starting with the next reappraisal cycle: Ramaswamy's rollback would cut bills but could squeeze the local services landlords and tenants rely on, while Acton's plan leaves most current rates in place and targets relief narrowly at seniors and lower-income homeowners."
