title: "HUD proposes work requirements and time limits for housing aid"
summary: "HUD proposed a rule allowing local housing authorities to require able-bodied adults on federal rental assistance to work up to 40 hours a week and imposing time limits of two years or more. Seniors, children and people with disabilities are exempt. HUD launched a Work and Dignity Coalition, with two Illinois housing authorities as first adopters. The rule needs a public comment period before taking effect. HUD says nearly half of non-elderly, able-bodied assisted households lacked a working adult in 2024, and average stays in rental assistance have grown to roughly eight to nine years."
detail: "HUD Secretary Scott Turner announced the proposed rule alongside officials from the Housing Authority of Champaign County and the Vermilion Housing Authority in Illinois, the first two agencies to join HUD's new Work and Dignity Coalition. The rule would give public housing agencies the option, not a mandate, to impose work requirements and time limits on able-bodied adults receiving public housing or Housing Choice Voucher assistance. Turner said the proposal preserves protections for elderly and disabled households and is intended to shorten waiting lists by moving long-term recipients toward self-sufficiency. HUD data cited in the announcement shows the average length of stay in major rental assistance programs has grown to nearly eight or nine years, and about half of able-bodied voucher holders have kept a voucher for more than 15 years. HUD estimates the change could add up to $51 million in aggregate income nationally if widely adopted. The rule must clear a public comment period before it is final, and each local housing authority would then decide separately whether to adopt work requirements for its own programs."
why_it_matters: "This is a proposal, not a final rule, but if Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority later adopts it, voucher tenants would face new work-hour and time-limit conditions to keep their subsidy, changing who stays eligible to rent from a Franklin County landlord under Section 8."
