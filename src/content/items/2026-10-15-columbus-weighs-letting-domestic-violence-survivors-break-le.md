detail: "Columbus City Councilmember Tiara Ross, who chairs the council's Housing, Homelessness and Building Committee, held a hearing Oct. 14 on proposed changes to the city's housing code aimed at helping domestic violence survivors leave unsafe living situations, the Columbus Dispatch reports. The proposal would let tenants end a lease without facing fees for breaking it or continuing rent obligations on a unit they can no longer safely occupy, and would extend the same protection to survivors of sexual assault and stalking.\n\nA parallel effort is underway at the Statehouse: House Bill 855, the Ohio Safe Homes Act, sponsored by state Reps. Michele Grim and Melanie Miller, would let survivors of sexual assault or domestic violence terminate a rental agreement without penalty statewide and would bar landlords from retaliating against them or sharing their identifying information. A similar 2023 bill from Grim did not advance in the Republican-controlled legislature; bipartisan sponsorship this time may improve its odds.\n\nColumbus police have received more than 21,800 domestic violence calls through Aug. 31 this year and project the total will top 30,000 by year's end. Domestic violence-related killings account for roughly 30% of the city's homicides."
why_it_matters: "If either the Columbus ordinance or House Bill 855 passes, Columbus-area landlords would have to waive early-termination fees and continued rent for tenants who document domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking, and could face retaliation claims for penalizing them, a lease-enforcement exception landlords don't currently have to plan around."
