title: "Review of 3,090 Rent to Retirement homes tests turnkey promises"
summary: "An independent third-party review of 3,090 homes sold by turnkey rental provider Rent to Retirement between 2017 and 2026 found that 75.5% of 2017-2018 buyers still own their homes seven to eight years later, and owners who held four or more years sold above their purchase price 96% of the time. Among 740 homes with both a projected and investor-reported rent, 80.8% collected at least 95% of the projected amount; Ohio rents were the most accurate in the sample, with 94.9% hitting that mark, while Florida homes reached it only 46% of the time."
detail: "Rent to Retirement, a turnkey single-family rental provider that says about 80% of its inventory is new construction, opened its sales records to an independent third-party review covering 3,090 homes sold between 2017 and 2026. Reviewers matched 2,763 of those homes to public resale records and compared projected rent against investor-reported rent on 740 of them. Among the 489 homes sold in 2017 and 2018, 75.5% remained with their original buyer seven to eight years later. Of 148 recorded resales, three in four sold above the original purchase price, and owners who held four or more years sold above cost 96% of the time, with a median sale price of 1.57 to 1.86 times their purchase price; those who sold within two years broke even at the median. On rent accuracy, 80.8% of the 740 reviewed homes collected at least 95% of their projected rent, and the median home rented for exactly the projected amount. Results varied by market and price: Ohio homes hit the 95% rent-accuracy mark 94.9% of the time, the strongest in the sample, while Florida homes did so only 46% of the time. Homes priced under $150,000 reached that mark 97.5% of the time and carried a median 10.8% gross yield, compared with 51.2% and a 7.7% yield for homes above $350,000."
why_it_matters: "For a Columbus investor weighing a turnkey purchase, the data shows rent projections run most accurate on lower-priced homes and in Ohio specifically, 94.9% of homes hit at least 95% of projection here versus 46% in Florida, so the same provider's numbers deserve more scrutiny the more expensive and out-of-state the property gets."
