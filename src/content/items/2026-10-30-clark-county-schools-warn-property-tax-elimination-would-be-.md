detail: "Clark County school superintendents warned at an Oct. 26 Springfield Rotary Club meeting that eliminating Ohio's local property taxes, an idea circulating in Statehouse property tax reform debates, would be catastrophic for schools and other local services. Clark-Shawnee superintendent Brian Kuhn said replacing the revenue that funds police, fire, mental health services, schools and libraries would require the state to find billions of dollars. Greenon superintendent Darrin Knapke said eliminating property taxes would wipe out 55% of his district's total revenue, and Springfield City Schools superintendent Bob Hill said property taxes now cover 70% of school funding, up from 48% in 1991, as the state's share has shrunk.\n\nHill said a 7 mill levy, already a heavy tax burden, would generate only about $6.5 million for Springfield City Schools, roughly one month of district operations. He pointed to the state's funding formula, which still relies on 2022 financial data, as a core driver of local budget shortfalls. Two legislative proposals are moving through the Statehouse: Senate Bill 93, which would shift to a statewide property tax and higher sales taxes, and House Bill 884, which would update the existing funding formula with 2025 cost figures."
