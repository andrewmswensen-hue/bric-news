title: "HUD proposes work requirements and time limits for federal housing aid"
summary: "HUD unveiled a proposed rule that would let local housing authorities require up to 40 hours of work per week and set time limits of two years or more for able-bodied adults on federal housing assistance. Seniors, children and people with disabilities would be exempt. The announcement came in Champaign, Illinois, where two housing authorities joined a new HUD coalition. The rule must go through a public comment period before it can take effect."
detail: "The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development announced a proposed rule that would give public housing agencies the option to impose work requirements and time limits on able-bodied adults who receive federal housing assistance. According to the report from WIXY, HUD Secretary Scott Turner said agencies could require up to 40 hours of work per week and set time limits of two years or more. Households with seniors, children or people with disabilities would not be affected.\n\nThe rule is a proposal only. It has to clear a public comment period and a federal rulemaking process before any final decision. HUD also launched a \"Work and Dignity Coalition\" for agencies that want to adopt work-focused policies, and the Housing Authority of Champaign County and the Vermilion Housing Authority in Illinois were the first two to join.\n\nTurner cited federal data showing that nearly half of non-elderly, able-bodied assisted households had no working adult in 2024, and that about half of able-bodied Section 8 voucher recipients spend more than 15 years in subsidized housing. HUD estimates the changes could raise household income nationally by up to $51 million. Assistant Secretary Benjamin Hobbs, who oversees the voucher and public housing programs, said the rule would let agencies align housing aid with other federal programs that already carry work requirements.\n\nThe rule would be optional for each housing authority, so any effect would depend on local decisions."
why_it_matters: "Voucher tenants pay part of their rent through a local housing authority, so an agency that adopts time limits or work rules could change how long a voucher household stays and how steady that rent stream is for a landlord in the program. The rule is optional and unfinished, so nothing changes until the comment period ends and each local authority decides."
