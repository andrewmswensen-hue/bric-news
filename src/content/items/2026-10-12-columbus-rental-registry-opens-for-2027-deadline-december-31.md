title: "Columbus Rental Registry Opens for 2027, Deadline December 31"
summary: "Columbus opened registration for its new rental property registry on October 1, with an initial deadline of December 31, 2026 for the 2027 cycle. The fee is $15 per unit per year, capped at $1,500 per complex, under Ordinance 0923-2026 (Columbus City Code Chapter 4515). Owners of 10 or more units must designate a local operator within 100 miles. A violation left unfixed after notice costs $2 per unit per day."
detail: "Columbus layers city ordinances on top of federal and Ohio landlord-tenant law, and they apply only inside Columbus city limits, not in suburbs such as Gahanna, Dublin, or Worthington, according to RL Property Management (RLPM), which manages about 740 units in the metro. The newest obligation is a rental property registry under Ordinance 0923-2026 (Columbus City Code Chapter 4515): initial registration for 2027 runs October 1 through December 31, 2026, at $15 per unit per year, capped at $1,500 per complex, and owners of 10 or more units need a local operator within 100 miles. An unresolved violation after notice costs $2 per unit per day. The registry joins several existing rules. Source-of-income protection (CCC 4551.03) bars refusing a Housing Choice Voucher applicant; income screening then applies to the tenant's share of rent, not the full contract rent. Operators with five or more units must offer security deposit installment plans (CCC 4551.04). Landlords must give rent and deposit receipts (CCC 4551.05) and accept third-party rent payments (CCC 4551.06). Pay-to-Stay (CCC 4551.07) lets a tenant who tenders past-due rent and fees before judgment raise it as an eviction defense. Leases signed or renewed after January 31, 2025 must promise 60 days' written notice before any rent increase over 10 percent, with misdemeanor penalties in effect since July 2025. The city has also expanded fair housing protected classes and strengthened its anti-retaliation rule. RLPM says no court has struck down these ordinances under Ohio's preemption statute, ORC 5321.19, as of September 2026."
