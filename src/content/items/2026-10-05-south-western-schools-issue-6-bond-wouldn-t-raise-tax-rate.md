title: "South-Western schools' Issue 6 bond wouldn't raise tax rate"
summary: "South-Western City Schools voters decide Nov. 3 on Issue 6, a 2.26-mill bond issue to replace Grove City and Westland high schools without raising the district's overall property tax rate, the district says. The no-new-millage bond would be repaid over up to 38 years and is paired with roughly $113 million the district could access through the Ohio Facilities Construction Commission toward a nearly $226 million project. The district estimates property taxes would instead fall about $79 a year per $100,000 of appraised value if Issue 6 fails. Grove City Council has endorsed the measure."
detail: "Issue 6 would authorize South-Western City Schools to issue a new 2.26-mill bond to fund replacement buildings for Grove City and Westland high schools, both of which opened in 1970. The district calls it a no-new-millage bond because it would not raise the overall property tax rate: as existing school construction debt is paid off, the collections freed up would offset the new levy, keeping the combined rate flat. Passage would let the district draw roughly $113 million the state has committed through the Ohio Facilities Construction Commission toward a project the district estimates at nearly $226 million total, split about evenly between state and local funding. If voters reject Issue 6, the district says it would not issue the new bonds or collect the 2.26-mill levy, and property owners would instead see a decrease of about $79 a year per $100,000 of appraised value as the old construction debt winds down. Bond proceeds could be used only for construction and renovation, not salaries or daily operations. Grove City Council unanimously endorsed the measure on Sept. 21. The district last passed a bond issue in 2018."
why_it_matters: "Grove City and Westland-area landlords should watch the Nov. 3 vote either way: passing Issue 6 keeps the district's tax rate flat while funding new schools, but a no vote means those owners would instead see roughly a $79 per $100,000 of value reduction as old debt retires."
