title: "Ohio Supreme Court sides with Trenton voters in data center ballot fight"
summary: "The Ohio Supreme Court ruled Sept. 1 that Trenton officials unlawfully blocked a citizen petition to put a ban on new data centers before voters, and ordered the city to certify it for the Butler County Board of Elections. The fight centered on how to count the Ohio Constitution's requirement that at least 10% of a city's electors sign a charter-amendment petition: residents said the threshold should follow turnout in Trenton's last municipal election, 128 signatures, while the city wanted it based on all eligible voters, 820."
detail: "The case began when Trenton residents filed a petition on July 8 with 336 valid signatures asking for a charter amendment that would stop future data center development in the city, about 38 miles north of Cincinnati. Trenton is already the site of two proposed projects: a 141-acre facility from California-based Prologis and an 18-building complex Amazon has discussed building outside city limits.\n\nTrenton City Council rejected the petition at an Aug. 3 meeting, arguing the Ohio Constitution's 10% elector threshold should be measured against all eligible voters, which would have required 820 signatures. Residents argued the threshold should instead track turnout in Trenton's last general municipal election, requiring only 128 signatures. The Ohio Supreme Court agreed with residents, writing that the council disregarded applicable law, though it declined to award the petitioners court costs or attorney fees.\n\nThe city must now certify the petition and send it to the Butler County Board of Elections, which will schedule a special election within 60 to 120 days of certification. The ruling sets a statewide precedent for how the 10% signature threshold is measured in future charter-amendment petitions, a question already in play in other Ohio communities weighing citizen votes on data center projects."
why_it_matters: "This ruling settles the math for citizen petitions statewide: the 10% signature threshold is measured against turnout in the last municipal election, not the full voter rolls, the same question already in play in Grove City's own data center ballot fight in Franklin County."
