detail: "K-Nova II LLC filed a writ of mandamus against the village of Commercial Point in Pickaway County in June, after the village suspended review of K-Nova's data center site-plan application and refunded its application deposit. Commercial Point's council had passed an 18-month moratorium on data center development on May 4, 2026, while K-Nova's application, filed in April, was still pending. K-Nova argued it held a vested right to have the application reviewed under the zoning rules in effect when it applied, and that it had no adequate remedy without a court order forcing review. The Ohio Chamber of Commerce joined the case as amicus curiae in support of K-Nova. Commercial Point countered that K-Nova had no vested right to develop a data center and no clear legal right to immediate review, and asked the court to dismiss the petition or stay it pending related Pickaway County litigation. On Oct. 27, the Supreme Court granted the village's motion to dismiss without issuing an opinion; Justices Fischer, DeWine, Brunner, Deters, Hawkins and Shanahan concurred, while Chief Justice Kennedy agreed only in part and would have ordered the village to file an answer and issued an alternative writ. Commercial Point Mayor Nancy Geiger called the outcome a win for the village."
why_it_matters: "The dismissal lets Commercial Point's data center moratorium stand even against a developer that had already filed a site-plan application, reinforcing that a Central Ohio village can cut off a pending zoning application once a council-passed moratorium takes effect, the same vested-rights question now playing out in dozens of other Ohio data center fights."
