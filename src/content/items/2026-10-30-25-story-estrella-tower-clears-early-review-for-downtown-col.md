title: "25-story Estrella tower clears early review for Downtown Columbus"
summary: "The Columbus Downtown Commission gave conceptual approval Oct. 27 to a revised plan for The Estrella, a 25-story mixed-use tower at 175 E. Rich St. Developer Bluestone Brothers' design calls for 296 apartments plus seven penthouses, 327 parking spaces, ground-floor retail including a grocery concept, and a rooftop pool and restaurant. The project previously won up to $4.5 million in Ohio tax credits. The developer must still return Nov. 17 for a formal approval vote, and building it requires demolishing nine structures dating to 1900-1940 on the site."
detail: "Columbus's Downtown Commission reviewed an updated design for The Estrella, a long-planned 25-story mixed-use tower at the southeast corner of East Rich and South Fourth streets, at its Oct. 27 special meeting and found the changes consistent with the project's design direction. Developer Bluestone Brothers, working with Berardi + Partners, has revised the proposal multiple times since it first came before the commission in February 2025, most recently adding apartments and parking. The current plan calls for 296 one- and two-bedroom units across floors eight through 24, seven penthouses on floor 25, and roughly 18,000 square feet of ground-floor retail built around an urban market concept, which the developer has said is meant to address food-desert concerns in the area. Floors two through five would hold 327 parking spaces, and the sixth and seventh floors would include office, fitness, and bar space beneath a rooftop plaza, pool, and restaurant. Because the Oct. 27 session was a conceptual hearing, Bluestone Brothers must return to the commission Nov. 17 with a formal application before a vote. The roughly 300-foot tower would require demolishing nine structures built between 1900 and 1940, though the latest site plans exclude several South Fourth Street buildings, including Dirty Frank's Hot Dog Palace, that earlier versions had targeted. The project previously received up to $4.5 million in state tax credits."
why_it_matters: "Nearly 300 new apartment units at this scale would add meaningful downtown rental supply and ground-floor retail investors can lease into, and the Nov. 17 vote is the next concrete checkpoint for tracking whether the project actually breaks ground."
