title: "Ohio Supreme Court orders Trenton to let residents vote on data center ban"
summary: "The Ohio Supreme Court ruled Sept. 1 that Trenton, Ohio officials unlawfully blocked a resident petition to ban new data center construction, and ordered the city to certify the petition for a special election within 60 to 120 days. The dispute centered on how to count the 10% of electors needed to qualify a charter-amendment petition: residents argued it should be based on turnout in the last municipal election (128 signatures), while the city argued for all eligible voters (820 signatures). The justices sided with residents."
detail: "The case began when Trenton residents filed a petition with 336 valid signatures on July 8 seeking a charter amendment to stop future data center development. The city council rejected the petition at an Aug. 3 meeting, arguing the Ohio Constitution's 10% elector threshold required about 820 signatures based on all eligible voters, well above what the petition gathered. Residents countered that the threshold should be measured against turnout in Trenton's most recent general municipal election, which would set the bar at 128 signatures, a mark the petition cleared easily. The Ohio Supreme Court agreed with the residents' reading and found the council disregarded applicable law, though it declined to award attorney fees. The city must now certify the petition and send it to the Butler County Board of Elections; since it is too late for the November ballot, the measure will instead go before Trenton voters in a special election within 60 to 120 days. The ruling sets a signature-counting precedent for any Ohio city facing a resident petition drive, including zoning and development fights in Central Ohio townships."
why_it_matters: "The ruling establishes that Ohio cities must count petition-drive signature thresholds against recent election turnout rather than the full voter rolls, a precedent any Central Ohio township or suburb can now cite if it blocks a resident zoning petition, including the data center fights already underway in Franklin and Delaware counties."
