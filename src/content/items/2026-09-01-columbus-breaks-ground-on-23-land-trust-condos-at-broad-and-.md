title: "Columbus breaks ground on 23 land trust condos at Broad and Gould"
summary: "The City of Columbus and its development partners broke ground Aug. 27 on Broad and Gould Condominiums, 23 income-restricted two- and three-bedroom units at the southeast corner of East Broad Street and South Gould Road on the East Side. The four-story building will hold units of roughly 950 to 1,300 square feet over first-level parking, with a community room and a fitness room. Homes are expected to sell for about $199,000 to income-qualified buyers. The city is contributing $3 million from its $500 million affordable housing bond plus $1.1 million in buyer assistance."
detail: "The site at East Broad Street and South Gould Road had sat vacant. Under the project structure, buyers own the condominium while the Central Ohio Community Land Trust continues to own the land beneath it, so a resale must go to another income-qualified buyer at an affordable price rather than at full market value. That lets an owner build equity while the price restriction carries forward. The land trust is a subsidiary of COCIC, the Franklin County Land Bank, whose president and chief executive, Curtiss Williams, said the project returns unused property to productive use. Finance Fund is serving as developer and builder. The land trust has built and sold more than 200 homes in Franklin County since it formed, according to the project news release. Columbus is putting $3 million toward construction from the $500 million affordable housing bond package voters approved in November, and another $1.1 million toward homebuyer assistance. Mayor Andrew Ginther tied the project to the bond program and to Zone In, the city's zoning overhaul. The four-story building will include parking on the first level, a community room, and a fitness room. Units are expected to sell for about $199,000 to buyers who qualify on income and meet Central Ohio Community Land Trust requirements. Construction is expected to finish in fall 2027."
why_it_matters: "This is an early construction start drawing on the $500 million housing bond, and it sets for-sale pricing on the East Side at about $199,000 with a permanent resale restriction attached to the land. For anyone underwriting nearby, 23 units that cannot resell at market value will show up in the comparable set without behaving like ordinary comparables."
