summary: "Short-term rental insurance is a stand-alone policy that serves as the primary insurance contract on a property, not a homeowner's policy with a home-sharing endorsement or a landlord dwelling policy with a short-term rental permission added, according to BiggerPockets. The piece lays out five questions owners should ask before comparing short-term rental coverage options, starting with whether the policy being quoted is actually a stand-alone primary contract or a product layered on top of another policy."
detail: "Shopping for short-term rental insurance gets confusing because very different products end up compared side by side, according to BiggerPockets. The piece establishes a baseline definition first: true short-term rental insurance is a stand-alone primary insurance contract on a property rented to guests for short or mid-term stays, regardless of whether a company brands it as short-term rental, vacation rental, or Airbnb insurance. That is distinct from a standard homeowner's policy with a home-sharing endorsement added on, a landlord dwelling-form policy with short-term rental use permitted, or a supplemental product meant to sit on top of another underlying policy.\n\nThe first of the five comparison questions BiggerPockets recommends asking is whether the coverage under review is actually a stand-alone policy that would replace whatever is currently on the property, as opposed to a product designed to layer over an existing homeowner or landlord policy. Getting that answer first matters because it determines whether two quotes are even comparable products before an owner starts comparing premiums or coverage limits.\n\nFor an owner weighing a move into short-term rentals, or already running one, the practical risk is assuming a homeowner's policy with an add-on endorsement provides the same protection as a true short-term rental policy, when the underlying contract, exclusions, and claims process can differ substantially between the two."
why_it_matters: "A Columbus-area landlord converting a unit to short-term rental, or buying a property that already operates as one, should confirm with the current policy whether it is a true stand-alone short-term rental contract before relying on it, since a homeowner's policy with a home-sharing endorsement is not the same coverage and can leave a claim exposed."
