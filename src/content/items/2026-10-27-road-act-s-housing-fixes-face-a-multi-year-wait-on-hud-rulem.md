detail: "The ROAD Act, described on the panel as the first comprehensive federal housing bill in decades, authorized close to a dozen new programs aimed at increasing housing supply, but most require congressional funding or formal HUD rulemaking before they take effect, according to a session hosted this week by the Bipartisan Policy Center and the National Association of Affordable Housing Lenders (NAAHL). HUD is responsible for roughly 70% of the law's 125 identified federal actions and must issue nine new rulemakings, a process that typically takes 18 months to two years, meaning some provisions may not finalize until early 2028. HUD's workforce has shrunk 34%, to about 5,800 employees from 8,800 in fiscal 2024, which panelists said could slow the work further. One of the more consequential changes removes HUD's permanent chassis requirement for manufactured housing, which would let manufacturers build taller, denser factory-built homes, but manufacturers cannot use the new flexibility until HUD drafts updated construction standards. The law also lets USDA decouple Section 521 rental assistance from Section 515 loans nearing maturity, so rural affordable housing can keep receiving subsidies after the original mortgage ends, and it raises the HOME Investment Partnerships Program's income eligibility to 100% of area median income. Congress has not yet appropriated technical assistance funds for the USDA change, and the House's FY2027 HUD funding bill does not cover the new ROAD provisions."
