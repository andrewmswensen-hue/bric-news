title: "Columbus Council advances ordinance charging data centers for water use"
summary: "Columbus City Council President Shannon Hardin and Councilmember Christopher Wyche held a news conference and committee hearing September 16 on an ordinance requiring Columbus water and power to set new rates for high-load, high-volume water users, including data centers. The ordinance received a first reading at Monday's meeting and would require large developments needing new water infrastructure to fund that build-out, rather than spreading the cost to residential and small business ratepayers. The hearing was the third the council has held on data center reform."
detail: "The ordinance would direct Columbus's water and power utility to develop a distinct rate, charge or fee structure for high-load, high-volume water users, a category that includes data centers alongside other industrial operations. City officials framed the goal as shifting the cost of new water infrastructure, pipes, treatment capacity and related build-out, onto the large users driving the demand rather than spreading it across residential and small business water bills.\n\nCouncil President Shannon Hardin acknowledged competing pressure from both directions on the broader data center debate. Wyche chaired a Public Utilities and Sustainability Committee hearing following the news conference, the third such hearing the council has held on data center policy this year. The package under discussion also touches efficiency incentives for high-volume water users and electronic waste disposal procedures tied to data center equipment.\n\nA speaker representing a site-selection firm told council that each data center job in Franklin County generates about 3.2 additional jobs, a figure cited to argue for continued data center investment even as the council moves toward new fees. The ordinance had its first reading at Monday's council meeting; a second reading and possible vote are expected later in September."
why_it_matters: "If the ordinance passes, any Columbus water customer, including landlords, would be shielded from subsidizing new water infrastructure built specifically for data centers, since the fee structure is designed to put that cost on the high-volume users instead of spreading it across the standard residential and small business rate base."
