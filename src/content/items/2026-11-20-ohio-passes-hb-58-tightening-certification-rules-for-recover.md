title: "Ohio passes HB 58, tightening certification rules for recovery homes"
summary: "The Ohio Senate unanimously passed House Bill 58 this week, sending it to Gov. Mike DeWine for signature. The bill requires recovery homes, transitional housing for people in addiction recovery, to obtain state certification to operate, eliminating the option to certify through an industry trade group instead. It also requires state officials to investigate a complaint against a recovery home within three days and gives local prosecutors more authority to seek court orders closing homes that violate state law."
detail: "HB 58 cleared the Ohio House unanimously in February and the Senate this week, and now goes to Gov. Mike DeWine, who is expected to sign it after an August executive order already put many of its provisions in place on a temporary basis; a permanent change requires the law itself. The bill is a top priority for officials in Scioto County, an epicenter of the opioid epidemic two decades ago that has since seen recovery-home operators proliferate there, according to Signal Cleveland, which reports the county ranks 35th in population statewide but fourth in recovery-home operators. Statewide, the number of recovery homes grew from 356 in 2022 to more than 1,700 this year, DeWine has said. Local officials have described struggling to regulate the fast-growing industry, with some operators found offering substandard conditions that prosecutors have had difficulty shutting down under current law. HB 58 responds by centralizing certification with the state, speeding up complaint investigations to a three-day window, and giving prosecutors clearer authority to seek closure orders."
why_it_matters: "Any Columbus-area landlord or investor leasing a property to a sober-living or recovery-home operator, or weighing that use, will need state certification in place once DeWine signs HB 58, and a single tenant or neighbor complaint can now trigger a state inspection within three days instead of the slower process operators have relied on."
