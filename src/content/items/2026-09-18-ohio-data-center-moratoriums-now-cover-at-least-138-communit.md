title: "Ohio data center moratoriums now cover at least 138 communities"
summary: "At least 138 Ohio municipalities and townships have active moratoriums on data center construction as of September 17, and 23 communities have passed outright bans, according to local government records compiled by the Ohio Capital Journal. A Bowling Green State University poll found 78% of Ohioans support moratoriums until more is known about data centers' effects, while 75% oppose construction in their own community. Voters in four Ohio cities with active moratoriums will decide in November whether to make their bans permanent."
detail: "The count, compiled from local government records and news reports, shows moratoriums concentrated in townships near existing or planned data center campuses, including clusters in Logan, Union and Champaign counties, where regional planning officials reviewed 28 nearly identical proposed bans between April and September. In Knox County, which borders Licking and Delaware counties, at least four townships have proposed bans. Columbus City Council is separately weighing usage-based water and power fees rather than a ban.\n\nA moratorium is a temporary construction pause that any municipality or township can enact quickly; a permanent ban requires a longer zoning process available only to communities with zoning authority. Some rural Ohio townships have no zoning at all, leaving bans to the county level, as in Butler County's six-month moratorium covering six of its 13 townships.\n\nCapital University economist Saurav Roychoudhury said developers increasingly prioritize de-risked sites with less community opposition, and that moratoriums lasting six months to a year can push a project to another state. He added that Ohio's state tax incentives for data centers are currently on hold, and that lawmakers from both parties are pushing to end them permanently. Voters in four cities with active moratoriums will decide this November whether to convert them into permanent bans."
