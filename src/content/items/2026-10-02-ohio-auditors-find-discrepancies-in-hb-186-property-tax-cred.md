title: "Ohio auditors find discrepancies in HB 186 property tax credit"
summary: "The County Auditors' Association of Ohio said its members found substantial discrepancies in how the state reimbursed school districts for House Bill 186's Inflation Cap Credit, a property tax credit meant to protect homeowners in the roughly 78% of Ohio school districts sitting at the 20-mill floor from tax increases when home values rise."
detail: "House Bill 186 created Ohio's Inflation Cap Credit to limit property tax increases when home values rise quickly, but the protection only applies in the roughly 78% of Ohio school districts sitting at the 20-mill floor, where tax bills otherwise track rising valuations directly. The law, effective March 20, 2026, caps how much a taxpayer's bill can increase and requires the state to reimburse the affected school districts for tax years 2025 and 2026 for the difference. The County Auditors' Association of Ohio (CAAO), working with the Ohio Department of Taxation, found the reimbursements did not line up with the credits actually granted: some districts were overpaid by as much as $54 million relative to the credits they issued, others were underpaid by roughly $15 million, and some received no reimbursement at all. CAAO President Cindy Waugh said the association's analysis found the state reimbursed districts nearly $39 million more in total than the credits given to taxpayers, and stressed the discrepancies are in the reimbursement calculations, not in the credits homeowners actually received."
why_it_matters: "Columbus-area landlords with rental property in a 20-mill floor school district should already be seeing the Inflation Cap Credit limit this year's property tax increase, but the reimbursement dispute between the state and school districts could put pressure on lawmakers to revisit how the credit is funded."
