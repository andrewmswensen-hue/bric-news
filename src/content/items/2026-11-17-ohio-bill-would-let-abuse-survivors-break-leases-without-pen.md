title: "Ohio bill would let abuse survivors break leases without penalty"
summary: "State Rep. Melanie Miller testified before the Ohio House Judiciary Committee on House Bill 855, the Ohio Safe Homes Act, which she is sponsoring with Rep. Michele Grim. The bill would let tenants who are victims of rape, domestic violence, dating violence, or a sexually oriented offense end their lease early without the usual termination penalty, and request a lock change at limited cost if they submit documentation. It also sets a tax credit for landlords whose tenants use the provision, while preserving landlords' ability to recover property damage under existing law."
detail: "House Bill 855, called the Ohio Safe Homes Act, is sponsored jointly by Rep. Melanie Miller (R-Ashland) and Rep. Michele Grim (D-Toledo). Miller gave sponsor testimony before the House Judiciary Committee describing the bill's two main protections for tenants who are victims of rape, attempted rape, domestic violence, dating violence, abuse, or a sexually oriented offense. First, qualifying tenants could terminate a rental agreement early without the lease-break penalties landlords can currently charge. Second, they could request that a landlord replace the locks on their unit, provided they submit required documentation, with the bill setting limits on what landlords can charge for that work. The legislation spells out which forms of documentation qualify and the procedures a landlord must follow for the lock change. To offset the lost rent from an early termination, the bill would give landlords a tax credit when a tenant uses the provision, and it preserves a landlord's existing right to recover property damage. The bill remains in the House Judiciary Committee and has not yet had a vote."
why_it_matters: "If this bill passes, a Columbus-area landlord could lose a tenant mid-lease with no early-termination fee whenever that tenant documents abuse or a sexual offense, though the proposed state tax credit would offset some of the lost rent, and landlords would need a documented process for swapping locks on short notice."
