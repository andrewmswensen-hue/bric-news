title: "166-Unit Affordable Housing Project Breaks Ground at Columbus State"
summary: "Woda Cooper Cos. and Columbus State Community Partners broke ground in August on Opportunity Pointe, a 166-unit affordable housing development on the Columbus State Community College campus downtown. The project includes two efficiency, 97 one-bedroom, 49 two-bedroom and 18 three-bedroom units for renters earning 30 to 80 percent of area median income, plus 20 additional one-bedroom market-rate units reserved for Columbus State's own supportive student housing use."
detail: "Opportunity Pointe is a public-private partnership between Woda Cooper Cos., a Columbus-based affordable housing developer, and Columbus State Community Partners, the college's real estate affiliate. The development broke ground in August on the Columbus State campus in downtown Columbus and will include a community room, package room, activity room, fitness room, covered outdoor patio and playground. Jewish Family Services will provide resident programming including counseling, job training and emergency financial assistance. Financing includes low-income housing tax credits allocated by the Ohio Housing Finance Agency, an equity investment from affordable housing investor OCCH, a construction loan from affiliate Ohio Capital Finance Corp., and a first mortgage from Rocky Mountain Community Reinvestment Corp. The city of Columbus holds a second, soft mortgage on the project and provided a 15-year property tax abatement, while the Columbus-Franklin County Finance Authority authorized a sales tax exemption on construction goods and materials for both project phases. Woda Construction is general contractor and Woda Management & Real Estate will handle leasing and property management. Columbus Mayor Andrew J. Ginther said the project reflects an effort to ensure the city's growth creates housing opportunity near jobs, education and transit."
why_it_matters: "Opportunity Pointe's 20 market-rate, Columbus State-only units sit outside the affordable set-aside entirely, so a downtown Columbus landlord renting to students should note this as a new, subsidized supportive-housing alternative aimed specifically at that population rather than a straightforward addition to the general market-rate supply."
