title: "Maryland Landlord Pays $100,000 to Settle Housing Voucher Bias Claims"
summary: "A Salisbury, Maryland landlord, Disney Properties LLC, will pay $100,000 and change its rental practices under a settlement with the state attorney general, which found the company, managing about 100 Eastern Shore rental properties, likely refused Housing Choice Voucher holders and required pay stubs that screened out tenants relying on disability or retirement income."
detail: "Maryland Attorney General Anthony Brown announced the settlement after the office's Civil Rights Division found reasonable cause to believe Disney Properties LLC discriminated against applicants using Housing Choice Vouchers, commonly called Section 8, at its roughly 100 rental properties on the Eastern Shore. Investigators said the company's rental applications required three to four current pay stubs, a standard that screened out tenants relying on rental assistance, disability benefits, or retirement income rather than a paycheck.\n\nUnder the settlement, Disney Properties must accept vouchers and other lawful income sources at every covered property, and when assessing income for subsidized applicants, consider only the portion of rent the tenant pays rather than the full rent amount. The company must drop pay-stub and physical Social Security card requirements where reasonable alternatives exist, revise its applications, ads, and leasing materials, adopt individualized criminal-history review, and centralize application-denial decisions. It must also waive fees for up to 100 voucher applicants and train staff on fair housing law.\n\nThe office said Maryland's Housing Opportunities Made Equal Act bars income-source discrimination statewide, with additional screening limits that took effect October 1. The agreement runs two years, with compliance reports due to the Civil Rights Division every six months."
why_it_matters: "Columbus already bars refusing applicants over how they pay rent under its 2021 Housing for All ordinance, so a landlord here who sets pay-stub-only income rules or a blanket no-voucher policy carries the same kind of fair housing exposure this settlement shows, just enforced locally as a misdemeanor rather than a state civil penalty."
