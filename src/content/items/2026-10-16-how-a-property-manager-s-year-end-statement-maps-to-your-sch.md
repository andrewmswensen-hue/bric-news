detail: "A property manager's year-end statement is the document a CPA builds Schedule E from, and RL Property Management's guide walks through how its line items map to IRS filing categories. The statement should show twelve months of activity sorted by tax category: gross rent collected before fees, management and leasing fees, itemized repairs, capital improvements listed separately, and reserve account activity. On Schedule E (Form 1040, Part I), gross rent belongs on Line 3, which should reconcile to the owner's Form 1099-MISC; management fees go on Line 11; repairs on Line 14; and capital improvements are depreciated over 27.5 years on Line 18 rather than deducted the year they're paid.\n\nTwo common errors trip up owners, according to the guide. Reporting net deposits instead of gross rent on Line 3 understates income while double-counting expenses already deducted elsewhere. And lumping a full roof replacement in with routine repairs overstates deductions in the year paid, since the IRS treats a full replacement as a depreciable improvement rather than a repair; patching a section of a roof is a repair, replacing the whole roof is not. Three IRS safe harbors can let some costs that would otherwise be capitalized be deducted immediately: a $2,500-per-invoice de minimis election, a routine maintenance safe harbor, and a small-taxpayer safe harbor for buildings with a basis under $1 million.\n\nA separate filing change affects 2026 statements: Public Law 119-21 raised the Form 1099-MISC threshold for rent a manager pays over to an owner from $600 to $2,000, effective for payments made after Dec. 31, 2025. Security deposits held in trust and expected to be returned are not taxable income under IRS Publication 527, but a deposit kept for damage or applied as last month's rent is."
why_it_matters: "The 1099-MISC rent threshold tripled to $2,000 for 2026 payments, so a Columbus owner receiving smaller monthly disbursements from a manager may not get a 1099 at all this filing season, a change worth flagging to a CPA before assuming last year's reporting pattern still applies."
