detail: "A new report from the Columbus-based firm Scioto Analysis finds that data center development added nearly 100,000 jobs and a net social benefit of about $62 billion to Ohio's economy between 2013 and 2026, but that the economic payoff shrinks with each additional facility once a county's supporting infrastructure is already built out. Ohio has 257 data centers, the fifth most of any state, according to Data Center Map. Scioto Analysis principal Rob Moore said counties with existing data centers see sustained higher employment, but added facilities draw more electricity and water, raising utility costs and particulate and nitrogen oxide emissions that erode public health benefits over time. The report projects that under current regulations, rising utility and environmental costs will soon nearly offset the economic gains from new data centers in Ohio. The findings arrive as data centers have become a flashpoint in the U.S. Senate race, with former Sen. Sherrod Brown criticizing Sen. Jon Husted's past support for data center projects. Husted has since backed legislation requiring data centers to generate their own electricity rather than draw on the shared grid; the bill failed in the Senate after Democratic opposition."
