title: "Columbus council weighs tax deal for $23.76M senior housing rehab"
summary: "Columbus City Council advanced a payment-in-lieu-of-taxes agreement Tuesday that would let Gene B. Glick Co. pay the city at least $68,000 a year, instead of its current $160,000 property tax bill, for 30 years while it rehabilitates Fairington of Columbus, a 136-unit, Section 8-supported senior housing community built in 1978, according to The Republic News. The $23.76 million project includes low-income housing tax credits, tax-exempt bonds and a sponsor loan."
detail: "The council voted 7-0 on first reading Tuesday to advance the PILOT ordinance for Gene B. Glick Co.'s planned rehabilitation of Fairington of Columbus, an age-restricted affordable senior community whose 136 units are all supported by a HUD Section 8 Housing Assistance Payments contract, according to The Republic News. The $23.76 million project, including $6.9 million in construction costs, is financed with about $12.5 million in mortgage tax-exempt bonds, roughly $7 million in low-income housing tax credit investor equity, a $2.5 million sponsor loan and $1.7 million in deferred fees. Under the negotiated PILOT terms, Glick's annual payment to the city starts at $68,000, up from an initial $40,800 offer, and rises 3% a year for 30 years, replacing the property's current $160,000 tax liability; the city's community development director said that would still send about $3.2 million into Columbus's affordable housing fund over the agreement's life. Glick will sign a land use restriction agreement at closing requiring the property to stay affordable for at least 40 years combined with other program commitments. The rehab covers kitchens, bathrooms, mechanical systems, elevators, exteriors and accessibility upgrades, with residents temporarily relocated at the company's expense. Second reading could come as soon as Nov. 4."
