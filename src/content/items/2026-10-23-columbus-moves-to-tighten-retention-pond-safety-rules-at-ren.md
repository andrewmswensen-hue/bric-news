title: "Columbus moves to tighten retention pond safety rules at rentals"
summary: "Columbus City Council is set to vote Nov. 2 on emergency legislation adding new requirements to the city's 2024 retention pond safety ordinance after two toddlers drowned in apartment-complex ponds this year, according to a WCMH report carried by AOL. The changes would require multiunit complexes to deliver pond-danger notices as a standalone document, posted visibly, rather than inside a lease or newsletter, and to maintain a 15-foot buffer of unmowed vegetation around pond edges."
detail: "Columbus's existing retention pond ordinance, passed in 2024, requires multiunit residential complexes with ponds to post at least four warning signs, place life preservers every 300 feet, and notify residents annually of the danger, and it lets the city's Building and Zoning Services department declare a pond a public nuisance if it has been the site of a drowning or lacks required safety features, forcing the owner to make changes. The emergency amendment up for a Nov. 2 vote would require the annual danger notice to be delivered as its own document and posted in a common area, rather than folded into a lease or newsletter, and would bar mowing or cutting vegetation within 15 feet of a pond's edge to create a physical barrier. Council President Pro Tempore Rob Dorans said the changes follow the drownings of two 2-year-olds this year, one at Polaris Place and one at Bayside Commons. The city's zoning department said about 110 ponds in Columbus are subject to the requirements and that it has issued violation notices to 28 properties since the 2024 ordinance passed, with 23 now in compliance and five still being worked through."
