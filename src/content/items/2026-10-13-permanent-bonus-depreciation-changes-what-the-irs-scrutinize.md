detail: "BiggerPockets outlines how the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), signed July 4, 2025, changed real estate tax rules investors rely on. The law made 100% bonus depreciation permanent for qualified property acquired and placed in service after Jan. 19, 2025, ending the prior schedule that would have phased the benefit down to zero by 2027. It also made the qualified business income deduction permanent and changed the SALT cap along with Low Income Housing Tax Credit and Opportunity Zone provisions. The IRS issued Notice 2026-11 in January 2026 to clarify the elections available under the new permanent bonus depreciation rule. Cost segregation, which reclassifies parts of a building such as flooring and site improvements into shorter five, seven or 15-year depreciation schedules instead of the standard 27.5-year residential schedule, remains legal, but the IRS has disallowed studies based on generic templates rather than property-specific engineering analysis. BiggerPockets points to 2026 audit trend reporting showing fewer total audits but more document-intensive review of Schedule E returns and Real Estate Professional Status claims, and cites an example of an investor with 12 rental properties facing a $180,000 proposed assessment after the IRS disallowed $520,000 in claimed cost segregation deductions from a prior year."
