title: "Ohio Supreme Court orders Trenton to let voters decide on data center ban"
summary: "The Ohio Supreme Court ruled unanimously Sept. 1 that the city of Trenton must place a proposed charter amendment banning large data centers before voters in a special election, after the Butler County Board of Elections certified 336 valid signatures, more than the 128 required under state law. The amendment would bar construction of data centers requiring more than 25 megawatts within city limits. It would not affect an existing Amazon Web Services campus already under construction on 141 acres in Trenton, or halt AWS's planned second project on about 600 acres in nearby Madison and St."
detail: "The petition drive was organized by Woodsdale and Trenton Environmental Resistance (W.A.T.E.R.), a group formed after residents raised concerns about the AWS data center campus and a planned second, larger project nearby. The petition was submitted in July, but Trenton City Council voted against advancing it to the ballot, arguing the threshold should be based on the number of registered voters, which the council said required roughly 820 signatures rather than the 128-signature threshold the group used.\n\nThe Ohio Supreme Court disagreed in a unanimous ruling issued Sept. 1, and ordered the special election to proceed. The proposed amendment would prohibit any data center requiring more than 25 megawatts of power from being built within Trenton city limits.\n\nThe ruling would not undo the current, roughly 900,000-square-foot AWS project already under construction on 141 acres, since it predates the amendment. But it could affect AWS's separate proposal for an 18-building, roughly 600-acre campus spanning Madison and St. Clair townships if any portion falls within city limits and if voters approve the ban. Organizer Barry Blankenship said the group's next step is educating residents on the ballot language before the special election."
why_it_matters: "This is the same referendum mechanism Grove City residents are using against data centers in Franklin County: a citizen petition forcing a binding public vote on a specific project type. The Supreme Court's ruling on Trenton's signature threshold gives Ohio courts a template for resolving the signature-count fights that will likely shape whether Grove City's own November ballot measure survives a legal challenge."
