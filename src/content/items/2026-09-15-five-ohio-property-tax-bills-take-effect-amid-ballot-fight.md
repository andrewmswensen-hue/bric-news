detail: "Five companion bills reworking Ohio's property tax system took effect between December 2025 and March 2026, according to the Ohio Capital Journal. Together they change how fast a property's taxable valuation can grow, adjust how the state's 20 mill floor, a formula limiting how much some local levies can shrink as values rise, is calculated, and revise who qualifies for the homestead exemption available to some homeowners. Lawmakers described the package as the biggest rewrite of the state's property tax system in decades. At the same time, a separate citizen campaign is collecting signatures for a ballot measure that would abolish property taxes in Ohio outright, rather than adjust the existing formula. Both efforts follow years of rising property tax bills driven by statewide reappraisals, which reset home values and can push tax bills higher even when local levy rates do not change. The report places the current debate against a longer backdrop: the Ohio Supreme Court ruled the state's property tax reliant school funding system unconstitutional four times between 1997 and 2002, and by 2003 had stopped enforcing its own rulings after the legislature did not replace the funding model. That underlying structure, not addressed by the new bills, still ties most school funding to local property values."
why_it_matters: "None of the five bills that took effect this year cap how much a rental property's own valuation can rise, so investors should expect this year's reappraisal cycle to keep driving tax bills higher even as the political fight over property taxes continues in Columbus."
