detail: "Columbia Gas of Ohio filed a preliminary base rate increase proposal with the Public Utilities Commission of Ohio on September 18, its second rate case in five years after a 2021 filing that sought more than $220 million and was ultimately approved at $68 million. Under the new proposal, the residential base rate, which customers pay regardless of how much gas they use, would rise from current levels to $68.05 per month in 2028, $72.92 in 2029, and $77.12 in 2030. The base rate covers only the utility's local delivery infrastructure and does not include the cost of the gas itself or transport charges, which are billed separately and fluctuate with market prices. Columbia Gas said in a statement that the increase would fund replacement of aging infrastructure, system monitoring technology, and emergency response capability, and that costs for contractors, pipe, and materials have risen since its last rate case. The utility serves nearly 1.5 million customers across 61 of Ohio's 88 counties. The Ohio Consumers' Counsel said it plans to intervene in the case and assemble a legal team to review the request, and encouraged customers to participate in public hearings once scheduled. PUCO has up to 180 days to review Columbia Gas' formal application, including public hearings, once it is filed; a final decision is unlikely before summer 2027. A separate legal challenge to Columbia Gas' 2021 rate increase was dismissed by the Ohio Supreme Court in June."
why_it_matters: "A landlord covering gas heat for tenants, or building gas costs into a lease, would see the minimum monthly charge per unit climb by about $19 total by 2030 even in units that use little gas, before any change in the per-unit gas commodity price."
