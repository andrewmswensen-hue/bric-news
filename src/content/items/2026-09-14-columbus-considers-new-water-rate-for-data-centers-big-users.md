title: "Columbus Considers New Water Rate for Data Centers, Big Users"
summary: "Columbus City Council is weighing a new \"peaking rate\" that would add a surcharge to the water bills of its largest industrial customers, including data centers, chip fabrication and food and beverage plants. Columbus Water and Power says the charge would recover the cost of building and maintaining the larger pipes big users need on standby, even when they are not using full capacity. The fee would not apply to residential customers."
detail: "Columbus Water and Power is proposing a new \"peaking rate\" aimed at the city's largest industrial water customers, including data centers, chip fabrication plants, and brewing, beverage and food processing facilities. Utility director Kristen Atha said high-volume users often require large-diameter pipes, in some cases 12 inches, to be ready to deliver a full supply on demand, even though they do not draw that much water every day. The proposed charge would recover the cost of building and maintaining that standby capacity, which the utility currently spreads across its regular rate paid by all customers, including residential ratepayers. Columbus Water and Power says it operates on a cost-of-service basis with no profit built in. An ordinance directing the utility to bring a specific peaking-rate proposal to council, developed with the Utility Advisory Board, had its first reading at this week's council meeting, with a target of returning a full proposal by the end of October. Councilmember Christopher Wyche, who chairs the council's Public Utilities and Sustainability Committee, said the goal is to make large-scale users pay their share of infrastructure costs rather than passing them to residential and small business customers. The change would not affect typical residential or small business water bills."
why_it_matters: "This charge targets only the city's largest industrial water users, so a landlord's standard residential or small multifamily water account would not see this fee, but it signals Columbus is willing to build new rate tiers rather than spread rising infrastructure costs evenly across all ratepayers."
