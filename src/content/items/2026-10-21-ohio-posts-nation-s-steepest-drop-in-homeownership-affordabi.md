detail: "The National Housing Conference's report, drawing on its Paycheck to Paycheck database and median income data across 390 metropolitan areas nationwide, found that Ohio had the largest loss of homeownership affordability of any state between 2020 and 2025. Statewide, home prices rose 121% over that five year span, while the share of employed Ohioans able to afford a home with a 10% down payment dropped 40.97 percentage points, according to the report. The squeeze extends to renters: the income required to afford a two-bedroom apartment rose 39% across Ohio, with Cincinnati seeing an outsized 49% increase. The report notes that workers in middle income occupations, not just low wage jobs, have been priced out in markets nationwide, citing billing clerks and social workers who could afford homes in 2020 but not by 2025. Julia Wright of the Cuyahoga Metropolitan Housing Authority said the findings reflect households facing greater challenges reaching stability, and pointed to CMHA's Family Self-Sufficiency program as one local response. The report lands amid a contested Ohio governor's race in which both candidates, Democrat Amy Acton and Republican Vivek Ramaswamy, have cited housing affordability as a priority, and follows new Census data showing new residential construction fell nationally in September."
