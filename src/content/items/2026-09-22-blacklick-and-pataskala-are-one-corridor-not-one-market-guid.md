title: "Blacklick and Pataskala Are One Corridor, Not One Market, Guide Says"
summary: "RL Property Management's 2026 guide distinguishes Blacklick, an unincorporated Jefferson Township community in Franklin County, from Pataskala, an incorporated Licking County city, despite shared \"Blacklick, OH\" mailing addresses spanning five school districts. The split determines school district, tax bill and, starting Oct. 1, 2026, whether a property must register with the city of Columbus under Chapter 4515. Three-bedroom rents in Blacklick ran roughly $1,945 to $2,445 in late August 2026, and RLPM places Blacklick in a 7 to 10 percent cap rate band alongside Whitehall and Eastmoor."
detail: "RLPM's guide separates two communities that get lumped together because they share a commuter corridor along East Broad Street and Waggoner Road east of I-270, near the New Albany employment cluster. Blacklick is not an incorporated city: it is a mailing address covering parts of Columbus, Reynoldsburg, Gahanna and unincorporated Jefferson Township in Franklin County, spanning five different school districts. Pataskala, by contrast, is an incorporated city in Licking County, enlarged by its 1996 merger with Lima Township. That legal distinction decides more than which school a renter's child attends. Blacklick addresses inside Columbus city limits must register under Columbus City Code Chapter 4515 between Oct. 1 and Dec. 31, 2026, at $15 per unit; township and Pataskala addresses carry no such requirement. On rents, RLPM found three-bedroom Blacklick homes running roughly $1,945 to $2,445 in late August 2026, and it places the area in a 7 to 10 percent cap rate band alongside older, lower-basis pockets like Whitehall and Eastmoor, not the newer $400,000 subdivisions nearby. About 72 percent of housing in the 43004 ZIP code was built in 2000 or later, compared with a median construction year of 1998 in Pataskala, a gap that shapes maintenance costs and turnover. The guide also flags that Intel's New Albany chip fabrication plant, a driver of demand expectations for the corridor, is now targeted for 2031 with a slower pace than originally announced, and cautions investors to underwrite purchases on today's rents rather than on anticipated Intel-driven growth."
why_it_matters: "An investor evaluating a Blacklick property inside Columbus city limits needs to budget for the $15-per-unit rental registration that opens Oct. 1, 2026, a requirement that does not apply to an otherwise similar property just across the line in Jefferson Township or Pataskala, so the exact parcel boundary changes the paperwork, not just the school district."
