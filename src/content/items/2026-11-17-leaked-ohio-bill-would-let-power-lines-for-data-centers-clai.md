title: "Leaked Ohio Bill Would Let Power Lines for Data Centers Claim Private Land"
summary: "A draft Ohio bill obtained by News 5 Cleveland would redefine public utility to include any agency holding a Power Siting Board certificate for an electric transmission line or gas pipeline, a change legal experts say could let transmission developers use eminent domain to secure land for data center power projects. The bill follows this month's elections, in which Ohio voters banned or restricted data centers in at least 14 communities, including every Central Ohio jurisdiction with a measure on the ballot."
detail: "The draft legislation, obtained by News 5 Cleveland's Statehouse bureau, would expand Ohio's legal definition of public utility to include any agency holding a Power Siting Board certificate of environmental compatibility and public need for an electric transmission line or gas pipeline. Currently that designation applies mainly to municipal utilities, electric cooperatives, and FERC-certified entities. Legal experts told the station the change could let a transmission developer invoke eminent domain to acquire land needed to power or site a data center, even without being the data center operator itself.\n\nThe Ohio Business Roundtable, a Statehouse trade group, recommended in June that lawmakers extend possession authority to energy infrastructure projects once a court establishes public use and necessity, a deposit-and-build model the group says 45 other states already use. The bill would also change the appraisal process so construction could begin while a landowner's compensation is still being litigated, rather than waiting for the case to resolve.\n\nThe Ohio Farm Bureau opposes the change, arguing it would let developers take land for what it called a corporate, private, for-profit purpose without first resolving compensation in court. The bill's timing follows a Nov. 3 election in which Ohio voters banned or restricted data centers in at least 14 communities, according to News 5 Cleveland."
why_it_matters: "Any Columbus-area landowner near a planned transmission line or substation upgrade should watch this bill: if it passes, a line built to serve a data center could carry the same eminent domain authority currently reserved for public utilities, ahead of the usual compensation fight."
