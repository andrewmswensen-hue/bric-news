detail: "The Ohio Supreme Court ruled Sept. 1 that Trenton City Council violated state law when it rejected a citizen petition seeking a charter amendment to ban new data center construction, the Cool Down reported, citing the Cincinnati Enquirer. The court ordered Mayor Ryan Perry and the council to certify the petition and forward it to the Butler County Board of Elections; because it missed the November ballot deadline, the question will instead go to voters in a special election held 60 to 120 days after certification. The dispute centered on how to count the Ohio Constitution's requirement that at least 10% of a city's electors support a charter amendment petition. Residents argued the threshold should be based on turnout in Trenton's last municipal election, requiring 128 signatures; the city argued it should be based on all eligible voters, requiring 820. The justices sided with residents, finding the council had disregarded applicable law, though they declined to award attorney's fees. Trenton, about 38 miles north of Cincinnati, already has two data center projects in the pipeline: a 141-acre Prologis facility and an 18-building complex Amazon has discussed building outside city limits. The ruling adds Trenton to a growing list of Ohio communities, including several townships and Dayton, weighing bans or restrictions on data center development."
