detail: "The average rate on a 30-year fixed mortgage crossed 7% on Thursday, reaching 7.07%, the highest level since May 2025, according to Mortgage News Daily data reported by CNBC. That is a 10 basis point jump from the prior day. Mortgage rates loosely track the 10-year Treasury yield, which rose as oil prices surged, overshadowing a Producer Price Index report showing wholesale prices up 0.4% in August, in line with expectations. Mortgage News Daily chief operating officer Matthew Graham called it 'a rough couple of days for the bond market,' pointing to a Treasury buyback announcement and the overnight oil price spike. Rates have been climbing since hitting a low of 5.99% the day before the Iran war started. The increase has a direct cost: for a buyer financing a $430,000 home, close to the national median price, with a 30-year fixed loan and 20% down, the monthly principal and interest payment is now $244 higher than at the end of February. Homebuilder stocks were already trading lower Thursday after a separate report showed existing home sales falling even as prices rose and supply increased, a combination that signals buyers are pulling back faster than sellers are adjusting."
