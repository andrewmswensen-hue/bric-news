detail: "Marysville's rental demand is tied closely to employment rather than commuting, RL Property Management's guide says. Honda's Marysville Auto Plant employs about 4,400 people and has invested $5.5 billion cumulatively since 1982, building the Accord and Acura Integra; Honda canceled plans for Ohio-built 0 Series electric vehicles in March 2026 but kept gas and hybrid production running. Marysville and Union County have both grown about 17 to 18% since April 2020, among the fastest rates in Ohio, with a median household income of $92,619 in the city and $112,322 countywide. Rent trackers vary by source, but Zillow put typical Marysville rent at $1,510 in August 2026, down 0.8% year over year, nearly matching Columbus's $1,495 citywide average; RentCafe showed a steeper 6.8% annual decline to $1,398. Single-family houses rent higher, from $1,725 to $2,700, and the guide's illustrative example shows a $2,100-a-month three-bedroom house against a $368,459 purchase price yielding about 6.8% gross before expenses and fees. New supply, including the 240-unit VC Center redevelopment and a planned 502-unit Foundry 33 project, is helping push rents down slightly for smaller units, though the guide says houses face less direct competition from new Class A apartments. Union County's six-year reappraisal, last done in 2019, produced a tentative 27.14% increase in property values for tax year 2025. The guide also notes Columbus-specific landlord rules, including its rental registry, Pay to Stay ordinance and rent-increase notice requirement, stop at the city limits, so Marysville landlords follow Ohio's statewide landlord-tenant law instead."
