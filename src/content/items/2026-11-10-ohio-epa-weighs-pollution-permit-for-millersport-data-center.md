detail: "The Ohio EPA is reviewing a draft air-pollution permit for the proposed Fairfield Energy Center, a 1,350-megawatt power plant that would serve a $2.1 billion hyperscale data center campus Vantage Data Centers has proposed on 450 acres near Millersport, upwind from Buckeye Lake, according to the Reporting Project. Advanced Power Services of Boston, which applied for the permit on the plant's behalf, estimates the facility would emit roughly 2,164 tons of air pollutants and more than 7.2 million tons of greenhouse gases a year; Ohio EPA documents describe the greenhouse gas figure as \"not applicable\" in the permitting process.\n\nThe draft permit states that Ohio's \"best available technology\" requirement, which applies when a pollutant such as volatile organic compounds or particulate matter exceeds 10 tons a year, does not apply to each of the plant's 40 individual turbines and diesel engines, since their individual emissions fall under that threshold. An Ohio EPA spokesperson told the Reporting Project the facility as a whole is still classified as a major emitting source required to use the technology, and that some smaller units are exempted under Ohio Revised Code 3704.03, a standard the agency said it applies statewide.\n\nA public hearing on the permit was held Nov. 10 at Millersport Elementary School; the Ohio EPA is accepting written comments through Nov. 12."
why_it_matters: "The Fairfield Energy Center sits just east of Licking County, inside the same data center-driven power and water demand corridor where Grove City, Granville, Pataskala, Sunbury and other Central Ohio communities passed new development limits on the Nov. 3 ballot; an investor tracking land near Buckeye Lake should watch whether the permit's emissions terms draw the kind of pushback that has already reshaped zoning in nearby jurisdictions."
