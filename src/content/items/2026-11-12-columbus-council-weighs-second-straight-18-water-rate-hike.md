detail: "Columbus City Council is scheduled to vote Nov. 23 on an 18% water rate increase, the second consecutive year the city has sought that size of hike, the Columbus Dispatch reported. Combined with proposed sewer and stormwater rate changes, the average Columbus household's annual water, sewer and stormwater bill would rise from $1,247 to $1,371, an increase of about $124, or roughly 10%. Suburban customers would see a steeper combined increase of about 11%, with the average suburban bill rising from $1,370 to $1,523. City officials say the increase would generate at least $20 million in additional revenue to help fund a roughly $2 billion fourth water plant, which received no bids when the city sought construction proposals this year, and to address about $1 billion in required lead service line replacement under a federal mandate. Two members of the city's seven-member Utilities Advisory Board, representing low-income residents and seniors, voted against the 18% water increase in an advisory vote; the board's vote does not bind the council. City officials acknowledged the water, sewer and power discount programs for income-qualified households remain underused and said they continue to struggle extending those discounts to renters in apartment buildings billed through a single master meter rather than individual units."
why_it_matters: "An owner of a master-metered apartment building should expect the full rate increase to land on the property's utility bill with no path for tenants to apply for the city's income-qualified discount directly, since Columbus officials say that gap for master-metered renters has not been solved, which pushes the roughly 10% to 11% cost increase onto the owner's operating budget instead."
