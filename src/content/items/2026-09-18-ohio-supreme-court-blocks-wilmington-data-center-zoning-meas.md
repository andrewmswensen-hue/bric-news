title: "Ohio Supreme Court Blocks Wilmington Data Center Zoning Measure From Ballot"
summary: "The Ohio Supreme Court ruled that a citizen-initiated zoning ordinance regulating data centers in Wilmington cannot appear on the Nov. 3 ballot because it improperly created a private right for residents to sue data center operators over violations, which is beyond a municipality's ballot-initiative power. The court cited its 2018 ruling in a similar Columbus case. Justice Patrick Fischer dissented, arguing boards of elections should not be deciding the legality of ballot measures before voters weigh in."
detail: "A citizen group had petitioned to add a new zoning chapter regulating data centers in Wilmington, setting standards for noise, lighting, emissions, water use and generator testing, enforceable in part through a provision letting residents sue data center operators directly, with fines of $5,000 to $10,000 per day. The Clinton County Board of Elections refused to certify the measure for the ballot after the city law director argued the private right to sue exceeded what a municipality may enact by initiative. The petitioners sought a court order forcing the board to place it on the ballot. In a per curiam opinion, the Ohio Supreme Court sided with the board, relying on its 2018 ruling in a Columbus case involving a proposed ban on hydrocarbon extraction, which similarly created a new private cause of action beyond municipal authority. Justice Patrick Fischer dissented, writing that boards of elections should not be making legal determinations about a proposed ordinance before voters have a chance to approve it, and that only courts should resolve such questions once a law is enacted. The ruling leaves Wilmington without a citizen-driven zoning check on data centers for now."
why_it_matters: "The ruling sets a statewide limit on how far a local zoning ballot initiative can go, meaning any Columbus or Franklin County group seeking to regulate data centers or other land uses by citizen petition cannot attach a private right to sue over violations; enforcement has to run through the city itself."
