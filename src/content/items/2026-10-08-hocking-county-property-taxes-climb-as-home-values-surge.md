detail: "Hocking County Auditor Chris Robers said property tax increases hitting homeowners are driven mainly by rising assessed values rather than new levies, according to the Logan Daily News. Ohio sets a property's taxable value at 35% of its appraised value, which is then multiplied by the tax rate approved through voted levies. Robers' office tracks county property values two ways: an overall, state-approved adjustment every three years based on recent sales data, and a closer review of individual properties every six years to account for improvements or disrepair that change a specific property's value. Robers said home prices in Hocking County, like much of the state and country, have risen sharply over the past ten years, following a national pattern that saw values fall during the 2007-2008 housing crash before resuming a long upward climb. Because many of the county's current levies are renewals that hold the millage rate steady, Robers said the valuation increase, not new taxing authority, explains most of the jump homeowners are seeing on their bills."
why_it_matters: "An owner of rental property in Hocking County should expect the next triennial reappraisal to raise their tax bill even if no new levy appears on the ballot, since valuation gains, not new levies, are driving most of the current increase."
