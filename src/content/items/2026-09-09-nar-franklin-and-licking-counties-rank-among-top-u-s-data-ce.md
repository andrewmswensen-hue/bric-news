detail: "A National Association of Realtors report using data from 3,200 U.S. counties found that 92% of counties have no data centers at all, while 10 counties hold 42% of the nationwide total, Commercial Observer reported. Franklin and Licking counties in central Ohio, together with California's Silicon Valley, account for about 10% of national data center development, behind only Loudoun and Prince William counties in Northern Virginia, which alone hold 19%. Counties with more than 10 data centers had a median home value of $431,750, compared with $174,500 in counties without any, and saw home values climb 95% between 2014 and 2024. Median household income in the cluster counties was $89,000, versus a $64,000 average elsewhere, and employment grew 16%, compared with 2% growth in non-cluster counties. NAR chief economist Lawrence Yun cautioned the numbers describe county-level trends, not the effect on an individual home next to a facility, and that other factors, including higher incomes and more college-educated residents, are also tied to stronger housing demand in these areas. A separate NAR survey of more than 2,300 agents found 61% of respondents said clients associate data centers with higher energy costs, and residential electricity rates rose 21.4% in high-cluster counties from 2020 to 2024, compared with 15.4% in counties without data centers."
