title: "What Ohio law actually requires before a landlord can enter a rental"
summary: "RL Property Management explains Ohio's landlord entry rules under ORC 5321.04. The statute names a specific number: 24 hours is presumed reasonable notice before a landlord enters an occupied unit, though that presumption can be challenged with evidence either way. Entry must happen at a reasonable time and for a legitimate purpose, such as a repair, inspection, or showing, with an emergency as the only exception. A landlord who enters improperly risks damages, an injunction, attorney's fees, or lease termination by the tenant."
detail: "Ohio Revised Code 5321.04(A)(8) requires a landlord to give a tenant reasonable notice before entering an occupied unit, and states that 24 hours is presumed to be reasonable notice absent evidence to the contrary. That presumption is rebuttable in both directions: a landlord who gives less than 24 hours must be prepared to justify it, and a tenant can argue that even 24 hours' notice was not enough given their schedule, such as someone working overnight shifts facing an early-morning visit. Entry has to occur at a reasonable time and serve a legitimate purpose, including an inspection, a repair, an agreed-upon service, or a showing to a prospective tenant or buyer; the statute's only named exception is an emergency or a situation where giving notice is impracticable. A separate provision, ORC 5321.04(A)(7), bars a landlord from abusing the right of access, while ORC 5321.05(B) puts a matching duty on tenants not to unreasonably withhold consent to a proper entry request. A landlord who enters without proper notice or purpose can face tenant remedies including damages, an injunction, recovery of attorney's fees, or termination of the lease. The article also notes that Columbus's rental registry inspections under Chapter 4515 reach only exteriors, common areas, and shared mechanical systems, not the interior of an occupied unit, so a registry inspection does not substitute for the separate notice a landlord still owes a tenant before entering a leased unit for a repair or a routine walkthrough. The piece stresses that the 24-hour figure is a presumption rather than a fixed rule either side can treat as automatic: a landlord who gives more than 24 hours stands on the safest ground, one who gives exactly 24 hours is following the statute's own default, and one who gives less is arguing against the presumption and should be ready to explain the shorter window if a tenant objects or the entry is later disputed."
why_it_matters: "A Columbus landlord who shows up with same-day notice for a routine repair or a showing is giving less than the 24 hours Ohio law presumes reasonable, which shifts the burden onto the landlord to justify the shorter notice if the tenant pushes back or later sues over the entry."
