title: "Reynoldsburg adopts short-term rental permit, primary residence and three-night rules"
summary: "Reynoldsburg City Council unanimously passed short-term rental regulations in July following resident complaints about parties. Owners must obtain a yearly city permit costing $225, and the property must be the owner's primary residence or owned by them. Guests must book a minimum of three nights, which rules out one- and two-night stays, and occupancy is capped at two people per bedroom. Vehicles are limited to garage capacity plus two in the driveway and two on the street."
detail: "Council President Bhuwan Pyakurel said public safety became the priority after the city received complaints about renters from outside Reynoldsburg holding parties, and that the concern was what those patterns could lead to rather than anything that had already happened in the city. WOSU reported the vote was unanimous. The ordinance sets an annual permit fee of $225 and requires that the property be the owner's primary residence or be owned by the operator, which removes the purely investor-held model from eligibility. A three-night minimum booking eliminates the one- and two-night stays associated with party rentals. Occupancy is limited to two people per bedroom, and the number of vehicles cannot exceed garage capacity plus two in the driveway and two on the street. The city can suspend or revoke a permit over noise violations, failure to follow guest requirements, delinquent city taxes, or guests entering neighboring private property within 500 feet uninvited. WOSU noted that Dublin, Gahanna and Hilliard enforce comparable permit and guest rules, while Upper Arlington, Westerville and Worthington have banned short-term rentals outright."
why_it_matters: "The primary residence requirement is the provision that changes ownership economics: a Reynoldsburg house bought purely to run as a short-term rental no longer qualifies for a permit, and the three-night minimum removes the weekend-stay revenue that supports many of those pro formas. With Upper Arlington, Westerville and Worthington already banning the use outright, the map of where short-term rental strategies still work in Franklin County keeps narrowing."
