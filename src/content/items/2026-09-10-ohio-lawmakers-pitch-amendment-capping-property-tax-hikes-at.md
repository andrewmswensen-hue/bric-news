detail: "State Reps. Jeff LaRe, a Violet Township Republican, and Kevin Miller, a Newark Republican, are pitching a constitutional amendment aimed at slowing the growth of Ohio property tax bills as home values rise, the Columbus Dispatch reported. The proposal would tie the taxable increase in a home's appraised value to the rate of inflation and cap that annual increase at 3%. Any appreciation beyond the 3% cap would be exempted from property tax, but only for owner-occupied homes: the exemption applies as long as the owner, or the owner's spouse, continues to live in the property. Because it is a constitutional amendment, the proposal would need to clear the Ohio legislature before going to voters as a statewide ballot issue, a process that has not yet begun. The measure follows a string of Ohio property tax fights this year as county reappraisals have pushed values, and tax bills, sharply higher in Franklin and other counties. As introduced, the amendment applies to owner-occupied homes rather than rental property, so a landlord's investment units would not qualify for the cap even if the landlord's own residence would."
why_it_matters: "If this reaches the ballot, it would cap tax growth only on a landlord's own primary residence, not rental units, so investors hoping for relief on rental property tax bills would need a separate fix."
