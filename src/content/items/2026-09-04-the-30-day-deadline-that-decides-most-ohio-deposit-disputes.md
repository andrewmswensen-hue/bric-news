title: "The 30-day deadline that decides most Ohio deposit disputes"
summary: "RL Property Management lays out the sequence Columbus landlords should follow after a tenant moves out, anchored on Ohio's deadline under ORC 5321.16(B): the itemized deduction list and any money owed are due within 30 days of lease termination and return of possession, not from when repairs finish. A deposit above the greater of $50 or one month's rent earns 5% annual interest once a tenant has held the unit six months, so a two-month deposit triggers the interest rule. A tenant wrongly denied deposit money can recover it plus matching damages plus attorney's fees."
detail: "Ohio Revised Code 5321.16 gives a landlord 30 days after a rental agreement ends and possession is returned to deliver a written, itemized list of any deductions along with whatever deposit money remains owed. Missing that deadline, or deducting something the landlord cannot support with documentation, exposes the landlord to the withheld amount plus damages equal to that amount plus the tenant's attorney's fees. A separate provision, ORC 5321.16(A), requires 5% annual interest on the portion of a deposit exceeding the greater of $50 or one month's rent, once the tenant has held the unit for six months, meaning a typical two-month security deposit on a Columbus rental triggers the interest requirement automatically. The recommended sequence starts before the tenant leaves: the lease should specify the notice period for ending a month-to-month tenancy (30 days under ORC 5321.17, or seven days for week-to-week), a specific condition standard for surrender, and a forwarding address for the deposit accounting. Vague surrender language, such as requiring only that a tenant \"leave the unit clean,\" tends to produce arguments over what counts as damage beyond normal wear and tear, while specific requirements, such as emptied appliances, patched drywall holes, and returned keys and openers, give both sides something concrete to point to during the move-out inspection. That inspection doubles as the scope document for the unit's turn, letting a landlord move straight from the walkthrough into repair scheduling without a separate round of assessment. Ohio has no statute prescribing how to notice, store, or dispose of belongings a tenant leaves behind, only a prohibition under ORC 5321.15 on a landlord seizing them outright, which leaves the handling of abandoned property as one of the few genuinely open questions in an otherwise deadline-driven process."
why_it_matters: "The 30-day deposit-accounting clock in ORC 5321.16 starts running the day possession is returned, not the day repairs are finished, so a Columbus landlord who waits to finish a turn before mailing the itemized deduction list risks missing the deadline and owing the tenant double the disputed amount plus attorney's fees."
