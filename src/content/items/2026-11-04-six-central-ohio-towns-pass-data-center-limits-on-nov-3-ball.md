title: "Six central Ohio towns pass data center limits on Nov. 3 ballot"
summary: "Voters in six central Ohio communities, Ashville, Granville, Grove City, Pataskala, Sunbury and Urbana, passed local measures restricting or slowing data center development on the Nov. 3 ballot, part of at least 18 such issues statewide. Grove City's charter amendment, passing with 55%, restricts any business using more than 50 contiguous acres, 20 megawatts of power or 500,000 gallons of water a day, building on a 12-month moratorium the city adopted in June."
detail: "At least 18 municipal ballot issues opposing data centers appeared on Ohio's Nov. 3 ballot, and six passed in central Ohio: Ashville, Granville, Grove City, Pataskala, Sunbury and Urbana, spanning Delaware, Franklin, Licking and Pickaway counties, according to the Columbus Dispatch. In Ashville, a Pickaway County village of 5,000, more than 83% of voters backed a referendum to reverse the village council's negotiated terms with EdgeConneX over two data center buildings and a gas-fired power plant, though the result awaits validation by the county board of elections. Grove City's charter amendment passed with 55% and restricts any business occupying more than 50 contiguous acres, using more than 20 megawatts of power or drawing more than 500,000 gallons of water a day, adding to a 12-month moratorium the city adopted in June. Granville, Pataskala and Sunbury adopted charter amendments capping data centers at 25 megawatts, passing by margins of 74 to 82%, and Urbana capped them at 7.5 megawatts with 77% support.\n\nGrove City Mayor Richard Ike Stage told the Dispatch he believes hysteria over water and power use drove the results, while Ashville village administrator Bert Cline said developers who have already spent $15 million on land are unlikely to walk away regardless of the vote."
why_it_matters: "Six of these restrictions landed in Delaware, Franklin, Licking and Pickaway counties, the same corridor where data centers have been driving up land prices and utility demand; an investor evaluating a parcel near one of these sites should check whether the new acreage, power or water caps apply before assuming a deal tied to a data center developer or its spillover demand still pencils."
