title: "Blacklick and Pataskala Are One Corridor, Two Different Rental Markets"
summary: "A \"Blacklick, OH\" mailing address can span Columbus, Reynoldsburg, Gahanna and unincorporated Jefferson Township across five school districts, while Pataskala is a separate incorporated Licking County city, and only parcels inside Columbus limits must register under the city's new rental registry starting Oct. 1. Three-bedroom rents in the corridor ran roughly $1,945 to $2,445 in late August, with older, lower-basis pockets producing higher cap rates than newer subdivisions built near the still-distant Intel campus."
detail: "Blacklick is an unincorporated community in Jefferson Township, Franklin County, with no city government or school district of its own; the 43004 ZIP code spans parcels governed by Columbus, Reynoldsburg, Gahanna and the township, split across five public school districts. Pataskala, by contrast, is an incorporated Licking County city with two school districts within its limits. That distinction determines which properties owe Columbus's new rental registration fee starting Oct. 1, since township and Pataskala parcels fall outside it. Reported three-bedroom rents in the corridor ranged from about $1,945 to $2,445 as of late August and early September, drawn from Zillow and Zumper listings. About 72 percent of housing in the 43004 ZIP was built in 2000 or later, versus a median construction year of 1998 in Pataskala. The guide places older, lower-basis pockets of Blacklick in a 7 to 10 percent cap rate band, comparable to Whitehall and Eastmoor, while newer subdivisions near $350,000 in value pencil closer to 6 to 8 percent. Intel's New Albany semiconductor campus, often cited as a demand driver for the corridor, is still targeted to open its first fab in 2031, with just over $5 billion invested through the end of 2025 against an unchanged $28 billion campus budget, and construction has slowed, so the guide cautions investors against pricing in fab-driven demand that has not yet materialized in current rent data. It also flags that Licking County, unlike Franklin County, falls below the population threshold that triggers a state rental-filing notification requirement under Ohio law, and that Blacklick Estates, a separate census-designated place several miles south in Madison and Truro townships, is a different, frequently confused market with its own price points."
why_it_matters: "An investor evaluating a \"Blacklick\" listing needs the Franklin or Licking County auditor's parcel record, not the mailing address, to know which school district, tax rate and rental registration rules actually apply, since two houses a mile apart can answer to different cities and different compliance deadlines."
