summary: "Columbus Mayor Andrew Ginther and City Council President Shannon Hardin, who is now running against him for mayor in 2027, are publicly disputing Ginther's plan to use about $18 million from the city's income tax set-aside fund and workers' compensation refunds to close an operating budget gap. Ginther warned that restricting the fund would cut his proposed $2.8 billion capital budget by $203 million; Hardin wants public hearings before council acts."
detail: "Columbus Mayor Andrew Ginther and City Council President Shannon Hardin have been publicly clashing since late August over how the city should fund gaps in its operating budget. Ginther's administration is proposing to draw about $10 million from the city's income tax set-aside fund, which normally goes toward bond debt payments, combined with roughly $8 million expected from the Ohio Bureau of Workers Compensation, to plug an $18 million hole in the operating budget. Ginther called proposals to restrict use of the set-aside fund \"fiscally unsound,\" saying such a limit would cut his proposed $2.8 billion capital budget by $203 million and jeopardize $87 million in local matching funds tied to $134 million in state and federal grants. Hardin, who recently announced he will challenge Ginther for mayor in 2027, said he wants to hold public hearings and be a \"governing partner\" on the budget rather than simply approve the mayor's plan. Ginther submitted his budget proposal about a week later than in 2025, which his office attributed to a later council recess. Past budgets have typically moved from proposal to passage within weeks; the current dispute could extend that timeline this year."
