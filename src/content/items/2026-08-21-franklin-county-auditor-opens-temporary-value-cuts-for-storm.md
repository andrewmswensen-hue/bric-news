title: "Franklin County auditor opens temporary value cuts for storm damaged property"
summary: "The Franklin County Auditor's Office said owners whose real property was damaged in recent severe storms and flooding may qualify for a temporary reduction in the auditor's appraised value. Ohio law lets county auditors reduce the value of damaged or destroyed real property so the appraised value reflects actual condition, and a reduction may lower real estate taxes for the affected tax year. The office said damage does not have to be a total loss, citing a fallen tree on a roof, flood damage, or structural damage from severe winds. Owners may apply without complete repair documentation."
detail: "As Franklin County residents cleaned up from severe storms and flooding, the Auditor's Office said owners of homes, businesses and other real property that sustained damage may qualify for a temporary reduction in the auditor's appraised value. According to WSYX, the office pointed to Ohio law allowing county auditors to reduce the value of real property that has been damaged or destroyed so that the appraised value more accurately reflects the property's condition, and noted that a lower value may in turn lower real estate taxes for the affected tax year. The office emphasized two points for owners deciding whether to file. First, the damage does not need to be a total loss: examples given include a fallen tree damaging a roof, flooding affecting a property, and severe winds causing structural damage. Second, owners are encouraged to apply even if they do not yet have all of their repair documentation assembled. The application and further detail are available through the Auditor's Office. The reduction is temporary and tied to the affected tax year rather than a permanent change to the property's value."
why_it_matters: "This runs on a different track from the triennial value review: it is condition-based relief tied to a specific tax year, and it applies to rentals and commercial property, not just owner-occupied homes. The auditor's own guidance is that partial damage counts and that missing repair documentation is not a reason to wait, which is the opposite of how most owners treat a paperwork gap."
