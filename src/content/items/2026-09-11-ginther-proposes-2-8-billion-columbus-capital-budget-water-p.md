title: "Ginther proposes $2.8 billion Columbus capital budget, water plant still stalled"
summary: "Columbus Mayor Andrew Ginther proposed a capital budget totaling more than $2.8 billion, including over $2 billion in new spending, with $24 million aimed at traffic and pedestrian safety along Livingston Avenue and smaller amounts for parks, fire equipment and pool repairs. The budget's largest single item remains a planned fourth water treatment plant in southwest Delaware County, expected to cost more than $1 billion, which is running behind schedule after no contractors submitted bids to build it. Columbus City Council must still vote on the budget."
detail: "Ginther announced the proposal at the Driving Park Community Center to highlight $24 million earmarked for reducing serious and fatal crashes along Livingston Avenue, part of a public service push that also includes $21.5 million for fire apparatus and $20.9 million for bikeways and sidewalks. Other proposed spending includes $13 million for new parks on the city's southwest side to offset the loss of McCoy Park, which is being converted to a women's soccer facility, $5 million to repair the storm-damaged Barack Community Center, and $13.5 million to reconstruct the Tuttle Park pool. The budget's biggest item by far is a planned fourth water treatment plant in southwest Delaware County, intended to meet demand from new residential growth and data centers straining the water system that Columbus and several surrounding communities rely on. The project, budgeted at more than $1 billion, was supposed to be complete by 2032, but Columbus Water and Power Director Kristen Atha said no contractors submitted bids on the project, a sign she attributed to renewed national competition for large contractors; the city is now meeting with multiple contractors and may adjust its bidding strategy. Ginther said rising construction costs are forcing the city to stretch capital dollars further. Columbus City Council still has to vote on the full budget."
why_it_matters: "The stalled fourth water treatment plant is the infrastructure meant to support new residential growth in southwest Delaware County, so investors targeting that corridor should watch whether the city's rebid effort gets contractors on board, since further delay could slow the utility capacity new development there depends on."
