title: "Columbus Metro Active Listings Sit Near 6,100, Far Below Normal"
summary: "The Columbus metro area, with about 2.23 million residents, had roughly 6,098 active home listings in early October, according to Central Ohio MLS data cited by Realtor Barbara Gale of the Gale Group at Keller Williams Consultants Realty. Gale says a more typical supply would run 10,000 to 15,000 listings; total listings, including pending sales, are closer to 8,600. She attributes the shortage largely to owners who refinanced or bought at 2020-2021 rates and have little incentive to sell and take on a new mortgage at today's higher rates."
detail: "Central Ohio MLS data cited by Barbara Gale, a Realtor with the Gale Group at Keller Williams Consultants Realty who has worked the Columbus and Dublin markets since the 1970s, puts active listings at about 6,098 for a metro of 2.23 million people, well under the 10,000 to 15,000 she considers a normal supply. Total listings, including pending transactions, run closer to 8,600. Gale says buyer demand has cooled from the pandemic-era bidding wars, with mortgage rates in the high sixes and low sevens, but the inventory increase many expected has not materialized because owners who locked in 2020-2021 rates have little reason to sell. In Dublin, available land is still supporting new construction. In the landlocked suburb of Upper Arlington, builders are instead buying older homes and replacing them with new construction, and Gale says older housing stock in established neighborhoods such as Upper Arlington and Clintonville Beechwold is drawing investor interest as renovation opportunities, increasingly sold at auction."
why_it_matters: "With active listings running well under half the normal supply, Columbus rents and resale values have a floor under them even as sales slow, and renovation-ready homes in older neighborhoods like Clintonville Beechwold are drawing enough investor competition to push sales to auction."
