title: "Columbus rental registry opens October 1, closes December 31"
summary: "Columbus City Council's Chapter 4515 requires owners of residential rental property, from a single rented condo to a full apartment complex, to register with the city between October 1 and December 31, 2026. Registration costs $15 per dwelling unit annually, capped at $1,500 per complex, and owners of 10 or more units must also name a local operator living within 100 miles of Columbus. The city had not published a registration portal as of September 1, so owners should start gathering parcel IDs, unit counts and contacts now. The enacted civil penalty for noncompliance is $2 per unit per day."
detail: "Columbus City Council passed ordinance 0923-2026 on April 20, 2026, by an 8-1 vote, creating a citywide Registry of Residential Rental Properties under Chapter 4515 of the Columbus City Code. Councilmember Nicholas Bankston sponsored the ordinance; Councilmember Emmanuel Remy cast the dissenting vote. The registry, administered by Building and Zoning Services, collects who owns each rental property, who manages it day to day, how many dwelling units it contains, its parcel identification, and an emergency contact.\n\nThe rule applies broadly: single-family homes, duplexes and condos are covered along with larger complexes, and only four narrow exemptions apply under CCC 4515.04, including properties already subject to the Vacant Building Registry. There is no carve-out for small owners, so a single rented house in Clintonville carries the same registration duty as a 200-unit building. Owners of 10 or more units must also designate a Local Operator whose primary residence is within 100 miles of Columbus.\n\nRegistration runs October 1 through December 31, 2026, with no phase-in by portfolio size, zip code or filing date. Registered properties become subject to a routine three-year inspection covering the building's exterior, common areas and shared mechanical systems; individual unit interiors are excluded unless a tenant or owner requests a look. The enacted civil penalty for failing to comply with a Notice of Violation is $2 per dwelling unit per day, well below the $10-per-day, $2,500-cap figures that circulated online from an earlier January 2026 draft of the ordinance. As of September 1, 2026, the city had not published a registration portal or online system, so owners' immediate task is assembling parcel IDs, unit counts and contact information ahead of the window opening."
