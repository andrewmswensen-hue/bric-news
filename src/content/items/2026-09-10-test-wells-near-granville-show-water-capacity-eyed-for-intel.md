title: "Test wells near Granville show water capacity eyed for Intel's future needs"
summary: "Test wells drilled near Alexandria and Granville as part of a state-funded search for water sources for Intel's New Albany chip campus found the Raccoon Creek valley could produce up to 9.3 million gallons of water a day. Officials from New Albany and Granville say there is no immediate plan to tap the sources; Intel's production start has slipped to 2031, and Columbus has said it will supply Intel's roughly 5-million-gallon daily need once production begins. Granville officials say any future development must protect the aquifer and its own well field."
detail: "Engineers tested three sites, two in St. Albans Township bookending the village of Alexandria and one just west of Granville, on land the New Albany Company bought from a former Granville Township trustee. Combined test results, compiled by Eagon & Associates for the City of New Albany, found potential yields ranging from about 3 million to 4.3 million gallons a day at the individual sites, adding up to the 9.3 million gallon estimate. New Albany spokesperson Adrienne Mayfield described the work as \"a fact-finding effort,\" done in coordination with Granville, St. Albans Township, Alexandria, Licking County and the Ohio EPA, following Intel's decision to locate its investment in central Ohio. The testing follows an earlier dispute: New Albany's 2024 request to drill test wells alarmed Granville officials before the city agreed to include the village in the process. Granville Village Manager Herb Koehler said the Granville Township site's relatively low yield should be reassuring, and stressed that any future water development must protect the aquifer and Granville's own well field, which sits near the site of the former Granville Solvents facility, a federal Superfund cleanup that left a contamination plume nearby. An engineering firm monitoring that plume for the EPA has warned that new wells drawing large volumes west of the village could pull the plume toward Granville's drinking water supply."
why_it_matters: "Investors holding property in Granville or Alexandria should watch this closely, since any future decision to draw millions of gallons a day from the Raccoon Creek aquifer for Intel would shape both local water availability and development pressure in those Licking County communities well before Intel's now-2031 production start."
