title: "HUD narrows assistance-animal rule; Ohio's own rule did not change"
summary: "HUD narrowed its own fair housing enforcement on assistance animals in a May 22 memo, saying it will find cause for a reasonable-accommodation violation only when an animal is individually trained to perform a task tied to a disability, RL Property Management explains. The memo does not change the Fair Housing Act itself, and it does not touch Ohio's separate civil rights rule, Ohio Administrative Code 4112-5-07, which still protects any animal that aids a person with a disability and bars extra charges for it."
detail: "HUD's Assistant Secretary for Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity issued an enforcement memo on May 22, 2026, narrowing when HUD itself will pursue a fair housing complaint over an assistance animal. Under the new posture, HUD will find cause for a reasonable-accommodation violation only when the animal has been individually trained to perform a task tied to a disability; the memo states that comfort, well-being, or companionship alone do not qualify. The change followed an April 6 Federal Register notice that formalized HUD's September 2025 withdrawal of its 2020 assistance-animal guidance. None of this touches the underlying Fair Housing Act, which Congress has not amended, and residents retain a private right to sue regardless of HUD's enforcement posture. Ohio has its own, separate rule. Ohio Administrative Code 4112-5-07(C) protects any 'animal assistant,' defined in OAC 4112-5-02(C) as 'any animal which aids the persons with a disability,' with no training requirement written into that text, and it still bars landlords from charging an extra fee for the animal. The Ohio Civil Rights Commission, which handles fair housing complaints referred by HUD as the state's certified enforcement agency, had not issued guidance reconciling its broader text with HUD's narrower federal standard as of September 2026. For a trained service animal, the accommodation obligation is unchanged and pet fees do not apply. For an untrained emotional support animal, HUD's own enforcement has stepped back, but Ohio's text has not, leaving landlords in a gap where a blanket denial carries exposure under state law even though HUD might decline to act. RL Property Management recommends an individualized, documented decision on each request rather than a categorical policy, and consulting an Ohio attorney before denying a specific request."
