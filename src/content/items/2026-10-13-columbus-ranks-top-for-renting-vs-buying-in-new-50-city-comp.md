summary: "A new comparison of the 50 largest U.S. metro areas ranks Columbus ninth among cities where renting beats buying, using home-value-to-rent and income ratios from The Zebra. Columbus has a median home price of $363,100 and average annual rent of $12,624, giving it a price-to-rent ratio of 28.76 and a home-price-to-income ratio of 3.9, the lowest home-price-to-income figure among the top 10. Columbus also ranks first among the 50 cities for rent as a share of median income, at 14.46%, according to the Mercury News, which cited data from TheZebra.com's analysis distributed by Stacker."
detail: "TheZebra.com analyzed the 50 most populous U.S. metro areas using three measures: the price-to-rent ratio (median home value divided by median annualized rent for a two-bedroom), rent as a share of median household income, and the home-price-to-income ratio. The analysis, distributed by Stacker and published by the Mercury News, placed Columbus ninth on the list of cities where renting is the better financial move. Columbus had a median home sales price of $363,100, among the lowest of the top 10 cities, against an average annual two-bedroom rent of $12,624. That gave Columbus a price-to-rent ratio of 28.76 and a home-price-to-income ratio of 3.9, the lowest of any city in the top 10, pointing to comparatively affordable home prices relative to local income. Columbus also had the lowest rent-to-income ratio of the entire top 10, at 14.46%, meaning local renters spend a smaller share of income on housing than renters in the other nine cities on the list. The report separately cited a 2026 State of Insurance figure showing the average U.S. homeowner now pays $2,966 a year for home insurance, noting that none of the top 10 rent-favorable cities, Columbus included, rank among the states with the highest home insurance costs."
