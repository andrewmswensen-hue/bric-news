title: "Landlord Right of Entry in Ohio: What Columbus Owners Can and Can't Do"
summary: "Ohio law requires landlords to give tenants reasonable notice before entering an occupied rental, and Ohio Revised Code 5321.04(A)(8) sets 24 hours as the presumed reasonable notice period, according to RL Property Management. Entry must happen at a reasonable time and for a legitimate purpose such as an inspection, repair or showing; emergencies are the exception. Tenants have a matching duty under ORC 5321.05(B) not to unreasonably withhold consent. Getting entry wrong can expose an owner to damages, an injunction, attorney's fees or a terminated lease."
detail: "Ohio Revised Code 5321.04(A)(8), last amended effective September 28, 2012, requires a landlord to give a tenant reasonable notice before entering an occupied unit, except in an emergency or when notice is impracticable. The statute names a number: 24 hours is presumed to be reasonable notice in the absence of evidence to the contrary. Entry also has to happen at a reasonable time and for a legitimate purpose, such as an inspection, a repair, an agreed service call or a showing.\n\nTenants carry a corresponding duty under ORC 5321.05(B) not to unreasonably withhold consent to a landlord's lawful entry request. If a landlord violates the notice or purpose requirements, the tenant can seek damages, an injunction, or attorney's fees, and in some circumstances may be able to terminate the lease.\n\nRL Property Management notes that Columbus's rental registry inspections, under Chapter 4515 of the city code, are limited to exteriors, common areas and shared mechanical systems. They do not reach the interior of an occupied unit, so a registry inspection does not substitute for the notice-and-purpose requirements that apply any time a landlord wants to enter a tenant's unit directly. This is informational and reflects Ohio law as of September 2026; it is not legal advice, and an owner with a specific entry dispute should consult an Ohio attorney."
why_it_matters: "A Columbus landlord who shows up unannounced for a routine inspection, rather than giving the 24-hour notice ORC 5321.04(A)(8) presumes reasonable, is handing a tenant grounds to seek damages or even terminate the lease over a single visit."
